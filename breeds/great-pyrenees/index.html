How big is a Great Pyrenees?

The Great Pyrenees is a giant-sized dog breed from France/Spain, in the Working group. Males stand 69–82 cm (27–32.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 46–57 kg (100–126 lb). Females stand 64–74 cm (25–28.9 in) and weigh 39–50 kg (85–110 lb). On average it is taller than 96% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 92%. At the shoulder, an average male Great Pyrenees reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, longer than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

About the Great Pyrenees

The Great Pyrenees (called the Pyrenean Mountain Dog in the UK and Europe) is a livestock guardian from the Pyrenees mountains between France and Spain. For centuries, shepherds used it to protect flocks from wolves and bears. It lived with the sheep day and night and worked independently, without a shepherd giving commands. In the 17th century it briefly became fashionable at the French royal court, and Louis XIV declared it the "Royal Dog of France". Today it is still used as a working guardian in many countries, and it is also kept as a calm, majestic family dog.

What a Pyrenees's size means at home

A male Great Pyrenees stands about 69–82 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and females about 64–74 cm. The AKC standard sets minimum weights only (about 45 kg / 100 lb for males and 38.5 kg / 85 lb for females), and many dogs are heavier. Its thick white coat makes it look even bigger. It needs plenty of space, a large vehicle and a 54″ (137 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Pyrenees vs Anatolian, Kangal and Komondor

These are all large livestock guardians. The Anatolian Shepherd and the Kangal are similar in height but short-coated and fawn with a black mask. The Komondor is about the same size, with its unique corded white coat.

A guardian, not a retriever

The Pyrenees was bred to think for itself and protect its flock. As a result it can be independent, stubborn and slow to obey, and many don't have reliable recall. It's naturally protective and barks a lot, especially at night, when predators would traditionally come. It needs a securely fenced garden, early socialisation and patient training. With its family, it's gentle and calm.

Coat and health

The double coat sheds heavily, especially in spring, and needs brushing at least weekly. Despite its length, it tends to shed dirt well. Pyrenees have double dewclaws on their hind legs, which the breed standard requires. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat, kneecap problems and bone cancer. They often live 10–12 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 137 (Chien de Montagne des Pyrénées); The Kennel Club breed standard.

Great Pyrenees size: common questions

How big does a Great Pyrenees get?

A fully grown male Great Pyrenees stands 69–82 cm (27–32.1 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 46–57 kg (100–126 lb). Females stand 64–74 cm (25–28.9 in) and weigh 39–50 kg (85–110 lb).

Is a Great Pyrenees a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 48 kg (105 lb), the Great Pyrenees counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is taller than 96% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Great Pyrenees live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, longer than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Can a Great Pyrenees fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Great Pyrenees weighs 39–57 kg (85–126 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
