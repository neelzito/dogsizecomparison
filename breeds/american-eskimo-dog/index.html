How big is an American Eskimo Dog?

The American Eskimo Dog is a medium-sized dog breed from the United States, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 38–49 cm (15–19.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 12–16 kg (25–35 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 39% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 36%. At the shoulder, an average American Eskimo Dog reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the American Eskimo Dog

Despite its name, the American Eskimo Dog has nothing to do with the Arctic. It descends from white German Spitz dogs brought to the US by German immigrants. During and after the First World War, anti-German feeling led owners to rename the breed the "American Eskimo". In the early 20th century, "Eskies" were stars of travelling circuses, where their intelligence and striking white coats made them crowd favourites. Today the Eskie is a bright, lively and affectionate companion.

Three sizes

The AKC recognises three sizes, measured at the shoulder:

  • Toy: 9–12 in (23–30.5 cm)
  • Miniature: over 12 in up to 15 in (30.5–38 cm)
  • Standard: over 15 in up to 19 in (38–48.5 cm)

This page shows the Standard size, which weighs about 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb) and reaches just below an adult's knee. The crate size calculator suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate for an average Standard Eskie. Toys and Miniatures need smaller crates, and many Toys fit an airline cabin carrier.

Eskie vs Finnish Spitz and German Spitz

The Finnish Spitz is a little taller and similar in weight, with a fox-red coat. The Eskie is closely related to the German Spitz and the Japanese Spitz, which look very similar but are recognised separately by different kennel clubs.

Temperament

Eskies are clever, eager to please and quick to learn, and they love to show off tricks. They're affectionate with their family and alert watchdogs that bark at anything unusual. They can be reserved with strangers. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and they don't like being left alone for long.

Coat and health

The thick white double coat sheds a lot, with heavy moults once or twice a year. It needs brushing several times a week, more during moults. Surprisingly, the coat tends to shed dirt well. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, loose kneecaps, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), for which a DNA test exists, and diabetes. Eskies often live 13–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; United Kennel Club breed standard; American Eskimo Dog Club of America.

American Eskimo Dog size: common questions

How big does an American Eskimo Dog get?

An adult American Eskimo Dog stands 38–49 cm (15–19.1 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 12–16 kg (25–35 lb).

Is an American Eskimo Dog a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 14 kg (30 lb), the American Eskimo Dog counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 39% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an American Eskimo Dog live?

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can an American Eskimo Dog fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A American Eskimo Dog weighs 12–16 kg (25–35 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
