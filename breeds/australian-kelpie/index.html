How big is an Australian Kelpie?

The Australian Kelpie is a medium-sized dog breed from Australia, in the Herding group. Adults stand 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 45% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 47%. At the shoulder, an average Australian Kelpie reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–13 years, shorter than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Australian Kelpie

The Australian Kelpie was developed in the late 19th century from Scottish collies brought to Australia, to work huge flocks of sheep in heat, dust and rough terrain. The breed is named after a famous early female, Kelpie. Kelpies are famous for "backing": running across the backs of tightly packed sheep to reach the other side of the flock. Today they remain one of the most important working dogs on Australian farms, and a Kelpie starred in the film Red Dog (2011).

Working Kelpie or show Kelpie?

In Australia, there are two lines. The Working Kelpie is bred purely for ability and comes in many colours and sizes. It's registered by working-dog associations, not kennel clubs. The Australian Kelpie, the show line recognised by kennel clubs, is bred to a standard appearance. Working Kelpies have even more drive, and both need a lot to do.

What a Kelpie's size means at home

The breed standard gives about 43–51 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's knee, and weights of about 16–22.5 kg (35–50 lb). A Kelpie is lean, agile and very fit. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Kelpie vs Australian Cattle Dog

The Australian Cattle Dog is very similar in size, but it's stockier and works cattle by nipping at their heels, while the Kelpie works sheep with speed and agility. The Border Collie is a close relative, a little taller with a longer coat.

Energy: almost unlimited

Kelpies were bred to work all day in the outback, and their energy is extraordinary. They need several hours of activity a day, plus mental challenges such as herding, agility, flyball or advanced training. They're intelligent, eager to work and loyal, but in a home without enough to do, they quickly become frustrated, noisy or destructive. They suit active, outdoor people, ideally with access to work or dog sports.

Care and health

The short, weatherproof double coat is easy to look after, with seasonal shedding. Kelpies are generally hardy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, cerebellar abiotrophy (a nerve disease for which a DNA test exists), and collie eye anomaly. They often live 10–13 years or more.

Sources: FCI standard No. 293 (Australian Kelpie); AKC breed information; Working Kelpie Council of Australia.

Australian Kelpie size: common questions

How big does an Australian Kelpie get?

An adult Australian Kelpie stands 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 16–23 kg (35–50 lb).

Is an Australian Kelpie a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 19 kg (42 lb), the Australian Kelpie counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 45% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an Australian Kelpie live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–13 years, shorter than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can an Australian Kelpie fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Australian Kelpie weighs 16–23 kg (35–50 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
