How big is an Afghan Hound?

The Afghan Hound is a medium-sized dog breed from Afghanistan, in the Hound group. Adults stand 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 23–27 kg (50–60 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 87% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 62%. At the shoulder, an average Afghan Hound reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–18 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Afghan Hound

The Afghan Hound is an ancient sighthound from the mountains and deserts of Afghanistan and the surrounding regions. Tribes used it to hunt hares, gazelles and even leopards over rough, rocky ground, and its long coat protected it from the cold of the mountains. British officers brought Afghans to Britain in the early 20th century, and the breed became a glamorous show dog. Behind the flowing coat is a fast, agile hunter with a proud, independent nature.

What an Afghan's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 27 in (68.5 cm) for males and 25 in (63.5 cm) for females, and around 23–27 kg (50–60 lb). An Afghan reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh, so it's tall, but it's slim and fine-boned under that coat. That's why its weight puts it in the medium size class on this site. It needs a large boot and, because of its height and long legs, the crate size calculator suggests a 48″ (122 cm) crate for an average dog.

Afghan Hound vs Sloughi

The Sloughi, a North African sighthound, is similar in height and weight but short-coated and much less well known. Both share the classic sighthound build and an independent mind.

The coat

An Afghan's long, silky coat is spectacular and very high-maintenance. A full coat needs thorough brushing several times a week, ideally every day, and regular bathing and careful drying to prevent mats. Many pet owners keep it shorter. Puppies have short, fluffy coats and develop the long adult coat over their first year or two.

Temperament and exercise

Afghans are dignified, independent and often aloof with strangers, but affectionate with their family. They're famously hard to train: not because they're not clever, but because they don't see the point. Like all sighthounds, they have a strong urge to chase and can't hear you at full speed, so they need a secure, enclosed area to run. Daily walks and regular chances to sprint keep them happy.

Health

Afghans are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, cataracts, hypothyroidism, and a sensitivity to some anaesthetics, as in other sighthounds. They're long-lived for their size, often 12–14 years or more.

Afghan Hound size: common questions

How big does an Afghan Hound get?

An adult Afghan Hound stands 64–69 cm (25–27 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 23–27 kg (50–60 lb).

Is an Afghan Hound a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 25 kg (55 lb), the Afghan Hound counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 87% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an Afghan Hound live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–18 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can an Afghan Hound fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Afghan Hound weighs 23–27 kg (50–60 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
