How big is a Pekingese?

The Pekingese is a toy dog breed from China, in the Toy group. Adults stand 15–23 cm (5.9–9.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 3.2–6.4 kg (7.1–14 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average that makes it the 4th shortest of the 256 breeds on this site. At the shoulder, an average Pekingese reaches about ankle height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, shorter than the 13.9-year average for toy breeds. At 3.2–6.4 kg (7.1–14 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb).

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Pekingese

The Pekingese is an ancient breed from China, where it was kept for centuries as a companion of the imperial family in the Forbidden City in Beijing (Peking). Owning one outside the palace was forbidden. In 1860, during the Second Opium War, British and French troops looted the Old Summer Palace and took several Pekingese back to Europe. One, named Looty, was given to Queen Victoria. Known as the "lion dog", the Pekingese is dignified, brave and independent, with a rolling gait and a lion-like mane.

What a Pekingese's size means at home

The AKC standard sets a maximum weight of 14 lb (6.4 kg), and most Pekes weigh about 3–6 kg. They stand only about 15–23 cm at the shoulder, around ankle height on an adult. They're low, heavy-fronted and longer than tall, and surprisingly heavy when you pick them up. A Peke suits any home and usually needs an 18″ (46 cm) crate or carrier. Check with the crate size calculator.

Flying with a Pekingese

A Pekingese usually fits the cabin weight limit. Even with a carrier, it stays under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) many airlines set. Check with the airline cabin check. Because of its very flat face, the cabin is usually the only option: many airlines don't accept snub-nosed breeds in the hold.

Pekingese vs Brussels Griffon

The Brussels Griffon is a similar size, also flat-faced, but leggier and wiry-coated, with a monkey-like expression.

Breathing, eyes and back

The Pekingese's very flat face shortens its airways, so many snore, struggle in the heat and tire quickly. Exercise in the cool part of the day and avoid hot cars entirely. The large, prominent eyes are easily injured, so see a vet promptly if one looks red or cloudy. The long back and short legs can be prone to disc problems, so keep your Peke lean and discourage jumping.

Coat and temperament

The long, thick double coat needs daily brushing to prevent mats. Many pet owners keep it trimmed shorter. Pekes are affectionate with their family but independent and often aloof with strangers. They're not built for long walks, but they enjoy short strolls and play. They often live 12–14 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; The Kennel Club breed standard; FCI standard No. 207 (Pekingese).

Pekingese size: common questions

How big does a Pekingese get?

An adult Pekingese stands 15–23 cm (5.9–9.1 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 3.2–6.4 kg (7.1–14 lb).

Is a Pekingese a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 4.8 kg (11 lb), the Pekingese counts as a toy dog breed on this site (toy = under 5 kg (11 lb) on average). It is the 4th shortest of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Pekingese live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, shorter than the 13.9-year average for toy breeds.

Can a Pekingese fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually yes. At 3.2–6.4 kg (7.1–14 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb). Check your airline's rules and carrier size limits before booking.
