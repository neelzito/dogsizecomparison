How big is a White Swiss Shepherd Dog?

The White Swiss Shepherd Dog is a large-sized dog breed from Switzerland, in the Herding group. Males stand 60–66 cm (23.6–26 in) at the shoulder and weigh 30–40 kg (66–88 lb). Females stand 55–61 cm (21.7–24 in) and weigh 25–35 kg (55–77 lb). On average it is taller than 71% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 80%. At the shoulder, an average male White Swiss Shepherd Dog reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

About the White Swiss Shepherd Dog

The White Swiss Shepherd Dog (Berger Blanc Suisse) descends from white German Shepherd Dogs. White coats appeared in early German Shepherd lines, but from the 1930s German breeders excluded white dogs from the breed. In North America, however, white Shepherds stayed popular and were bred as "American-Canadian White Shepherds". In the 1970s, some were imported to Switzerland, where breeders developed them into a separate breed. It was fully recognised by the FCI in 2011, with Switzerland as its country of origin. Today it's a gentle, intelligent and versatile family and working dog.

What a White Swiss Shepherd's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 60–66 cm at the shoulder and about 30–40 kg for males, and 55–61 cm and about 25–35 kg for females. A White Swiss Shepherd reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's well-muscled and elegant, a little longer than tall. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

White Swiss Shepherd vs German Shepherd

The White Swiss Shepherd is about the same size as a German Shepherd, but it's always pure white, and its back is straighter rather than sloping. Most White Swiss Shepherds are also a little softer and calmer in temperament than working-line German Shepherds. The French Briard is a similar size, with a long, shaggy coat.

Temperament and exercise

White Swiss Shepherds are friendly, affectionate and eager to please, devoted to their family and usually good with children and other dogs. They're alert but not aggressive, and sometimes reserved with strangers. They're intelligent and easy to train, and they do well in obedience, agility, tracking and as therapy dogs. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they don't like being left alone for long.

Coat and health

White Swiss Shepherds come with a medium-length (stock) coat or a long coat. Both have a dense undercoat that sheds heavily, especially seasonally, so expect white hair everywhere and brush several times a week. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, degenerative myelopathy and the MDR1 drug sensitivity, for which DNA tests exist. White Swiss Shepherds often live 12–14 years.

Sources: FCI standard No. 347 (Berger Blanc Suisse); Swiss Kennel Club (SKG); United Kennel Club breed standard.

White Swiss Shepherd Dog size: common questions

How big does a White Swiss Shepherd Dog get?

A fully grown male White Swiss Shepherd Dog stands 60–66 cm (23.6–26 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 30–40 kg (66–88 lb). Females stand 55–61 cm (21.7–24 in) and weigh 25–35 kg (55–77 lb).

Is a White Swiss Shepherd Dog a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 33 kg (72 lb), the White Swiss Shepherd Dog counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 71% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a White Swiss Shepherd Dog live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a White Swiss Shepherd Dog fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A White Swiss Shepherd Dog weighs 25–40 kg (55–88 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
