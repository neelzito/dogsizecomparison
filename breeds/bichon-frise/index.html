How big is a Bichon Frise?

The Bichon Frise is a small-sized dog breed from the Mediterranean, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 24–29 cm (9.4–11.4 in) at the shoulder and weigh 5.4–8.2 kg (12–18 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 12% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 15%.

Bichon Frise compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average Bichon Frise stands 27 cm (10.4 in) at the shoulder: 15% of the person's height, reaching about mid-shin. In feet, adults stand 9 in to 11 in tall at the shoulder. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to just below the knee. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 14–15 years, longer than the 13.7-year average for small breeds. Lighter individuals fit airline cabin limits of 8 kg (17.6 lb) for dog and carrier; heavier ones need an airline that weighs only the carrier size, or travel in the hold.

About the Bichon Frise

The Bichon Frise belongs to an old family of small white Mediterranean companion dogs that also includes the Maltese, Havanese and Bolognese. Its ancestors, the "Tenerife" dogs, were spread by sailors and traders. They became favourites of European royalty in the 16th century, especially at the French court, and later earned their living as street performers and circus dogs. The name comes from the French bichon à poil frisé, "curly-haired little dog". Today the Bichon is a cheerful, affectionate family companion that loves people and attention.

What a Bichon's size means at home

A Bichon Frise stands about 24–29 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult, and weighs about 5.5–8 kg (12–18 lb). It is sturdy rather than delicate, and that powder-puff coat makes it look bigger than it is. It suits flats and small homes, enjoys daily walks and play, and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. See the crate size calculator.

Can a Bichon fly in the cabin?

Sometimes. With a carrier, smaller Bichons can stay under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, but bigger ones won't. Check yours with the airline cabin check.

Bichon vs Maltese and Havanese

The Bichon is bigger and sturdier than the Maltese, and its coat is curly rather than straight and silky. The Havanese, its Cuban cousin, is similar in height but clearly lighter, and it comes in many colours, not just white.

Coat care

The Bichon's double coat is soft and dense underneath, with a curly outer coat. It sheds very little, which is why many people with allergies get on well with Bichons. But because the loose hair stays in the coat, it mats quickly. Plan on brushing down to the skin several times a week and a professional groom every 4–6 weeks for the classic rounded trim. Keep the hair around the eyes trimmed, and wipe away tear stains.

Temperament and health

Bichons are friendly with almost everyone, including other pets, and are usually good with children. They can be hard to house-train, so be patient and consistent. They often dislike being left alone. Health issues include allergies and skin problems, loose kneecaps, bladder stones and dental disease. Bichons often live 14–15 years or more.

Bichon Frise size: common questions

How big does a Bichon Frise get?

An adult Bichon Frise stands 24–29 cm (9.4–11.4 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 5.4–8.2 kg (12–18 lb).

How tall is a Bichon Frise in feet?

An adult Bichon Frise stands 9 in to 11 in tall at the shoulder (24–29 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, that reaches about mid-shin.

Is a Bichon Frise a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 6.8 kg (15 lb), the Bichon Frise counts as a small dog breed on this site (small = 5–10 kg (11–22 lb) on average). It is taller than 12% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does a Bichon Frise live?

Typical life expectancy is 14–15 years, longer than the 13.7-year average for small breeds.

Can a Bichon Frise fly in the airplane cabin?

Sometimes. Lighter individuals fit airline cabin limits of 8 kg (17.6 lb) for dog and carrier; heavier ones need an airline that weighs only the carrier size, or travel in the hold. Rules vary by airline, so check before booking.
