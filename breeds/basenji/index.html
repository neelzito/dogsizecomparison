How big is a Basenji?

The Basenji is a medium-sized dog breed from Central Africa, in the Hound group. Males stand 43 cm (16.9 in) at the shoulder and weigh 11 kg (24 lb). Females stand 41 cm (15.9 in) and weigh 10 kg (22 lb). On average it is taller than 36% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 29%. At the shoulder, an average male Basenji reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 13–14 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Basenji

The Basenji is an ancient hunting dog from Central Africa, where people in the Congo basin used it to drive small game into nets. Genetic studies place it among the breeds closest to the earliest domestic dogs. European travellers brought Basenjis to Britain in the 1930s. The breed is famous as the "barkless dog": because of the shape of its larynx, it doesn't bark, but it yodels, howls and makes a sound often called a "baroo".

What a Basenji's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 17 in (43 cm) and 24 lb (11 kg) for males, and 16 in (40.5 cm) and 22 lb (10 kg) for females. A Basenji reaches just below an adult's knee. It's light, elegant and square, with a curled tail and a wrinkled forehead. It suits flats and houses, as long as it gets enough exercise, and the crate size calculator suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate because of its leg length. A Basenji is too heavy for most airline cabins.

Basenji vs Cirneco and Podengo

The Basenji shares its "primitive" look with other ancient hunting breeds. The Cirneco dell'Etna from Sicily is a little taller and similar in weight, with large upright ears. The Portuguese Podengo (Medium) is taller and heavier.

Cat-like and independent

Basenjis are often described as cat-like: they're clean, groom themselves, dislike water and can be aloof with strangers. They're clever but independent, and training takes patience and creativity. Their prey drive is strong, so recall is often unreliable and small pets may not be safe. They're also skilled escape artists and climbers. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and they can be destructive if bored.

Care and health

The short, fine coat sheds very little and has almost no doggy smell. Basenjis feel the cold. Like wild dogs, females usually come into season only once a year. Known health problems include Fanconi syndrome, a kidney disease for which a DNA test exists, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and hypothyroidism. Basenjis often live 13–14 years.

Basenji size: common questions

How big does a Basenji get?

A fully grown male Basenji stands 43 cm (16.9 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 11 kg (24 lb). Females stand 41 cm (15.9 in) and weigh 10 kg (22 lb).

Is a Basenji a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 11 kg (23 lb), the Basenji counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 36% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Basenji live?

Typical life expectancy is 13–14 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Basenji fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Basenji weighs 10–11 kg (22–24 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
