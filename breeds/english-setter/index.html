How big is an English Setter?

The English Setter is a large-sized dog breed from England, in the Sporting group. Males stand 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 30–37 kg (65–80 lb). Females stand 59–64 cm (23–25 in) and weigh 21–25 kg (45–55 lb). On average it is taller than 80% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 72%. At the shoulder, an average male English Setter reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12 years, about average for large breeds.

About the English Setter

The English Setter is one of the oldest gundog breeds. Setting spaniels were used in England as early as the 14th century to find birds and then crouch ("set") so that hunters could throw nets over the birds. The modern breed was shaped in the 19th century by Edward Laverack and, later, R. L. Purcell Llewellin, whose names are still used for two types: the heavier Laverack show type and the lighter, faster Llewellin field type. The English Setter is known for its gentle nature and its speckled "belton" coat.

What an English Setter's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 25 in (63.5 cm) for males and 24 in (61 cm) for females, and the figures on this page cover about 63.5–68.5 cm and 29.5–36.5 kg (65–80 lb) for males, and 58.5–63.5 cm and 20.5–25 kg (45–55 lb) for females. An English Setter reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's elegant, athletic and long-legged. It usually needs a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

English vs Irish and Gordon Setter

The Irish Setter is a little taller, with a solid mahogany coat. The Gordon Setter from Scotland is a little shorter and lighter on average, with a black-and-tan coat and a heavier build.

Belton colours

The English Setter's coat is white with small coloured flecks, called "belton" after a village in Northumberland. Colours include blue belton (black flecks), orange belton, lemon belton, liver belton, and tricolour (blue belton with tan markings). Puppies are born mostly white, and their flecks develop over the first months.

Temperament and exercise

English Setters are gentle, friendly and affectionate, usually excellent with children and other dogs. They're mellower than many gundogs, especially show lines, but they still need daily exercise and like to run. Field-bred Setters have much more energy. They can be stubborn and easily distracted by scents and birds, so recall needs work. They're sensitive and respond best to gentle training.

Coat and health

The long, silky coat with feathering needs brushing a few times a week and regular trimming of the feet and ears. Deafness is more common in English Setters than in most breeds, so ask breeders about BAER hearing tests. Hip and elbow dysplasia, hypothyroidism and allergies also occur. English Setters often live about 12 years.

English Setter size: common questions

How big does an English Setter get?

A fully grown male English Setter stands 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 30–37 kg (65–80 lb). Females stand 59–64 cm (23–25 in) and weigh 21–25 kg (45–55 lb).

Is an English Setter a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 28 kg (61 lb), the English Setter counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 80% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an English Setter live?

Typical life expectancy is 12 years, about average for large breeds.

Can an English Setter fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A English Setter weighs 21–37 kg (45–80 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
