How big is a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever?

The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever is a medium-sized dog breed from Canada, in the Sporting group. Males stand 46–54 cm (17.9–21.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb). Females stand 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb). On average it is taller than 51% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 48%.

Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever stands 50 cm (19.5 in) at the shoulder: 28% of the person's height, reaching about knee height. An average female, at 47 cm (18.5 in), reaches about knee height. In feet, males stand 1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 9 in tall at the shoulder and females 1 ft 5 in to 1 ft 8 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about mid-thigh. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever

The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, or "Toller", was developed in the 19th century in Yarmouth County, Nova Scotia, in eastern Canada. It has an unusual hunting technique called "tolling": the dog plays and frolics along the shoreline, which makes curious ducks swim closer, within range of the hidden hunter. The dog then retrieves the shot birds from the water. The behaviour is thought to copy the way foxes lure waterfowl. The Toller is the smallest of the retrievers and the provincial dog of Nova Scotia.

What a Toller's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–21 in (45.5–53.5 cm) for males and 17–20 in (43–51 cm) for females, and about 16–22.5 kg (35–50 lb). A Toller reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's compact, muscular and agile. The crate size calculator suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult, and a smaller Toller may fit a 36″ crate. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Toller vs Golden Retriever, Brittany and Springer

People often mistake a Toller for a small Golden Retriever. It's much smaller, with a redder coat and usually white markings on the chest, feet and tail tip. The Brittany is about the same height but lighter. The English Springer Spaniel is a little bigger.

Energy and the "Toller scream"

Tollers are intelligent, energetic and playful, with a strong desire to retrieve. They need plenty of exercise every day, ideally with swimming and fetch, plus mental work. They excel at agility, obedience and dock diving. When excited, many make a high-pitched shriek known as the "Toller scream". They're affectionate with their family, sometimes reserved with strangers, and can be sensitive.

Coat and health

The medium-length, water-repellent double coat sheds heavily, especially seasonally, and needs brushing a few times a week. Known health problems include Addison's disease, autoimmune conditions, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), hip dysplasia and cleft palate. DNA tests exist for some of these. Tollers often live 12–14 years.

Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever size: common questions

How big does a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever get?

A fully grown male Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever stands 46–54 cm (17.9–21.1 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 16–23 kg (35–50 lb). Females stand 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb).

How tall is a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever in feet?

A male Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever stands 1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 9 in tall at the shoulder (46–54 cm) and a female 1 ft 5 in to 1 ft 8 in (43–51 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about knee height.

Is a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 19 kg (42 lb), the Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 51% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever weighs 16–23 kg (35–50 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
