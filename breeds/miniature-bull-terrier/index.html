How big is a Miniature Bull Terrier?

The Miniature Bull Terrier is a medium-sized dog breed from England, in the Terrier group. Adults stand 26–36 cm (10–14 in) at the shoulder and weigh 8.2–13 kg (18–28 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 19% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 29%.

Miniature Bull Terrier compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average Miniature Bull Terrier stands 31 cm (12 in) at the shoulder: 17% of the person's height, reaching about mid-shin. In feet, adults stand 10 in to 1 ft 2 in tall at the shoulder. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to just below the knee. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 11–13 years, shorter than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Miniature Bull Terrier

The Miniature Bull Terrier is the small version of the Bull Terrier. Small Bull Terriers were bred alongside the standard size from the 19th century, but they were only recognised as a separate breed later: by The Kennel Club in 1939 and the AKC in 1991. It has the same egg-shaped head, muscular build and clownish personality as its bigger cousin, in a body that fits more easily into modern homes.

What a Mini Bull Terrier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 10–14 in (25.5–35.5 cm) at the shoulder, with weight in proportion to height. Most weigh about 8–12.5 kg (18–28 lb). A Mini Bull reaches around mid-shin on an adult, but it's muscular and solid, heavy enough to put it in the medium size class on this site. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Most Mini Bulls are too heavy for the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit once you add a carrier.

Miniature vs standard Bull Terrier

The Bull Terrier has no height limits and is much bigger, typically about 53–56 cm and twice the weight or more. The Mini is the same in shape and temperament, just smaller. Next to a Sealyham Terrier, a Mini Bull is a little taller and similar in weight.

Temperament and exercise

Mini Bull Terriers are playful, funny and affectionate, very attached to their people. They have a lot of energy and need daily exercise and play. They can be stubborn and may not get on with other dogs, so early socialisation and consistent, reward-based training matter. Some develop compulsive behaviours such as tail chasing. They don't like being left alone for long.

Coat and health

The short, flat coat needs very little grooming. White Mini Bulls are prone to sunburn and are more often deaf, so ask breeders whether puppies have been BAER hearing tested. Primary lens luxation (a displaced lens in the eye) is common in the breed, and a DNA test exists for it. Kidney disease and heart conditions are also known. Mini Bulls often live 11–13 years.

Miniature Bull Terrier size: common questions

How big does a Miniature Bull Terrier get?

An adult Miniature Bull Terrier stands 26–36 cm (10–14 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 8.2–13 kg (18–28 lb).

How tall is a Miniature Bull Terrier in feet?

An adult Miniature Bull Terrier stands 10 in to 1 ft 2 in tall at the shoulder (26–36 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, that reaches about mid-shin.

Is a Miniature Bull Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 10 kg (23 lb), the Miniature Bull Terrier counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 19% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does a Miniature Bull Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 11–13 years, shorter than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Miniature Bull Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Miniature Bull Terrier weighs 8.2–13 kg (18–28 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
