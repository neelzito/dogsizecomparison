How big is a Dogue de Bordeaux?

The Dogue de Bordeaux is a giant-sized dog breed from France, in the Working group. Males stand 59–69 cm (23–27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 50–65 kg (110–143 lb). Females stand 59–66 cm (23–26 in) and weigh 45–57 kg (99–126 lb). On average it is taller than 79% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 95%.

Dogue de Bordeaux compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Dogue de Bordeaux stands 64 cm (25 in) at the shoulder: 36% of the person's height, reaching about mid-thigh. An average female, at 62 cm (24.5 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 1 ft 11 in to 2 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder and females 1 ft 11 in to 2 ft 2 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about hip height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 5–8 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

About the Dogue de Bordeaux

The Dogue de Bordeaux, or French Mastiff, is one of the oldest French breeds, from the Bordeaux region in south-west France. For centuries it worked as a guard, a drover of cattle, a hunter of boar and bear and, sadly, a fighting dog. The breed nearly vanished after the World Wars, but French enthusiasts rebuilt it. It became famous worldwide in 1989 as Tom Hanks's co-star in Turner & Hooch. With its massive head, red coat and serious expression, it is one of the most impressive mastiffs.

What a Dogue's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 23.5–27 in (60–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 23–26 in (58.5–66 cm) for females. It sets minimum weights of 110 lb (50 kg) for males and 99 lb (45 kg) for females, and many dogs are heavier. A Dogue reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. Its head is one of the largest of any breed relative to body size. It's heavy enough to put it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle and a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Dogue vs Bullmastiff

The Bullmastiff is a little taller on average and a little lighter. The Dogue has a broader head, more wrinkles and a shorter muzzle, and it is always some shade of fawn to mahogany.

Temperament

Dogues are calm, loyal and very affectionate with their family, often gentle with children they know. They're naturally protective and can be wary of strangers or other dogs. They're strong and stubborn, so early socialisation and consistent, confident training are essential. They need moderate exercise and struggle in the heat because of their short muzzle. Expect a lot of drool.

Health and a short life

Unfortunately, the Dogue de Bordeaux has one of the shortest life spans of any breed, often only 5–8 years. Known problems include heart disease, cancer, bloat, hip and elbow dysplasia, and skin and eye problems in the facial folds. Choose a breeder who health-tests the parents, and ask how long their relatives lived.

Dogue de Bordeaux size: common questions

How big does a Dogue de Bordeaux get?

A fully grown male Dogue de Bordeaux stands 59–69 cm (23–27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 50–65 kg (110–143 lb). Females stand 59–66 cm (23–26 in) and weigh 45–57 kg (99–126 lb).

How tall is a Dogue de Bordeaux in feet?

A male Dogue de Bordeaux stands 1 ft 11 in to 2 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder (59–69 cm) and a female 1 ft 11 in to 2 ft 2 in (59–66 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about mid-thigh.

Is a Dogue de Bordeaux a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 54 kg (120 lb), the Dogue de Bordeaux counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is taller than 79% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does a Dogue de Bordeaux live?

Typical life expectancy is 5–8 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Can a Dogue de Bordeaux fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Dogue de Bordeaux weighs 45–65 kg (99–143 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
