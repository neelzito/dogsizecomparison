How big is a Weimaraner?

The Weimaraner is a large-sized dog breed from Germany, in the Sporting group. Males stand 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 32–41 kg (71–90 lb). Females stand 59–64 cm (23–25 in) and weigh 25–34 kg (55–75 lb). On average it is taller than 81% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 82%.

Weimaraner compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Weimaraner stands 66 cm (26 in) at the shoulder: 38% of the person's height, reaching about mid-thigh. An average female, at 61 cm (24 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 2 ft 1 in to 2 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder and females 1 ft 11 in to 2 ft 1 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about waist height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 10–13 years, shorter than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

About the Weimaraner

The Weimaraner was developed in the early 19th century at the court of Grand Duke Karl August of Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach in Germany. Nobles wanted an all-round hunting dog with speed, a good nose, courage and intelligence, at first for big game such as boar and deer, and later for birds. For decades the Germans guarded the breed closely, so few dogs left the country until the 20th century. Its silvery-grey coat and pale amber, grey or blue-grey eyes earned it the nickname "the grey ghost".

What a Weimaraner's size means at home

A male Weimaraner stands about 64–69 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 32–41 kg (70–90 lb). Females are about 59–64 cm and 25–34 kg. It is tall, lean and athletic, with a lot of strength. It needs a large car boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Weimaraner vs Vizsla

People often compare the two. The Vizsla is smaller and lighter, with a golden-rust coat. The Weimaraner is taller, heavier and grey. Both are affectionate "Velcro dogs" that want to be with their people all the time, and both need a lot of exercise.

Exercise and separation

Weimaraners are high-energy hunting dogs. They need at least an hour or two of hard exercise every day, such as running, swimming, retrieving or dog sports, plus mental challenges. They're very people-focused and are known for separation anxiety: left alone too long, many become destructive or noisy. They suit active owners who are often at home. Their prey drive is strong, so small pets and wildlife may not be safe.

Coat and care

The short, smooth grey coat needs very little grooming. A long-haired variety also exists, and the FCI recognises it, but it's rare. The short coat gives little protection against cold, so a coat helps in winter.

Health

Weimaraners are deep-chested, which puts them at high risk of bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus), a life-threatening emergency. Feed two or three smaller meals a day and avoid hard exercise right after eating. Hip dysplasia, eyelid problems and some immune and blood disorders are also known. Weimaraners often live 10–13 years.

Weimaraner size: common questions

How big does a Weimaraner get?

A fully grown male Weimaraner stands 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 32–41 kg (71–90 lb). Females stand 59–64 cm (23–25 in) and weigh 25–34 kg (55–75 lb).

How tall is a Weimaraner in feet?

A male Weimaraner stands 2 ft 1 in to 2 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder (64–69 cm) and a female 1 ft 11 in to 2 ft 1 in (59–64 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about mid-thigh.

Is a Weimaraner a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 33 kg (73 lb), the Weimaraner counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 81% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Weimaraner live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–13 years, shorter than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

Can a Weimaraner fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Weimaraner weighs 25–41 kg (55–90 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
