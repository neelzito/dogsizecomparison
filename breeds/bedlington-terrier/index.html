How big is a Bedlington Terrier?

The Bedlington Terrier is a small-sized dog breed from England, in the Terrier group. Adults stand 38–45 cm (15–17.5 in) at the shoulder and weigh 7.7–11 kg (17–23 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 37% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 26%. At the shoulder, an average Bedlington Terrier reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 11–16 years, about average for small breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Bedlington Terrier

The Bedlington Terrier comes from the mining town of Bedlington in Northumberland, in north-east England. Miners and Romany travellers bred it in the 18th and 19th centuries as a fast, tough terrier for catching rats, rabbits and badgers. Some greyhound or whippet ancestry gave it speed and an arched back. Its soft, curly coat and pear-shaped head make it look like a lamb, but underneath it's a true terrier.

What a Bedlington's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 16.5 in (42 cm) at the shoulder for males and 15.5 in (39.5 cm) for females, and about 7.5–10.5 kg (17–23 lb). A Bedlington reaches just below an adult's knee. It's lean, graceful and leggy, with an arched back. The crate size calculator suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate for an average adult because of its leg length. It's too heavy for most airline cabins.

Bedlington vs Welsh Terrier and Whippet

The Welsh Terrier is a little shorter and about the same weight, with a wiry black-and-tan coat. With its arched back and fast gallop, the Bedlington is also reminiscent of a small Whippet, and the two are sometimes crossed to make "Bedlington lurchers".

Temperament and exercise

Bedlingtons are gentle and affectionate at home, often calm and cuddly, but fast and determined outdoors. They need daily exercise and a chance to run in a safe area, and their chase instinct means recall can be unreliable. They can be scrappy with other dogs if challenged.

Coat and health

The soft, curly coat sheds very little but needs brushing a couple of times a week and clipping every 6–8 weeks. Bedlingtons are born dark (blue or liver) and lighten as they grow. The breed is known for copper toxicosis, an inherited liver disease caused by copper build-up, for which a DNA test exists. Eye problems and kidney disease also occur. Bedlingtons often live 11–16 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; The Kennel Club breed standard; Bedlington Terrier Club of America.

Bedlington Terrier size: common questions

How big does a Bedlington Terrier get?

An adult Bedlington Terrier stands 38–45 cm (15–17.5 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 7.7–11 kg (17–23 lb).

Is a Bedlington Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 9.1 kg (20 lb), the Bedlington Terrier counts as a small dog breed on this site (small = 5–10 kg (11–22 lb) on average). It is taller than 37% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Bedlington Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 11–16 years, about average for small breeds.

Can a Bedlington Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Bedlington Terrier weighs 7.7–11 kg (17–23 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
