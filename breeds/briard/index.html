How big is a Briard?

The Briard is a large-sized dog breed from France, in the Herding group. Males stand 59–69 cm (23–27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 25–46 kg (55–100 lb). Females stand 56–65 cm (22–25.6 in) and weigh 25–46 kg (55–100 lb). On average it is taller than 77% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 83%. At the shoulder, an average male Briard reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12 years, about average for large breeds.

About the Briard

The Briard (Berger de Brie) is an old French herding breed from the Brie region east of Paris. It herded and guarded sheep, often working independently over large areas. During the First World War, Briards served the French army as sentries, messengers and search dogs, finding wounded soldiers on the battlefield. With its long, shaggy coat, beard and eyebrows, it has been described as "a heart wrapped in fur". Like the Beauceron, it has double dewclaws on its hind legs, which the standard requires.

What a Briard's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 23–27 in (58.5–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22–25.5 in (56–65 cm) for females. Briards typically weigh about 25–45.5 kg (55–100 lb). A Briard reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and its coat makes it look even bigger. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Briard vs White Swiss Shepherd and Saarloos

The White Swiss Shepherd is about the same size, with a smoother white coat. The Saarloos Wolfdog is taller and a little heavier. The Briard's long coat can be black, grey or tawny.

Temperament and training

Briards are loyal, intelligent and protective, very devoted to their family and often reserved with strangers. They have a strong herding instinct and may try to herd children or other pets. They can be independent and strong-willed, so they need early socialisation and consistent, positive training. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they excel at herding, agility and obedience.

Coat care

The Briard's long, slightly wavy coat is a major commitment. It needs a thorough brush, down to the skin, at least a couple of times a week to prevent mats. The beard collects food, water and dirt and needs regular cleaning. The coat sheds little but traps everything from leaves to snow, so expect mess.

Health

Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat, hypothyroidism, and congenital stationary night blindness, an inherited eye disease for which a DNA test exists. Briards often live about 12 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 113 (Berger de Brie); Briard Club of America.

Briard size: common questions

How big does a Briard get?

A fully grown male Briard stands 59–69 cm (23–27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 25–46 kg (55–100 lb). Females stand 56–65 cm (22–25.6 in) and weigh 25–46 kg (55–100 lb).

Is a Briard a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 35 kg (78 lb), the Briard counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 77% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Briard live?

Typical life expectancy is 12 years, about average for large breeds.

Can a Briard fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Briard weighs 25–46 kg (55–100 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
