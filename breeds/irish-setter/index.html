How big is an Irish Setter?

The Irish Setter is a large-sized dog breed from Ireland, in the Sporting group. Males stand 69 cm (27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 32 kg (71 lb). Females stand 64 cm (25 in) and weigh 27 kg (60 lb). On average it is taller than 88% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 75%. At the shoulder, an average male Irish Setter reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

About the Irish Setter

The Irish Setter was developed in Ireland in the 18th century as a gundog that ranges ahead of the hunter, finds game birds and "sets", freezing or crouching to show where the birds are. Early Irish Setters were often red and white, and the solid mahogany red became fashionable in the 19th century. The Irish Red and White Setter survives as a separate breed. The film Big Red (1962) helped make the Irish Setter a popular family dog in the US. It's known for its glossy red coat, elegance and joyful, outgoing nature.

What an Irish Setter's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 27 in (68.5 cm) and 70 lb (32 kg) for males, and 25 in (63.5 cm) and 60 lb (27 kg) for females. An Irish Setter reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's tall, lean and racy, with long legs. Because of its height, the crate size calculator suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male. It needs a big boot and space to move.

Irish Setter vs Gordon Setter

The Gordon Setter from Scotland is a little shorter and similar in weight, with a black-and-tan coat and a heavier build. The English Setter is also a little shorter, usually white with coloured speckles.

Exercise and temperament

Irish Setters are friendly, enthusiastic and full of energy, and they love people and other dogs. They mature slowly and stay puppyish and boisterous for several years. They need at least one to two hours of exercise every day, ideally including free running in a safe area. Their urge to range and chase birds means recall needs a lot of work. They're sensitive and respond best to positive training.

Coat care

The long, silky red coat with feathering on the ears, chest, legs and tail needs brushing a few times a week to prevent tangles, and regular trimming of the feet and ears. It sheds moderately.

Health

Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat (as in other deep-chested breeds), hypothyroidism, epilepsy, and progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and canine leukocyte adhesion deficiency (CLAD), both of which have DNA tests. Irish Setters often live 12–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 120 (Irish Red Setter); The Kennel Club breed standard.

Irish Setter size: common questions

How big does an Irish Setter get?

A fully grown male Irish Setter stands 69 cm (27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 32 kg (71 lb). Females stand 64 cm (25 in) and weigh 27 kg (60 lb).

Is an Irish Setter a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 30 kg (65 lb), the Irish Setter counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 88% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does an Irish Setter live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

Can an Irish Setter fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Irish Setter weighs 27–32 kg (60–71 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
