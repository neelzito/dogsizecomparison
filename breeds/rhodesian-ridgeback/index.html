How big is a Rhodesian Ridgeback?

The Rhodesian Ridgeback is a large-sized dog breed from Southern Africa, in the Hound group. Males stand 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 39 kg (85 lb). Females stand 61–66 cm (24–26 in) and weigh 32 kg (71 lb). On average it is taller than 84% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 83%. At the shoulder, an average male Rhodesian Ridgeback reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

About the Rhodesian Ridgeback

The Rhodesian Ridgeback was developed in southern Africa by European settlers, who crossed their dogs with the half-wild, ridged dogs of the Khoikhoi people. They needed a tough, adaptable dog that could guard farms, cope with heat and little water, and hunt. In what was then Rhodesia (now Zimbabwe), packs of these dogs were famously used to track lions and hold them at bay until the hunters arrived, which earned the breed the nickname "African Lion Hound". The first breed standard was written in Bulawayo in 1922.

The ridge

The breed's trademark is the ridge: a strip of hair along the back that grows in the opposite direction to the rest of the coat, with two matching whorls ("crowns") at the front. It is caused by a genetic variant, and some Ridgebacks are born without it. The same variant is linked to dermoid sinus, a tube-like defect under the skin along the spine that can become infected. Breeders check puppies for it at birth, and it can be removed surgically.

What a Ridgeback's size means at home

The AKC standard gives a height range and one ideal weight: males stand about 64–69 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and should weigh about 38.5 kg (85 lb). Females stand about 61–66 cm and weigh about 32 kg (70 lb). Ridgebacks are muscular and athletic. They need a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Ridgeback vs Vizsla

At a glance, a Ridgeback can look like a large Vizsla, but it is taller and much heavier, with a darker wheaten coat and, of course, the ridge.

Temperament and training

Ridgebacks are loyal, dignified and affectionate with their families, and reserved with strangers. They're independent and intelligent, and they can be stubborn, so they need consistent, positive training from puppyhood. They need daily exercise and like to run, but they're usually calm at home. Their prey drive is strong, and they're good at stealing food off kitchen counters.

Care and health

The short coat needs very little grooming. Known health problems besides dermoid sinus include hip and elbow dysplasia, hypothyroidism and degenerative myelopathy, a spinal cord disease. Ridgebacks often live 10–12 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 146 (Rhodesian Ridgeback); Salmon Hillbertz et al., Nature Genetics (2007) on the ridge and dermoid sinus.

Rhodesian Ridgeback size: common questions

How big does a Rhodesian Ridgeback get?

A fully grown male Rhodesian Ridgeback stands 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 39 kg (85 lb). Females stand 61–66 cm (24–26 in) and weigh 32 kg (71 lb).

Is a Rhodesian Ridgeback a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 35 kg (78 lb), the Rhodesian Ridgeback counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 84% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Rhodesian Ridgeback live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a Rhodesian Ridgeback fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Rhodesian Ridgeback weighs 32–39 kg (71–85 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
