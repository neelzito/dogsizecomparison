How big is a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel?

The Cavalier King Charles Spaniel is a small-sized dog breed from England, in the Toy group. Adults stand 31–33 cm (12–13 in) at the shoulder and weigh 5.9–8.2 kg (13–18 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 22% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 17%. At the shoulder, an average Cavalier King Charles Spaniel reaches about mid-shin on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for small breeds. Lighter individuals fit airline cabin limits of 8 kg (17.6 lb) for dog and carrier; heavier ones need an airline that weighs only the carrier size, or travel in the hold.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel

The Cavalier King Charles Spaniel is named after King Charles II of England, who was famous for his small spaniels in the 17th century. Over the following centuries, those toy spaniels were bred to have flatter faces, and became today's King Charles Spaniel. In the 1920s an American, Roswell Eldridge, offered prize money at Crufts for spaniels that looked like the longer-nosed dogs in old paintings. Breeders took up the challenge, and the result was recognised as the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel. It is a gentle, affectionate companion that gets on with almost everyone.

What a Cavalier's size means at home

A Cavalier stands about 30–33 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult, and weighs about 6–8 kg (13–18 lb). That makes it a small, easy dog for a flat, a small car or a lap. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It's sturdier than many toy breeds and happy on long walks.

Can a Cavalier fly in the cabin?

Sometimes. Many airlines limit dog and carrier together to 8 kg (17.6 lb), so a light Cavalier of about 6 kg in a light carrier may fit, but heavier dogs won't. Use the airline cabin check for your dog and airline.

Cavalier vs King Charles Spaniel and Cavapoo

The King Charles Spaniel (called the English Toy Spaniel in the US) is slightly smaller, with a domed head and a much flatter face. The Cavapoo, a cross between a Cavalier and a Poodle, varies more in size and coat, depending on the Poodle parent.

Health: heart and brain

The Cavalier's biggest health problem is mitral valve disease (MVD), a heart valve disease. It usually starts with a murmur, and it starts much earlier in Cavaliers than in other breeds. More than half of Cavaliers have a murmur by about age five, and almost all do by age ten. Many Cavaliers also have syringomyelia, where fluid-filled cavities form in the spinal cord because the skull is too small for the brain. In one MRI study it was found in a quarter of one-year-olds and about 70% of dogs over six. Signs include scratching at the neck and shoulders, and pain. If you're buying a puppy, look for breeders who follow heart-testing and MRI screening schemes and can show the parents' results.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; The Kennel Club breed standard; Parker et al. (2011) MRI study of syringomyelia prevalence in Cavalier King Charles Spaniels; Cavalier Health (cavalierhealth.net).

Cavalier King Charles Spaniel size: common questions

How big does a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel get?

An adult Cavalier King Charles Spaniel stands 31–33 cm (12–13 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 5.9–8.2 kg (13–18 lb).

Is a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 7.1 kg (16 lb), the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel counts as a small dog breed on this site (small = 5–10 kg (11–22 lb) on average). It is taller than 22% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for small breeds.

Can a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel fly in the airplane cabin?

Sometimes. Lighter individuals fit airline cabin limits of 8 kg (17.6 lb) for dog and carrier; heavier ones need an airline that weighs only the carrier size, or travel in the hold. Rules vary by airline, so check before booking.
