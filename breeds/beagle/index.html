How big is a Beagle?

The Beagle is a medium-sized dog breed from England, in the Hound group. Males stand 33–38 cm (13–15 in) at the shoulder and weigh 9.5–14 kg (21–30 lb). Females stand 33–38 cm (13–15 in) and weigh 9–14 kg (20–30 lb). On average it is taller than 27% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 33%. At the shoulder, an average male Beagle reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–15 years, shorter than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Beagle

The Beagle is a small English scent hound, bred to hunt rabbits and hares in packs, with hunters following on foot. Hounds of this kind have been kept in England for centuries, and the modern Beagle took shape in the 19th century. Everything about it suits pack work: a superb nose, stamina, a cheerful, sociable nature with other dogs, and a loud, musical bay that lets hunters know where the pack is. Those traits make it a friendly family dog, but also one that follows its nose first.

Two sizes in the US

The AKC divides Beagles into two varieties by height at the shoulder: 13 inches (33 cm) and under, and over 13 up to 15 inches (38 cm). The UK and FCI standards give one range of 33–40 cm. This page shows the AKC range of 33–38 cm. At that size a Beagle reaches just below an adult's knee. That makes it a manageable mid-sized dog for flats and family cars. It usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. See the crate size calculator.

Heavier than it looks

Beagles weigh about 9–14 kg (20–30 lb), more than many dogs of similar height, because they are solidly built with a deep chest. That's too heavy for most airline cabins, where dog and carrier together are often limited to 8 kg (17.6 lb). They are also famously food-motivated and expert at stealing it, so overweight Beagles are very common. Measure meals, count treats, and keep bins and kitchen counters out of reach.

The nose rules

A Beagle on a scent can forget everything else, including its name. Many Beagles can't be trusted off the lead in open areas, and a secure garden fence is a must: they dig and squeeze through gaps. They also bay and howl, which neighbours may not love, especially if the dog is left alone. Beagles do best with company, whether that's people at home or another dog, plus daily walks long enough to let them sniff.

Beagle vs Basset Hound and Harrier

The Beagle is the smallest of the English pack hounds. The Basset Hound is shorter but much heavier and longer, and the Harrier looks like a larger Beagle. All three share the same nose and sociable pack temperament.

Beagle size: common questions

How big does a Beagle get?

A fully grown male Beagle stands 33–38 cm (13–15 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 9.5–14 kg (21–30 lb). Females stand 33–38 cm (13–15 in) and weigh 9–14 kg (20–30 lb).

Is a Beagle a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 11 kg (25 lb), the Beagle counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 27% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Beagle live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–15 years, shorter than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Beagle fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Beagle weighs 9–14 kg (20–30 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
