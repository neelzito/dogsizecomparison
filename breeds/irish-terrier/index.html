How big is an Irish Terrier?

The Irish Terrier is a medium-sized dog breed from Ireland, in the Terrier group. Males stand 46 cm (17.9 in) at the shoulder and weigh 12 kg (26 lb). Females stand 46 cm (17.9 in) and weigh 12 kg (25 lb). On average it is taller than 43% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 33%. At the shoulder, an average male Irish Terrier reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Irish Terrier

The Irish Terrier is one of the oldest terrier breeds, used on Irish farms for centuries to hunt rats, otters and other vermin, and to guard the home. Its fiery red coat and bold temperament earned it the nickname "the Daredevil". During the First World War, Irish Terriers served as messenger and sentry dogs in the trenches, and their courage became famous. Today the breed is a loyal, spirited companion, and less common than its reputation deserves.

What an Irish Terrier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 18 in (45.5 cm) at the shoulder, with weights of about 27 lb (12 kg) for males and 25 lb (11.5 kg) for females. An Irish Terrier reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's lean, racy and leggy for a terrier, longer in the leg than many of its cousins. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Irish Terrier vs other terriers

The Irish Terrier sits between the smaller Welsh and Lakeland Terriers and the much larger Airedale. Next to a Kromfohrländer, it's a little taller and similar in weight. Its solid red coat, from golden to deep red, sets it apart from most other terriers.

Temperament and exercise

Irish Terriers are bold, energetic and fiercely loyal, affectionate with their family and usually good with children. They have a strong prey drive and can be combative with other dogs, especially of the same sex, so early socialisation and supervision are important. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation and enjoy running, hiking, agility and games. They're clever but independent, so training should be consistent and varied.

Coat care

The dense, wiry coat sheds very little and keeps out the weather. To keep its texture and colour, it's traditionally hand-stripped a few times a year. Clipping is easier but softens the coat and dulls the colour. The beard needs regular cleaning.

Health

Irish Terriers are generally healthy. Known problems include hereditary footpad hyperkeratosis (thickened, cracked paw pads), for which a DNA test exists, and cystinuria (bladder stones), along with some eye conditions. They often live 13–15 years.

Irish Terrier size: common questions

How big does an Irish Terrier get?

A fully grown male Irish Terrier stands 46 cm (17.9 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 12 kg (26 lb). Females stand 46 cm (17.9 in) and weigh 12 kg (25 lb).

Is an Irish Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 12 kg (26 lb), the Irish Terrier counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 43% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an Irish Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can an Irish Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Irish Terrier weighs 12 kg (25–26 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
