How big is a Great Dane?

The Great Dane is a giant-sized dog breed from Germany, in the Working group. Males stand 76–82 cm (29.9–32.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 64–80 kg (140–175 lb). Females stand 71–76 cm (28–29.9 in) and weigh 50–64 kg (110–140 lb). On average that makes it the 4th tallest of the 256 breeds on this site. At the shoulder, an average male Great Dane reaches about hip height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 7–10 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

About the Great Dane

The Great Dane is one of the tallest dog breeds in the world, and also one of the gentlest. Despite its name, it isn't Danish: it was developed in Germany, where it is called the Deutsche Dogge (German mastiff). German nobles used these big, fast dogs to hunt wild boar and to guard their estates. Over time breeders softened the temperament, and today's Great Dane is a calm, friendly companion that is often called a "gentle giant". Great Danes have held the Guinness World Record for the tallest dog several times.

What a Great Dane's size means at home

A male Great Dane stands about 76–81 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's hip height, and can easily rest its head on a dining table. On its hind legs it can be taller than most people. Everything about owning one is large: a crate of 54″ (137 cm) or bigger (try the crate size calculator), a large estate car or van, a big bed and a big food bill. Its long, swinging tail can clear a coffee table. Because it is so heavy, it flies in the hold, and many airlines need a special giant-size travel crate for it.

How fast a Great Dane puppy grows

Great Danes grow extremely fast: from about 1 kg at birth to 45 kg (100 lb) or more by their first birthday. They usually reach their full height at around 15–18 months but keep filling out until about 2–3 years. Growing that fast puts a lot of strain on young bones and joints, so:

  • Feed a large- or giant-breed puppy food, and don't add extra calcium.
  • Keep puppies lean rather than chubby.
  • Avoid long runs, lots of jumping and endless stairs until the growth plates close.

Bloat and life span

Great Danes are the breed most at risk of gastric dilatation-volvulus (GDV, or "bloat"), where the stomach fills with gas and twists. It is a life-threatening emergency. Studies estimate that more than a third of Great Danes have bloat at some point in their lives. To lower the risk, feed two or three smaller meals a day rather than one large one, and avoid hard exercise right after eating. Many owners also ask their vet about a preventive gastropexy, an operation that stitches the stomach in place, often done at the same time as neutering. Signs of bloat include a swollen belly, retching without bringing anything up, and restlessness. If you see them, go to a vet at once.

Like most giant breeds, Great Danes have a short life, usually 7–10 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; Glickman et al., JAVMA (2000) on bloat risk factors in large and giant breeds.

Great Dane size: common questions

How big does a Great Dane get?

A fully grown male Great Dane stands 76–82 cm (29.9–32.1 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 64–80 kg (140–175 lb). Females stand 71–76 cm (28–29.9 in) and weigh 50–64 kg (110–140 lb).

Is a Great Dane a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 64 kg (141 lb), the Great Dane counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is the 4th tallest of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Great Dane live?

Typical life expectancy is 7–10 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Can a Great Dane fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Great Dane weighs 50–80 kg (110–175 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
