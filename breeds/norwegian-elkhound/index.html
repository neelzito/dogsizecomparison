How big is a Norwegian Elkhound?

The Norwegian Elkhound is a medium-sized dog breed from Norway, in the Hound group. Males stand 52 cm (20.5 in) at the shoulder and weigh 25 kg (55 lb). Females stand 50 cm (19.5 in) and weigh 22 kg (49 lb). On average it is taller than 53% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 57%. At the shoulder, an average male Norwegian Elkhound reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Norwegian Elkhound

The Norwegian Elkhound (Norsk Elghund Grå) is an ancient Nordic spitz used for thousands of years in the forests of Norway. Its main job was hunting moose, called "elk" in Europe. The dog finds a moose, holds it at bay by barking and dodging around it, and keeps it there until the hunter arrives. Elkhound-type dogs have been found in Viking-era graves, and the breed is Norway's national dog. It's a hardy, bold and friendly companion, still used for hunting in Scandinavia.

What an Elkhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 20.5 in (52 cm) at the shoulder and 55 lb (25 kg) for males, and 19.5 in (49.5 cm) and 48 lb (22 kg) for females. An Elkhound reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's square, compact and sturdy, with a tightly curled tail. It suits most homes, and the crate size calculator suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Elkhound vs Harrier

The Harrier, an English pack hound, is about the same height and weight, but a very different type of dog. The Elkhound is a spitz with a thick coat and curled tail, the Harrier a short-coated scent hound. The Bavarian Mountain Scent Hound is a little shorter.

Temperament and exercise

Elkhounds are friendly, confident and independent, very loyal to their family and usually good with children. They were bred to work independently and bark at game, so they're vocal and can be stubborn. Patient, consistent training works best, and recall can be unreliable. They need daily exercise and enjoy hiking, especially in cold weather. They love food, so watch their weight.

Coat and health

The thick, weatherproof double coat sheds a lot, with heavy moults once or twice a year. It needs brushing a couple of times a week and daily during moults. The coat insulates well against cold but makes Elkhounds sensitive to heat. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and Fanconi syndrome, a kidney disease. Elkhounds often live 12–15 years.

Norwegian Elkhound size: common questions

How big does a Norwegian Elkhound get?

A fully grown male Norwegian Elkhound stands 52 cm (20.5 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 25 kg (55 lb). Females stand 50 cm (19.5 in) and weigh 22 kg (49 lb).

Is a Norwegian Elkhound a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 24 kg (52 lb), the Norwegian Elkhound counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 53% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Norwegian Elkhound live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Norwegian Elkhound fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Norwegian Elkhound weighs 22–25 kg (49–55 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
