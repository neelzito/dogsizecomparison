How big is a Silky Terrier?

The Silky Terrier is a toy dog breed from Australia, in the Toy group. Adults stand 23–26 cm (9.1–10 in) at the shoulder and weigh 4.1–5 kg (9–11 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 4% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 7%. At the shoulder, an average Silky Terrier reaches about mid-shin on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, about average for toy breeds. At 4.1–5 kg (9–11 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb).

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Silky Terrier

The Silky Terrier (called the Australian Silky Terrier in many countries) was developed in Australia in the late 19th century by crossing Yorkshire Terriers with Australian Terriers. The result was a small, silky-coated terrier with more substance than a Yorkie and plenty of terrier spirit. It was used to kill rats and snakes around homes and farms. Today it's a lively, affectionate companion, sometimes mistaken for a large Yorkshire Terrier.

What a Silky's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 9–10 in (23–25.5 cm) at the shoulder and about 8–10 lb (3.5–4.5 kg). The figures on this page show about 4–5 kg. A Silky reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's fine-boned but sturdy, a little longer than tall. It suits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Flying with a Silky

A Silky Terrier is an easy size for the cabin, staying well under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set, even with a carrier. Check with the airline cabin check.

Silky vs Yorkshire Terrier, Havanese and Japanese Chin

A Silky is a little taller and heavier than a Yorkshire Terrier. Its coat is silky but shorter, usually not reaching the ground, and its head is more wedge-shaped. The Havanese and the Japanese Chin are similar in size but are companion breeds, much less terrier-like in temperament.

Temperament and exercise

Silkies are alert, curious, bold and energetic, real terriers in a small body. They need daily walks and play, and they love to dig and chase, so a secure garden is important. They can be bossy with other dogs and vocal. They're clever and learn quickly with reward-based training, but they can be stubborn.

Coat and health

The straight, silky coat sheds very little but needs brushing several times a week to prevent tangles, and regular trimming. It comes in blue and tan. Known health problems include loose kneecaps, Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (a hip condition), tracheal collapse, allergies and dental problems. Silkies often live 13–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 236 (Australian Silky Terrier); Silky Terrier Club of America.

Silky Terrier size: common questions

How big does a Silky Terrier get?

An adult Silky Terrier stands 23–26 cm (9.1–10 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 4.1–5 kg (9–11 lb).

Is a Silky Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 4.6 kg (10 lb), the Silky Terrier counts as a toy dog breed on this site (toy = under 5 kg (11 lb) on average). It is taller than 4% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Silky Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, about average for toy breeds.

Can a Silky Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually yes. At 4.1–5 kg (9–11 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb). Check your airline's rules and carrier size limits before booking.
