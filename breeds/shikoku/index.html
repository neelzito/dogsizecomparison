How big is a Shikoku?

The Shikoku is a medium-sized dog breed from Japan, in the Working group. Adults stand 43–56 cm (16.9–22 in) at the shoulder and weigh 16–25 kg (35–55 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 52% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 52%. At the shoulder, an average Shikoku reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, shorter than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Shikoku

The Shikoku is a medium-sized Japanese spitz from the mountains of Kōchi Prefecture on the island of Shikoku. It was bred to hunt wild boar and deer in steep, forested terrain, and it's known for its stamina, agility and determination. It was declared a natural monument of Japan in 1937, and in Japan it's sometimes called the Kōchi-ken. It's one of the six native Japanese spitz breeds, between the Shiba and the Akita in size, and still rare outside Japan.

What a Shikoku's size means at home

The FCI standard gives about 52 cm at the shoulder for males and 46 cm for females, with a small tolerance. The figures on this page, about 43–56 cm and 16–25 kg (35–55 lb), cover both sexes. A Shikoku reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's well-muscled, agile and compact. The crate size calculator suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Shikoku vs Shiba, Kai Ken and Akita

The Shikoku is taller and heavier than the Shiba Inu and much smaller than the Akita. The Kai Ken, another Japanese hunting spitz, is similar in height but lighter, and usually brindle. The Shikoku comes in sesame, black sesame and red sesame. Next to a Portuguese Water Dog, it's a little shorter and lighter.

Temperament and exercise

Shikokus are loyal, alert and energetic, devoted to their family and often reserved with strangers. They're intelligent but independent, with a strong prey drive. Recall can be unreliable, and small pets may not be safe. They need plenty of daily exercise and enjoy hiking and running. They can be wary of other dogs, so early socialisation is important. They suit experienced owners who know spitz breeds.

Coat and health

The thick double coat sheds heavily once or twice a year and needs regular brushing, daily during moults. Shikokus are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, allergies and eye conditions. They often live 10–12 years.

Sources: FCI standard No. 319 (Shikoku); AKC breed information; Nihon Ken Hozonkai (NIPPO).

Shikoku size: common questions

How big does a Shikoku get?

An adult Shikoku stands 43–56 cm (16.9–22 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 16–25 kg (35–55 lb).

Is a Shikoku a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 21 kg (45 lb), the Shikoku counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 52% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Shikoku live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, shorter than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Shikoku fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Shikoku weighs 16–25 kg (35–55 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
