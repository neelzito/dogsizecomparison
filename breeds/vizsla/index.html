How big is a Vizsla?

The Vizsla is a medium-sized dog breed from Hungary, in the Sporting group. Males stand 56–61 cm (22–24 in) at the shoulder and weigh 25–27 kg (55–60 lb). Females stand 54–59 cm (21.1–23 in) and weigh 20–25 kg (44–55 lb). On average it is taller than 63% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 61%. At the shoulder, an average male Vizsla reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Vizsla

The Vizsla, also called the Magyar Vizsla or Hungarian Pointer, is Hungary's national pointing dog. Its ancestors were hunting dogs of the Magyar tribes who settled in the Carpathian Basin over a thousand years ago. Later, Hungarian nobles refined it into an all-round gundog that points, flushes and retrieves birds and hares, on land and in water. The breed nearly vanished after the World Wars, but Hungarian emigrants took dogs abroad and helped save it. Today the Vizsla is admired for its golden-rust coat, elegance and gentle, devoted nature.

What a Vizsla's size means at home

A male Vizsla stands about 56–61 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 25–27 kg (55–60 lb). Females stand about 53–59 cm and weigh 20–25 kg. It is lean and athletic, lighter than it looks for its height. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate (check with the crate size calculator) and a home with space and access to open countryside.

The "Velcro dog"

Vizslas are known as "Velcro dogs" because they love to be physically close to their people, following them from room to room and leaning against their legs. They don't do well left alone for long hours and can become anxious or destructive. If you work away from home all day, a Vizsla may not be the right choice.

Vizsla vs Weimaraner, GSP and Rhodesian Ridgeback

  • The Weimaraner is bigger and heavier, with a grey coat.
  • The German Shorthaired Pointer is a little taller and heavier, usually liver and white.
  • The Rhodesian Ridgeback is taller and much heavier, with its typical ridge of hair along the back.
  • The Wirehaired Vizsla is a separate breed with the same colour but a wiry coat and beard.

Exercise and care

A Vizsla needs a lot of exercise: at least an hour or two a day of running, swimming or retrieving, plus mental work. It's an excellent partner for runners and cyclists once fully grown. The short coat needs very little grooming and has little odour, but offers little protection against cold, so a coat helps in winter.

Health

Vizslas are generally healthy. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, epilepsy, allergies and some eye conditions, so ask breeders about health tests. They often live 12–14 years.

Vizsla size: common questions

How big does a Vizsla get?

A fully grown male Vizsla stands 56–61 cm (22–24 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 25–27 kg (55–60 lb). Females stand 54–59 cm (21.1–23 in) and weigh 20–25 kg (44–55 lb).

Is a Vizsla a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 24 kg (53 lb), the Vizsla counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 63% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Vizsla live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Vizsla fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Vizsla weighs 20–27 kg (44–60 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
