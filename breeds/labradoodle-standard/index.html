How big is a Labradoodle (Standard)?

The Labradoodle (Standard) is a medium-sized dog breed from Australia, in the designer & mixed category. Males stand 53–61 cm (20.9–24 in) at the shoulder and weigh 23–30 kg (51–66 lb). Females stand 53–58 cm (20.9–22.8 in) and weigh 18–25 kg (40–55 lb). On average it is taller than 62% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 61%. At the shoulder, an average male Labradoodle (Standard) reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Labradoodle (Standard)

The Labradoodle is a cross between a Labrador Retriever and a Poodle. It became famous in 1989, when Wally Conron of the Royal Guide Dog Association of Australia bred one for a blind woman whose husband was allergic to dogs. The idea of a low-shedding guide dog caught on, and Labradoodles quickly spread around the world. Conron later said he regretted starting the craze, because of the many careless breeders it attracted. Today there are two main kinds: first-generation Labradoodles (a Labrador crossed with a Poodle) and the Australian Labradoodle, a multi-generation type that also includes Cocker Spaniel ancestry and is bred towards its own standard. Neither is recognised by the major kennel clubs.

How big does a Labradoodle get?

Size depends mainly on the Poodle parent. Labradoodles are usually described as:

  • Standard: about 53–61 cm at the shoulder and 23–30 kg (50–65 lb) for males
  • Medium: about 43–51 cm
  • Miniature: about 35–43 cm

This page shows the Standard Labradoodle, which reaches about knee height on an adult. It is a little shorter and lighter than a Labrador on average, and very close to a Standard Poodle. Puppies from the same litter can vary a lot, so ask about both parents' size, and use the puppy weight calculator as a rough guide.

What a Labradoodle's size means at home

A Standard Labradoodle is a medium-sized, active dog that needs an estate car or SUV boot and usually a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It needs daily exercise and is far too heavy for an airline cabin.

Coat: low-shedding isn't guaranteed

A Labradoodle's coat can be curly (fleece or wool, like a Poodle), wavy, or flat and shedding like a Labrador's. First-generation crosses are especially unpredictable. No dog is truly hypoallergenic, so if allergies are the reason you want one, spend time with the actual puppy's parents first. Curly and wavy coats don't shed much but mat easily, so they need brushing several times a week and a clip every 6–8 weeks.

Labradoodle vs Goldendoodle

The Goldendoodle is very similar in size, slightly taller on average, with a softer, often longer coat. Both are friendly, clever, energetic family dogs. Their temperament reflects the Labrador or Golden Retriever as much as the Poodle.

Sources: Australian Labradoodle Association of America; Wally Conron interviews (2014, 2019); AKC breed standards for the Labrador Retriever and the Poodle.

Labradoodle (Standard) size: common questions

How big does a Labradoodle (Standard) get?

A fully grown male Labradoodle (Standard) stands 53–61 cm (20.9–24 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 23–30 kg (51–66 lb). Females stand 53–58 cm (20.9–22.8 in) and weigh 18–25 kg (40–55 lb).

Is a Labradoodle (Standard) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 24 kg (53 lb), the Labradoodle (Standard) counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 62% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Labradoodle (Standard) live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Labradoodle (Standard) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Labradoodle (Standard) weighs 18–30 kg (40–66 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
