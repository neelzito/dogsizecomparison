How big is a Bulldog (English)?

The Bulldog (English) is a medium-sized dog breed from England, in the Non-Sporting group. Males stand 36–38 cm (14–15 in) at the shoulder and weigh 23 kg (50 lb). Females stand 36–38 cm (14–15 in) and weigh 18 kg (40 lb). On average it is taller than 30% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 52%. At the shoulder, an average male Bulldog (English) reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 8–10 years, shorter than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Bulldog (English)

The Bulldog (often called the English or British Bulldog) has a dark history. It was originally bred in England for bull-baiting, a blood sport in which dogs attacked a tethered bull. When the sport was banned in 1835, the breed nearly died out. Fanciers then bred it into the gentle companion it is today: calm, affectionate and good-natured, though still stubborn. Its jowly face and rolling walk made it a national symbol of Britain and a popular mascot.

What a Bulldog's size means at home

A Bulldog stands only about 36–38 cm at the shoulder, just below an adult's knee, but males weigh about 23 kg (50 lb) and females 18 kg (40 lb). That makes it one of the heaviest breeds for its height. It is wide and low, with a broad chest and short, sturdy legs. A Bulldog is happy in a flat and doesn't need long walks. It usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Lifting one into a car is a real effort, so a ramp helps.

Bulldog vs French Bulldog

The French Bulldog descends from small English bulldogs, but today the two are very different in size. A Bulldog is a little taller and about twice as heavy. The Frenchie has upright bat ears, while the Bulldog has small folded "rose" ears.

Breathing, heat and water

Like other flat-faced (brachycephalic) breeds, many Bulldogs have narrowed airways. They snore, tire quickly, and can overheat dangerously in warm weather or after exercise. Keep walks short and in the cool part of the day, and never leave a Bulldog in a warm car. Most Bulldogs can't swim because of their heavy front and short legs, so a life jacket and supervision near water are essential. Many airlines don't carry snub-nosed breeds in the hold, and a Bulldog is far too heavy for the cabin. Check your airline's rules before you plan a trip.

Health to know about

Bulldog puppies' large heads mean that most litters are born by caesarean section: one UK study found more than 80%. Skin folds on the face need regular cleaning to prevent infections. Joint problems, cherry eye and breathing problems are common, and life expectancy is short, around 8–10 years. If you're buying a puppy, look for breeders who select for open nostrils, a slightly longer nose and good breathing, and ask about breathing assessments for the parents.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; The Kennel Club breed standard; Evans & Adams, Journal of Small Animal Practice (2010) on caesarean rates.

Bulldog (English) size: common questions

How big does a Bulldog (English) get?

A fully grown male Bulldog (English) stands 36–38 cm (14–15 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 23 kg (50 lb). Females stand 36–38 cm (14–15 in) and weigh 18 kg (40 lb).

Is a Bulldog (English) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 20 kg (45 lb), the Bulldog (English) counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 30% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Bulldog (English) live?

Typical life expectancy is 8–10 years, shorter than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Bulldog (English) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Bulldog (English) weighs 18–23 kg (40–50 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
