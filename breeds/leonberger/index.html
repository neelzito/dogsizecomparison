How big is a Leonberger?

The Leonberger is a giant-sized dog breed from Germany, in the Working group. Males stand 71–80 cm (28–31.5 in) at the shoulder and weigh 50–77 kg (110–170 lb). Females stand 65–75 cm (25.6–29.5 in) and weigh 41–64 kg (90–140 lb). On average that makes it the 9th tallest of the 267 breeds on this site.

Leonberger compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Leonberger stands 76 cm (29.7 in) at the shoulder: 43% of the person's height, reaching about hip height. An average female, at 70 cm (27.6 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 7 in tall at the shoulder and females 2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 6 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about waist height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 7 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

About the Leonberger

The Leonberger comes from the town of Leonberg near Stuttgart in Germany. In the 1840s, Heinrich Essig, the town's mayor, set out to breed a dog that looked like the lion in the town's coat of arms. He is said to have crossed Saint Bernards, Newfoundlands and Pyrenean Mountain Dogs. The big, lion-like dogs became fashionable with European royalty, including Empress Elisabeth of Austria. Both World Wars nearly wiped out the breed, and it was rebuilt from just a handful of dogs. Today the Leonberger is a gentle, family-loving giant.

What a Leonberger's size means at home

A male Leonberger stands about 71–80 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's hip, and weighs about 50–77 kg (110–170 lb). Females stand about 65–75 cm and weigh 41–64 kg (90–140 lb). With its thick mane, it looks even bigger. It needs a large vehicle, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (check with the crate size calculator) and lots of space. Leonbergers often like to lean against their people, and at that weight it is noticeable.

Leonberger vs Newfoundland and Landseer

The Leonberger is taller than the Newfoundland, with a similar weight, a lion-coloured coat and a black mask. The Landseer, a black-and-white relative of the Newfoundland, is about the same size.

Temperament and exercise

Leonbergers are calm, patient and friendly, famously good with children and usually easy-going with other dogs. They need daily walks and enjoy swimming, but they aren't built for long runs. They want to be part of family life and don't do well left alone in a garden or kennel.

Coat and care

The Leonberger's long, water-resistant double coat sheds heavily, especially twice a year. It needs brushing several times a week, and daily during moults. Like other heavy-coated giants, Leonbergers struggle in the heat and need shade, water and rest on warm days. Expect some drool and muddy paws.

Health and life span

The Leonberger has a short life, about 7 years on average. Cancer, especially bone cancer and hemangiosarcoma, is the most common cause of death. Other known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, and two inherited nerve diseases, Leonberger polyneuropathy and leukoencephalomyelopathy, for which DNA tests exist. Ask breeders for the parents' DNA and hip and elbow results.

Leonberger size: common questions

How big does a Leonberger get?

A fully grown male Leonberger stands 71–80 cm (28–31.5 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 50–77 kg (110–170 lb). Females stand 65–75 cm (25.6–29.5 in) and weigh 41–64 kg (90–140 lb).

How tall is a Leonberger in feet?

A male Leonberger stands 2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 7 in tall at the shoulder (71–80 cm) and a female 2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 6 in (65–75 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about hip height.

Is a Leonberger a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 58 kg (128 lb), the Leonberger counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is the 9th tallest of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Leonberger live?

Typical life expectancy is 7 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Can a Leonberger fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Leonberger weighs 41–77 kg (90–170 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
