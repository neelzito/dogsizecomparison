How big is a Chinook?

The Chinook is a large-sized dog breed from the United States, in the Working group. Males stand 61–66 cm (24–26 in) at the shoulder and weigh 25–41 kg (55–90 lb). Females stand 56–61 cm (22–24 in) and weigh 23–30 kg (50–65 lb). On average it is taller than 71% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 74%. At the shoulder, an average male Chinook reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

About the Chinook

The Chinook was developed in New Hampshire in the 1910s by Arthur Walden, who crossed a large mastiff-type farm dog with Greenland sled dogs to produce a strong, friendly sled dog. Walden's lead dog, Chinook, gave the breed its name. Chinooks took part in Admiral Byrd's Antarctic expedition in 1928. The breed later became very rare, but it was saved by dedicated breeders. It's the state dog of New Hampshire, and the AKC recognised it in 2013.

What a Chinook's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24–26 in (61–66 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22–24 in (56–61 cm) for females, with weights of about 25–41 kg (55–90 lb) for males and 22.5–29.5 kg (50–65 lb) for females. A Chinook reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Chinook vs Akita and Boxer

The Japanese Akita and the Boxer are about the same size. The Chinook is always tawny, from honey to reddish-gold, with dark ears and muzzle.

Temperament and exercise

Chinooks are gentle, friendly and calm, very good with children and other dogs. They need plenty of exercise and enjoy pulling sports and hiking. They're intelligent and eager to please, but sensitive.

Care and health

The double coat sheds seasonally. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, seizures and allergies. Chinooks often live 12–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; Chinook Club of America.

Chinook size: common questions

How big does a Chinook get?

A fully grown male Chinook stands 61–66 cm (24–26 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 25–41 kg (55–90 lb). Females stand 56–61 cm (22–24 in) and weigh 23–30 kg (50–65 lb).

Is a Chinook a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 30 kg (65 lb), the Chinook counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 71% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Chinook live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a Chinook fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Chinook weighs 23–41 kg (50–90 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
