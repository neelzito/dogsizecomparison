How big is a Scottish Deerhound?

The Scottish Deerhound is a large-sized dog breed from Scotland, in the Hound group. Males stand 76–82 cm (29.9–32.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 39–50 kg (85–110 lb). Females stand 71–76 cm (28–29.9 in) and weigh 34–43 kg (75–95 lb). On average that makes it the 5th tallest of the 256 breeds on this site. At the shoulder, an average male Scottish Deerhound reaches about hip height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 8–11 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

About the Scottish Deerhound

The Scottish Deerhound is an ancient sighthound from the Scottish Highlands, bred to course and bring down red deer. For centuries, it was the dog of Highland chiefs, and according to tradition no one below the rank of earl was allowed to own one. The breed declined when deer hunting with guns replaced coursing and the clan system collapsed after 1745, but enthusiasts revived it in the 19th century. Sir Walter Scott called his Deerhound, Maida, "the most perfect creature of heaven". Today it's a gentle, quiet and dignified giant.

What a Deerhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 30–32 in (76–81.5 cm) and above at the shoulder and 85–110 lb (38.5–50 kg) for males, and from 28 in (71 cm) and 75–95 lb (34–43 kg) for females. A Deerhound reaches roughly an adult's hip. It's very tall but slim and light for its height. It needs a big vehicle and, because of its height, the crate size calculator suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male. A Deerhound's head is easily level with a kitchen worktop, so keep food out of reach.

Deerhound vs Irish Wolfhound and Borzoi

The Irish Wolfhound looks like a bigger, heavier Deerhound: taller and much more solid. The Borzoi is about the same height and a little lighter, with a silky coat. The Deerhound's coat is harsh and wiry, usually blue-grey, grey or brindle.

Temperament and exercise

Deerhounds are gentle, friendly and calm, quiet at home and good with children and other dogs. They're too friendly to make good guard dogs. Like all sighthounds, they love to run and have a strong prey drive, so they need regular chances to sprint in a large, secure area. They can't hear you at full speed. They're sensitive and respond best to gentle training.

Care and health

The rough, wiry coat needs only weekly brushing and occasional tidying. Deerhounds need soft bedding, as their bony joints are prone to calluses. Known health problems include bloat, heart disease (cardiomyopathy), bone cancer and cystinuria, a bladder stone disorder. Like many giant breeds, Deerhounds have a short life, usually 8–11 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 164 (Deerhound); Scottish Deerhound Club of America.

Scottish Deerhound size: common questions

How big does a Scottish Deerhound get?

A fully grown male Scottish Deerhound stands 76–82 cm (29.9–32.1 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 39–50 kg (85–110 lb). Females stand 71–76 cm (28–29.9 in) and weigh 34–43 kg (75–95 lb).

Is a Scottish Deerhound a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 41 kg (91 lb), the Scottish Deerhound counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is the 5th tallest of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Scottish Deerhound live?

Typical life expectancy is 8–11 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a Scottish Deerhound fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Scottish Deerhound weighs 34–50 kg (75–110 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
