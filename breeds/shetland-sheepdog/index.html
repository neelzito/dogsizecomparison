How big is a Shetland Sheepdog?

The Shetland Sheepdog is a small-sized dog breed from Scotland (Shetland), in the Herding group. Adults stand 33–41 cm (13–15.9 in) at the shoulder and weigh 6.8–12 kg (15–25 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 30% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 27%. At the shoulder, an average Shetland Sheepdog reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, shorter than the 13.7-year average for small breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Shetland Sheepdog

The Shetland Sheepdog, or "Sheltie", comes from the Shetland Islands north of mainland Scotland, where small, hardy animals, from Shetland ponies to small sheep, were the norm. Island farmers used small herding dogs to keep sheep and ponies away from crops. In the early 20th century, breeders crossed these dogs with Rough Collies and other breeds, giving the Sheltie its Collie-like look. Although it looks like a miniature Rough Collie, it is a separate breed with its own history. Today it is a gentle, clever and sensitive companion, and a star at agility and obedience.

What a Sheltie's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 13–16 in (33–40.5 cm) at the shoulder, around just below an adult's knee. Most weigh about 7–11.5 kg (15–25 lb). Its thick coat makes it look bigger than it is. It suits flats and houses and usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Most Shelties are too heavy for the cabin once you add a carrier.

Sheltie vs Rough Collie

The Rough Collie is much bigger: about 20–25 cm taller and roughly three times as heavy. The Sheltie is more compact, often more energetic, and quicker to bark. Both have the same long, double coat and come in sable, tricolour and blue merle.

Temperament and training

Shelties are very intelligent, eager to please and among the easiest breeds to train. They're devoted to their families and often reserved with strangers. Many are barkers, which made them useful as farm alarms. They're sensitive and respond best to gentle, reward-based training. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they love dog sports.

Coat and health

The Sheltie's long double coat sheds a lot, especially seasonally. It needs brushing several times a week, with extra attention behind the ears and on the "trousers" where it mats. Like Collies, many Shelties carry the MDR1 gene variant, which makes them sensitive to some common medicines, so a DNA test is worth doing. Collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism and dermatomyositis (a skin and muscle disease) are also known. Shelties often live 12–14 years.

Shetland Sheepdog size: common questions

How big does a Shetland Sheepdog get?

An adult Shetland Sheepdog stands 33–41 cm (13–15.9 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 6.8–12 kg (15–25 lb).

Is a Shetland Sheepdog a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 9.2 kg (20 lb), the Shetland Sheepdog counts as a small dog breed on this site (small = 5–10 kg (11–22 lb) on average). It is taller than 30% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Shetland Sheepdog live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, shorter than the 13.7-year average for small breeds.

Can a Shetland Sheepdog fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Shetland Sheepdog weighs 6.8–12 kg (15–25 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
