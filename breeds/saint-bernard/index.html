How big is a Saint Bernard?

The Saint Bernard is a giant-sized dog breed from Switzerland, in the Working group. Males stand 71–76 cm (28–29.9 in) at the shoulder and weigh 64–82 kg (140–180 lb). Females stand 66–71 cm (26–28 in) and weigh 55–64 kg (120–140 lb). On average it is taller than 94% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is the 6th heaviest breed.

Saint Bernard compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Saint Bernard stands 74 cm (28.9 in) at the shoulder: 42% of the person's height, reaching about mid-thigh. An average female, at 69 cm (27 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 6 in tall at the shoulder and females 2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 4 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about waist height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 8–10 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

About the Saint Bernard

The Saint Bernard takes its name from the Great St Bernard Hospice, a refuge for travellers on one of the highest passes in the Swiss Alps. From the 17th century, the monks kept large dogs there that guided them through snow and fog and helped find lost or buried travellers. The most famous, Barry, is said to have saved more than 40 people in the early 1800s. His body is still on display in the Natural History Museum in Bern. The image of a Saint Bernard with a barrel of brandy round its neck is a myth, but the breed's gentle, patient nature is real.

What a Saint Bernard's size means at home

A male Saint Bernard stands about 71–76 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 64–82 kg (140–180 lb). Females stand about 66–71 cm and weigh 55–64 kg. With its massive head and body, a Saint fills a room and a car. It needs a van or very large estate, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (check with the crate size calculator) and a strong, calm owner on the lead. Expect a lot of drool.

Saint Bernard vs Newfoundland, Mastiff and Bernese

The Saint is a little taller and heavier than a Newfoundland, and much heavier than a Bernese Mountain Dog. The English Mastiff is bigger still, both taller and heavier.

Short coat or long coat

Saint Bernards come in two coats: short-haired (smooth, dense and closer to the original hospice dogs) and long-haired. Both shed heavily, especially twice a year, and need brushing at least weekly. Both are bred for cold mountains and struggle in heat, so they need shade, water and rest in summer.

Growth and health

Puppies grow enormously fast, gaining several kilos a month at their peak. Feed a giant-breed puppy food, keep them lean, and protect their joints from jumping, stairs and long walks until the growth plates close at around 18 months. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, heart disease (dilated cardiomyopathy), eyelid problems and bone cancer. Like most giant breeds, Saint Bernards live a short life, usually 8–10 years.

Saint Bernard size: common questions

How big does a Saint Bernard get?

A fully grown male Saint Bernard stands 71–76 cm (28–29.9 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 64–82 kg (140–180 lb). Females stand 66–71 cm (26–28 in) and weigh 55–64 kg (120–140 lb).

How tall is a Saint Bernard in feet?

A male Saint Bernard stands 2 ft 4 in to 2 ft 6 in tall at the shoulder (71–76 cm) and a female 2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 4 in (66–71 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about mid-thigh.

Is a Saint Bernard a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 66 kg (145 lb), the Saint Bernard counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is taller than 94% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does a Saint Bernard live?

Typical life expectancy is 8–10 years, shorter than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Can a Saint Bernard fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Saint Bernard weighs 55–82 kg (120–180 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
