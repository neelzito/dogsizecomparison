How big is a Korean Jindo?

The Korean Jindo is a medium-sized dog breed from South Korea, in the Working group. Males stand 49–56 cm (19.1–22 in) at the shoulder and weigh 18–23 kg (40–50 lb). Females stand 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) and weigh 14–18 kg (30–40 lb). On average it is taller than 52% of the 247 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 43%. At the shoulder, an average male Korean Jindo reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 14–15 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Korean Jindo size: common questions

How big does a Korean Jindo get?

A fully grown male Korean Jindo stands 49–56 cm (19.1–22 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 18–23 kg (40–50 lb). Females stand 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) and weigh 14–18 kg (30–40 lb).

Is a Korean Jindo a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 18 kg (40 lb), the Korean Jindo counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 52% of the 247 breeds listed.

How long does a Korean Jindo live?

Typical life expectancy is 14–15 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Korean Jindo fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Korean Jindo weighs 14–23 kg (30–50 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
