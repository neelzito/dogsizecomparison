How big is a Dalmatian?

The Dalmatian is a large-sized dog breed from Croatia, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 49–61 cm (19.1–24 in) at the shoulder and weigh 21–32 kg (45–71 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 58% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 65%. At the shoulder, an average Dalmatian reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 11–13 years, about average for large breeds.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Dalmatian

The Dalmatian is named after Dalmatia, the coastal region of today's Croatia, which the FCI lists as its country of origin, although its early history is uncertain. The breed found its true calling in Georgian and Victorian England as a carriage dog. Dalmatians ran alongside horse-drawn coaches to guard the horses and travellers, and they were known for their stamina. In the US they became the mascots of fire stations, running ahead of horse-drawn fire engines to clear the way. The spotted coat, made famous by 101 Dalmatians, is unique among dog breeds.

What a Dalmatian's size means at home

A Dalmatian stands about 48–61 cm at the shoulder, around an adult's knee or a bit higher, and weighs about 20–32 kg (45–70 lb). It is muscular, athletic and built for endurance. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate (check with the crate size calculator) and plenty of exercise. Dalmatians are too heavy for an airline cabin.

Dalmatian vs Labrador

Next to a Labrador, the Dalmatian is usually a little shorter and clearly lighter, with a narrower chest and a longer stride. It also has a lot more running stamina.

Spots and puppies

Dalmatian puppies are born pure white, and their spots appear over the first few weeks. Spots are usually black or liver (brown). Dalmatians shed a surprising amount for a short-coated dog, all year round.

Deafness

Deafness is linked to the gene that produces the white coat. Studies have found that roughly 18–30% of Dalmatians are deaf in one or both ears. Responsible breeders have every puppy's hearing tested with a BAER test before it goes to a new home. Dogs deaf in one ear usually live a normal life, but fully deaf dogs need special training and care.

Urinary stones and diet

Nearly all Dalmatians carry a gene variant that makes them excrete high levels of uric acid in their urine, which can form painful urinary stones, especially in males. A diet that isn't too high in purines and plenty of fresh water help prevent them. Some breeders produce "low uric acid" (LUA) Dalmatians, from a 1970s outcross to a Pointer, which don't carry the variant.

Temperament

Dalmatians are friendly, playful and energetic, with a lot of stamina. They need at least an hour or two of exercise a day and enjoy running, cycling alongside an owner or canicross. Without enough exercise, they can become restless. They often live 11–13 years.

Dalmatian size: common questions

How big does a Dalmatian get?

An adult Dalmatian stands 49–61 cm (19.1–24 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 21–32 kg (45–71 lb).

Is a Dalmatian a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 26 kg (58 lb), the Dalmatian counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 58% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Dalmatian live?

Typical life expectancy is 11–13 years, about average for large breeds.

Can a Dalmatian fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Dalmatian weighs 21–32 kg (45–71 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
