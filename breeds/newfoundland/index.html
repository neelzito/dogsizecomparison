How big is a Newfoundland?

The Newfoundland is a giant-sized dog breed from Canada, in the Working group. Males stand 71 cm (28 in) at the shoulder and weigh 59–68 kg (130–150 lb). Females stand 66 cm (26 in) and weigh 46–55 kg (100–120 lb). On average it is taller than 92% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 95%.

Newfoundland compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Newfoundland stands 71 cm (28 in) at the shoulder: 41% of the person's height, reaching about mid-thigh. An average female, at 66 cm (26 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 2 ft 4 in tall at the shoulder and females 2 ft 2 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about waist height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 9–10 years, about average for giant breeds.

About the Newfoundland

The Newfoundland comes from the island of Newfoundland, off the east coast of Canada. Fishermen used these huge, strong dogs to haul nets, carry loads and pull carts, and above all to rescue people from the cold Atlantic. Webbed feet, a water-resistant double coat and a powerful swimming stroke make the "Newfie" a natural water-rescue dog, and it is still trained for water rescue in several countries today. The breed is famous for its gentle, patient nature. The poet Lord Byron wrote a famous epitaph for his Newfoundland, Boatswain.

What a Newfoundland's size means at home

A male Newfoundland stands about 71 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 59–68 kg (130–150 lb). Females are about 66 cm and 45–55 kg (100–120 lb). With its massive head, broad body and thick coat, a Newfie looks enormous and fills a room. It needs a large vehicle, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (see the crate size calculator) and plenty of indoor space. Expect drool: on shirts, walls and ceilings.

Newfoundland vs Saint Bernard and Leonberger

These three gentle giants are often compared. The Saint Bernard is a little taller and can be heavier. The Leonberger is taller, with a similar weight and a lion-like mane. All three need lots of space, grooming and a strong owner.

Water, heat and exercise

Newfies love water and are happiest near a lake or the sea. They need moderate daily exercise rather than long runs. Their thick black, brown or black-and-white coat makes them very sensitive to heat, so in summer they need shade, water and rest. The coat sheds heavily, especially twice a year, and needs brushing several times a week.

Growth and health

Newfoundland puppies grow enormously fast, so feed a large- or giant-breed puppy food, keep them lean and avoid hard exercise or lots of stairs while the joints develop. They usually reach full size at around 2 years. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, a heart condition called subaortic stenosis (SAS), and cystinuria, a kidney and bladder stone disorder for which a DNA test exists. Like most giant breeds, Newfoundlands have a short life, typically 9–10 years.

Newfoundland size: common questions

How big does a Newfoundland get?

A fully grown male Newfoundland stands 71 cm (28 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 59–68 kg (130–150 lb). Females stand 66 cm (26 in) and weigh 46–55 kg (100–120 lb).

How tall is a Newfoundland in feet?

A male Newfoundland stands 2 ft 4 in tall at the shoulder (71 cm) and a female 2 ft 2 in (66 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about mid-thigh.

Is a Newfoundland a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 57 kg (125 lb), the Newfoundland counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is taller than 92% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Newfoundland live?

Typical life expectancy is 9–10 years, about average for giant breeds.

Can a Newfoundland fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Newfoundland weighs 46–68 kg (100–150 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
