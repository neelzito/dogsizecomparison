How big is a Papillon?

The Papillon is a toy dog breed from France/Belgium, in the Toy group. Adults stand 21–28 cm (8.1–11 in) at the shoulder and weigh 2.3–4.5 kg (5.1–9.9 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 4% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is the 9th lightest breed. At the shoulder, an average Papillon reaches about mid-shin on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 14–16 years, longer than the 13.9-year average for toy breeds. At 2.3–4.5 kg (5.1–9.9 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb).

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Papillon

The Papillon is a toy spaniel whose name means "butterfly" in French, after its large, fringed, upright ears. Small spaniels like it appear in European paintings from the 16th century onwards, often on the laps of noblewomen, and they were favourites at the French royal court. Early toy spaniels had drooping ears. That type still exists as the Phalène ("moth"), shown as the drop-eared variety of the same breed. Today the Papillon is a lively, clever and friendly companion, and a regular winner at agility and obedience.

What a Papillon's size means at home

A Papillon stands about 20–28 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult, and weighs about 2.5–4.5 kg (5–10 lb). Under its silky coat it is fine-boned and light. It fits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Its small size means it can be hurt by falls from furniture or arms, so teach children to sit down when holding one.

Flying with a Papillon

A Papillon is an ideal size for cabin travel. Even with a carrier, it stays well under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set. Check carrier sizes with the airline cabin check.

Papillon vs Chihuahua and Japanese Chin

The Papillon is taller than a Chihuahua and a little heavier, with a long silky coat and feathered butterfly ears. The Japanese Chin is similar in size but has a flat face and a more cat-like personality.

Temperament and exercise

Papillons are bright, happy and active, more athletic and less of a lapdog than they look. They're among the easiest toy breeds to train, and they love learning tricks and doing agility. They need daily walks and play. Some are bold with bigger dogs, so supervise play to avoid injuries.

Coat and health

The Papillon has a single coat of long, silky hair with no undercoat. It sheds moderately but rarely mats, and it needs brushing only once or twice a week. Known health problems include loose kneecaps, dental disease, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), and fontanelles (soft spots on the skull). Papillons are long-lived, often 14–16 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 77 (Continental Toy Spaniel); The Kennel Club breed standard.

Papillon size: common questions

How big does a Papillon get?

An adult Papillon stands 21–28 cm (8.1–11 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 2.3–4.5 kg (5.1–9.9 lb).

Is a Papillon a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 3.4 kg (7.5 lb), the Papillon counts as a toy dog breed on this site (toy = under 5 kg (11 lb) on average). It is taller than 4% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Papillon live?

Typical life expectancy is 14–16 years, longer than the 13.9-year average for toy breeds.

Can a Papillon fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually yes. At 2.3–4.5 kg (5.1–9.9 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb). Check your airline's rules and carrier size limits before booking.
