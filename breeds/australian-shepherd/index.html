How big is an Australian Shepherd?

The Australian Shepherd is a medium-sized dog breed from the United States, in the Herding group. Males stand 51–59 cm (20.1–23 in) at the shoulder and weigh 23–30 kg (50–65 lb). Females stand 46–54 cm (17.9–21.1 in) and weigh 18–25 kg (40–55 lb). On average it is taller than 56% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 58%. At the shoulder, an average male Australian Shepherd reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Australian Shepherd

Despite its name, the Australian Shepherd was developed in the United States. Its ancestors probably arrived in the 19th century with sheep imported from Australia, possibly together with Basque shepherds, which may explain the name. American ranchers in the West bred these dogs into tough, versatile herders that could work sheep and cattle all day in heat, cold and rough country. The breed became popular after the Second World War through rodeos, horse shows and films. Today the "Aussie" is a favourite with active families and in dog sports.

What an Aussie's size means at home

A male Australian Shepherd stands about 51–58 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's knee, and weighs 22–30 kg (50–65 lb). Females are smaller and lighter, at about 46–53 cm and 18–25 kg. It is a medium-sized, well-muscled dog that is slightly longer than tall. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It is too heavy for an airline cabin.

Aussie vs Miniature American Shepherd

The Miniature American Shepherd (sometimes called the "Mini Aussie") was bred from small Australian Shepherds and is now recognised as a separate breed by the AKC. It looks much the same but stands only about 33–46 cm. Next to the Border Collie, the Aussie is a little taller and stockier, often with a natural bobtail.

Coat and colours

Aussies have a medium-length, weather-resistant double coat that sheds a lot, especially in spring and autumn, so weekly brushing is the minimum. The breed is known for its merle colours (blue merle and red merle), often with white and copper markings and blue or odd-coloured eyes. Two merle dogs should never be bred together: about a quarter of their puppies may be "double merles", which are at high risk of deafness and blindness.

Energy and health

Aussies are bred to work, and they need plenty of daily exercise and mental challenges. Bored Aussies may herd children or other pets, nip, bark or chew. Many Aussies carry a gene variant (MDR1) that makes them dangerously sensitive to some common medicines, including certain wormers. A simple DNA test shows whether your dog carries it, and it's worth telling your vet the result. Hip dysplasia and eye diseases are also known, so ask breeders for health test results for both parents.

Sources: AKC breed standards (Australian Shepherd, Miniature American Shepherd) and breed history; Washington State University Veterinary Clinical Pharmacology Lab (MDR1).

Australian Shepherd size: common questions

How big does an Australian Shepherd get?

A fully grown male Australian Shepherd stands 51–59 cm (20.1–23 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 23–30 kg (50–65 lb). Females stand 46–54 cm (17.9–21.1 in) and weigh 18–25 kg (40–55 lb).

Is an Australian Shepherd a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 24 kg (52 lb), the Australian Shepherd counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 56% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an Australian Shepherd live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can an Australian Shepherd fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Australian Shepherd weighs 18–30 kg (40–65 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
