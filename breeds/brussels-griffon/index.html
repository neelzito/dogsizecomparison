How big is a Brussels Griffon?

The Brussels Griffon is a toy dog breed from Belgium, in the Toy group. Adults stand 18–26 cm (7.1–10 in) at the shoulder and weigh 3.6–4.5 kg (7.9–9.9 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average that makes it the 9th shortest of the 266 breeds on this site. At the shoulder, an average Brussels Griffon reaches about mid-shin on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for toy breeds. At 3.6–4.5 kg (7.9–9.9 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb).

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Brussels Griffon

The Brussels Griffon started out as a stable dog in 19th-century Brussels, kept by coachmen to catch rats. Crosses with the Pug, the King Charles Spaniel and the Affenpinscher gave it its flat face and big personality, and it became a favourite of Belgian royalty, especially Queen Marie Henriette. Its almost human expression, with large eyes and a bearded, monkey-like face, made it a film star in As Good as It Gets (1997). Today it's an affectionate, sensitive and confident companion.

One breed or three?

The FCI recognises three related breeds: the Griffon Bruxellois (rough, red coat), the Griffon Belge (rough coat in black or black and tan) and the Petit Brabançon (smooth coat). The AKC and The Kennel Club treat them as one breed, the Brussels Griffon, with rough and smooth coats and several colours.

What a Brussels Griffon's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 8–10 lb (3.5–4.5 kg). Brussels Griffons stand about 18–25 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult. They're square, sturdy little dogs with a short back. A Griffon suits any home and usually needs an 18″ (46 cm) crate or carrier. Check with the crate size calculator.

Flying with a Brussels Griffon

A Griffon easily fits in the cabin, staying far under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set, even with a carrier. Check with the airline cabin check. Because of its flat face, many airlines won't accept it in the hold anyway.

Griffon vs Pekingese and Japanese Chin

The Pekingese and the Japanese Chin are similar in size and also flat-faced. The Peke is lower and longer, with a long coat, and the Chin is more elegant, with a silky coat.

Temperament and health

Griffons are very attached to their people, often one person in particular. They're sensitive, alert and sometimes stubborn, with a lot of self-importance. They need gentle training and don't like being left alone. Rough coats need regular brushing and hand-stripping, while smooth coats shed more but need less care. Health issues include breathing problems from the flat face, eye injuries, loose kneecaps, and syringomyelia, a condition affecting the spinal cord. Griffons often live 12–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standards No. 80, 81 and 82 (Griffon Bruxellois, Griffon Belge, Petit Brabançon).

Brussels Griffon size: common questions

How big does a Brussels Griffon get?

An adult Brussels Griffon stands 18–26 cm (7.1–10 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 3.6–4.5 kg (7.9–9.9 lb).

Is a Brussels Griffon a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 4.1 kg (8.9 lb), the Brussels Griffon counts as a toy dog breed on this site (toy = under 5 kg (11 lb) on average). It is the 9th shortest of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Brussels Griffon live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for toy breeds.

Can a Brussels Griffon fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually yes. At 3.6–4.5 kg (7.9–9.9 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb). Check your airline's rules and carrier size limits before booking.
