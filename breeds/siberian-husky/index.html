How big is a Siberian Husky?

The Siberian Husky is a medium-sized dog breed from Russia (Siberia), in the Working group. Males stand 54–60 cm (21.1–23.4 in) at the shoulder and weigh 21–27 kg (45–60 lb). Females stand 51–56 cm (20.1–22 in) and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb). On average it is taller than 60% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 54%. At the shoulder, an average male Siberian Husky reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Siberian Husky

The Siberian Husky was bred by the Chukchi people of north-eastern Siberia as a sled dog that could pull light loads over long distances in extreme cold while eating little. Huskies were brought to Alaska in the early 20th century for sled racing. They became famous in 1925, when relay teams of sled dogs carried diphtheria serum to Nome, Alaska. Togo ran the longest leg and Balto the final one. Today the Husky is popular for its wolf-like looks, blue or bi-coloured eyes and friendly nature, but it is still a working sled dog at heart.

What a Husky's size means at home

A Siberian Husky is a medium-sized dog: males stand about 53–60 cm at the shoulder, roughly knee height on an adult, and weigh 20–27 kg (45–60 lb). It is built for speed and endurance, not strength: light, athletic and lean. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Huskies are too heavy for an airline cabin.

Husky vs Malamute

People often mistake the two. The Alaskan Malamute is much bigger and heavier, a freight dog built to haul heavy loads, with brown eyes and a plumed tail carried over the back. The Siberian Husky is the lighter racing dog. The Alaskan Klee Kai is a much smaller dog bred to look like a miniature Husky.

Exercise, escapes and heat

Huskies were bred to run for hours, and they need plenty of daily exercise: long walks, running, canicross or bikejoring. Many Huskies are expert escape artists. They dig, jump and climb, so they need a secure garden with a high fence. Their urge to run and their strong prey drive mean many can't be trusted off the lead. Their thick double coat sheds massively ("blowing the coat") once or twice a year. Huskies cope badly with heat, so exercise them in the cool part of the day in summer, and never shave the coat, because it insulates against heat as well as cold.

Temperament

Huskies are friendly with people, including strangers, and usually get along with other dogs. That makes them poor guard dogs. They are independent thinkers and not especially eager to please, so training takes patience. They rarely bark much, but many howl and "talk".

Siberian Husky size: common questions

How big does a Siberian Husky get?

A fully grown male Siberian Husky stands 54–60 cm (21.1–23.4 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 21–27 kg (45–60 lb). Females stand 51–56 cm (20.1–22 in) and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb).

Is a Siberian Husky a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 22 kg (47 lb), the Siberian Husky counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 60% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Siberian Husky live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Siberian Husky fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Siberian Husky weighs 16–27 kg (35–60 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
