How big is an Otterhound?

The Otterhound is a large-sized dog breed from England, in the Hound group. Males stand 69 cm (27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 52 kg (115 lb). Females stand 61 cm (24 in) and weigh 37 kg (80 lb). On average it is taller than 84% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 90%.

Otterhound compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Otterhound stands 69 cm (27 in) at the shoulder: 39% of the person's height, reaching about mid-thigh. An average female, at 61 cm (24 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 2 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder and females 2 ft. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about waist height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 10–13 years, shorter than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

About the Otterhound

The Otterhound is a large, rough-coated British scent hound, bred to hunt otters along rivers. It has webbed feet, an oily, waterproof double coat and a powerful nose that can follow a scent over water. When otter hunting was banned in the UK in 1978, the breed lost its job, and it's now one of the rarest breeds in the world, with only a few hundred dogs. The Kennel Club lists it as a vulnerable native breed.

What an Otterhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 27 in (68.5 cm) at the shoulder and about 115 lb (52 kg) for males, and about 24 in (61 cm) and 80 lb (36.5 kg) for females. An Otterhound reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's big, shaggy and strong. It needs a large vehicle and, because of its height, the crate size calculator suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

Otterhound vs Bloodhound and Airedale

The Bloodhound is a little shorter and lighter, with a short coat and loose skin. The Airedale Terrier was developed partly from the Otterhound and is much smaller.

Temperament and exercise

Otterhounds are friendly, boisterous and affectionate, good with children and other dogs. They need a lot of exercise and love water. Their nose leads them, so recall is unreliable. They have a loud, deep bay.

Care and health

The rough coat needs weekly brushing and gets very muddy. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat and a bleeding disorder (Glanzmann's thrombasthenia), for which a DNA test exists. Otterhounds often live 10–13 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; The Kennel Club vulnerable native breeds list; Otterhound Club of America.

Otterhound size: common questions

How big does an Otterhound get?

A fully grown male Otterhound stands 69 cm (27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 52 kg (115 lb). Females stand 61 cm (24 in) and weigh 37 kg (80 lb).

How tall is an Otterhound in feet?

A male Otterhound stands 2 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder (69 cm) and a female 2 ft (61 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about mid-thigh.

Is an Otterhound a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 44 kg (98 lb), the Otterhound counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 84% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does an Otterhound live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–13 years, shorter than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

Can an Otterhound fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Otterhound weighs 37–52 kg (80–115 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
