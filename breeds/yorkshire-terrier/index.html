How big is a Yorkshire Terrier?

The Yorkshire Terrier is a toy dog breed from England, in the Toy group. Adults stand 18–21 cm (7.1–8.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 1.8–3.2 kg (4–7.1 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average that makes it the 5th shortest of the 266 breeds on this site. At the shoulder, an average Yorkshire Terrier reaches about ankle height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 11–15 years, shorter than the 14.1-year average for toy breeds. At 1.8–3.2 kg (4–7.1 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb).

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Yorkshire Terrier

The Yorkshire Terrier was developed in Yorkshire and Lancashire, in the north of England, in the 19th century. Workers in the textile mills and coal mines kept small terriers to catch rats. Over time the dogs got smaller and their coats longer and silkier, and the Yorkie went from working ratter to fashionable lapdog in Victorian England. Underneath the silky coat it is still a terrier: brave, curious and sometimes bossy.

What a Yorkie's size means at home

A Yorkshire Terrier stands about 18–20 cm at the shoulder, around ankle height on an adult, and weighs about 2–3 kg. The AKC standard sets an upper limit of 7 lb (3.2 kg). It is one of the smallest breeds and fits easily into a flat, a car seat or a lap. It usually needs an 18″ (46 cm) crate or carrier. See the crate size calculator. Its tiny size also means it can be hurt by a fall from a sofa or arms, or by being stepped on, so supervise it with young children and bigger dogs.

Flying with a Yorkie

A Yorkie is one of the easiest breeds to take on a plane. Even with a carrier, it stays well under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dogs in the cabin, so it can usually travel at your feet. Use the airline cabin check for your airline, and get your Yorkie used to its carrier before the trip.

Coat care

The Yorkie's coat is fine and silky, more like human hair than fur, and it sheds very little. A show coat is grown to the floor, but most pet owners keep it in a short "puppy cut", which is much easier to look after. Either way, it needs regular brushing and trimming, and the hair on top of the head is often tied up so it stays out of the eyes.

Health

Small breeds often have dental problems because their teeth are crowded in a small jaw. Brush your Yorkie's teeth regularly and have them checked at vet visits. Loose kneecaps (luxating patella) and a weak windpipe (tracheal collapse) are also known in the breed, so many owners use a harness instead of a collar. Be wary of "teacup" Yorkies: very small dogs bred below the standard size tend to have more health problems. Yorkies often live 11–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard; The Kennel Club breed standard; Yorkshire Terrier Club of America.

Yorkshire Terrier size: common questions

How big does a Yorkshire Terrier get?

An adult Yorkshire Terrier stands 18–21 cm (7.1–8.1 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 1.8–3.2 kg (4–7.1 lb).

Is a Yorkshire Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 2.5 kg (5.5 lb), the Yorkshire Terrier counts as a toy dog breed on this site (toy = under 5 kg (11 lb) on average). It is the 5th shortest of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Yorkshire Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 11–15 years, shorter than the 14.1-year average for toy breeds.

Can a Yorkshire Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually yes. At 1.8–3.2 kg (4–7.1 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb). Check your airline's rules and carrier size limits before booking.
