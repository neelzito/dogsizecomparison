How big is a Japanese Spitz?

The Japanese Spitz is a small-sized dog breed from Japan, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 31–38 cm (12–15 in) at the shoulder and weigh 4.5–12 kg (9.9–25 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 25% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 23%.

Japanese Spitz compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average Japanese Spitz stands 34 cm (13.5 in) at the shoulder: 20% of the person's height, reaching just below the knee. In feet, adults stand 1 ft to 1 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about knee height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 10–14 years, shorter than the 13.7-year average for small breeds. Lighter individuals fit airline cabin limits of 8 kg (17.6 lb) for dog and carrier; heavier ones need an airline that weighs only the carrier size, or travel in the hold.

About the Japanese Spitz

The Japanese Spitz was developed in Japan in the 1920s and 30s from white German Spitz dogs, probably with some Samoyed and other spitz breeds. It became very popular in Japan after the Second World War. It's always pure white, with a fluffy stand-off coat, pointed ears, black eyes and nose, and a plumed tail curled over its back. It's a cheerful, affectionate companion.

What a Japanese Spitz's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for about 30–38 cm at the shoulder, and Japanese Spitzes weigh about 4.5–11.5 kg (10–25 lb). A Japanese Spitz reaches just below an adult's knee. It suits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Lighter dogs in a light carrier may stay within the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit. Check with the airline cabin check.

Japanese Spitz vs German Spitz, Samoyed and Eskie

The Medium German Spitz is about the same height and a little heavier. The Samoyed is much bigger, and the American Eskimo Dog is similar but comes in three sizes. The Löwchen is about the same height and lighter, with a lion clip.

Temperament and exercise

Japanese Spitzes are lively, friendly and loyal, good with children and usually other pets. They're alert watchdogs but less noisy than many spitz breeds. They need daily walks and play and enjoy learning tricks.

Coat and health

The white coat looks hard to care for, but its texture sheds dirt well. It sheds heavily once or twice a year and needs regular brushing. Known health problems include loose kneecaps and runny eyes, which can stain the fur. Japanese Spitzes often live 10–14 years.

Sources: FCI standard No. 262 (Japanese Spitz); The Kennel Club breed standard; United Kennel Club.

Japanese Spitz size: common questions

How big does a Japanese Spitz get?

An adult Japanese Spitz stands 31–38 cm (12–15 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 4.5–12 kg (9.9–25 lb).

How tall is a Japanese Spitz in feet?

An adult Japanese Spitz stands 1 ft to 1 ft 3 in tall at the shoulder (31–38 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, that reaches just below the knee.

Is a Japanese Spitz a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 8 kg (18 lb), the Japanese Spitz counts as a small dog breed on this site (small = 5–10 kg (11–22 lb) on average). It is taller than 25% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Japanese Spitz live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–14 years, shorter than the 13.7-year average for small breeds.

Can a Japanese Spitz fly in the airplane cabin?

Sometimes. Lighter individuals fit airline cabin limits of 8 kg (17.6 lb) for dog and carrier; heavier ones need an airline that weighs only the carrier size, or travel in the hold. Rules vary by airline, so check before booking.
