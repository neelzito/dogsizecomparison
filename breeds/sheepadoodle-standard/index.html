How big is a Sheepadoodle (Standard)?

The Sheepadoodle (Standard) is a large-sized dog breed from the United States, in the designer & mixed category. Males stand 53–69 cm (20.9–27.2 in) at the shoulder and weigh 27–36 kg (60–79 lb). Females stand 51–64 cm (20.1–25.2 in) and weigh 23–32 kg (51–71 lb). On average it is taller than 69% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 74%. At the shoulder, an average male Sheepadoodle (Standard) reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

About the Sheepadoodle (Standard)

The Sheepadoodle is a cross between an Old English Sheepdog and a Poodle. Crosses like this have existed for decades, but the Sheepadoodle became popular in the 2010s, especially in North America, as part of the wider "doodle" trend. People are drawn to its fluffy black-and-white look, friendly temperament and often low-shedding coat. As a crossbreed, it has no official standard, so size and coat vary.

How big does a Sheepadoodle get?

Size depends mainly on the Poodle parent:

  • Standard Sheepadoodle (Standard Poodle parent): about 53–69 cm at the shoulder and 27–36 kg (60–80 lb) for males
  • Mini Sheepadoodle: about 38–50 cm
  • Toy or Micro Sheepadoodle: smaller still

These are informal terms used by breeders. This page shows the Standard Sheepadoodle, which reaches roughly mid-thigh on an adult. It's a little smaller and lighter than a Bernedoodle on average, and a little taller and heavier than a Goldendoodle. Ask breeders about both parents' size, and use the puppy weight calculator as a rough guide.

What a Sheepadoodle's size means at home

A Standard Sheepadoodle is a big, fluffy dog that needs a large boot and space indoors. The crate size calculator suggests a 48″ (122 cm) crate for an average adult. It needs daily exercise and is too heavy for an airline cabin.

Coat care

Sheepadoodle coats are usually wavy or curly and shed little, but they mat easily, especially behind the ears, under the legs and on the chest. Plan on brushing down to the skin several times a week and a professional groom every 6–8 weeks. Puppies that are born black and white often fade to grey and white as adults. No dog is truly hypoallergenic.

Temperament and health

Sheepadoodles are usually friendly, playful and clever, and good with children, though the herding instinct of the Old English Sheepdog may show up as gentle nudging. They need daily walks and play, and they don't like being left alone for long. They can inherit problems from both parents, including hip dysplasia, eye diseases and the MDR1 drug sensitivity found in herding breeds, so choose a breeder who health-tests both parents. Sheepadoodles often live 12–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standards for the Old English Sheepdog and the Poodle.

Sheepadoodle (Standard) size: common questions

How big does a Sheepadoodle (Standard) get?

A fully grown male Sheepadoodle (Standard) stands 53–69 cm (20.9–27.2 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 27–36 kg (60–79 lb). Females stand 51–64 cm (20.1–25.2 in) and weigh 23–32 kg (51–71 lb).

Is a Sheepadoodle (Standard) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 30 kg (65 lb), the Sheepadoodle (Standard) counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 69% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Sheepadoodle (Standard) live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a Sheepadoodle (Standard) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Sheepadoodle (Standard) weighs 23–36 kg (51–79 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
