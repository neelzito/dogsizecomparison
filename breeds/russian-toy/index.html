How big is a Russian Toy?

The Russian Toy is a toy dog breed from Russia, in the Toy group. Adults stand 21–28 cm (8.1–11 in) at the shoulder and weigh 1.4–2.9 kg (3.1–6.4 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 4% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is the 2nd lightest breed.

Russian Toy compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average Russian Toy stands 24 cm (9.5 in) at the shoulder: 14% of the person's height, reaching about mid-shin. In feet, adults stand 8 in to 11 in tall at the shoulder. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to just below the knee. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, shorter than the 14.1-year average for toy breeds. At 1.4–2.9 kg (3.1–6.4 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb).

About the Russian Toy

The Russian Toy is a tiny Russian companion dog, developed in the early 20th century from English Toy Terriers brought to Russia. It became popular with the Russian aristocracy. After the Russian Revolution and the Second World War, it was rebuilt from a small number of dogs. Breeders later developed a long-haired variety alongside the smooth one. It's an elegant, lively and affectionate little dog.

What a Russian Toy's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 20–28 cm at the shoulder and a weight of up to about 3 kg. Most weigh about 1.5–3 kg (3–6.5 lb). A Russian Toy reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's fine-boned and long-legged for its size. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It's ideal for cabin travel. Check with the airline cabin check.

Russian Toy vs Toy Poodle and Chihuahua

A Toy Poodle is about the same size, with a curly coat. A Chihuahua is shorter and similarly light. The Russian Toy has very long, fringed ears in the long-haired variety.

Temperament and care

Russian Toys are cheerful, alert and very attached to their people. They need gentle handling because their legs break easily, and they feel the cold. They need short daily walks and play. Known health problems include fractures, loose kneecaps and dental problems. Russian Toys often live 12–14 years.

Sources: FCI standard No. 352 (Russian Toy); AKC breed information; Russian Kennel Club (RKF).

Russian Toy size: common questions

How big does a Russian Toy get?

An adult Russian Toy stands 21–28 cm (8.1–11 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 1.4–2.9 kg (3.1–6.4 lb).

How tall is a Russian Toy in feet?

An adult Russian Toy stands 8 in to 11 in tall at the shoulder (21–28 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, that reaches about mid-shin.

Is a Russian Toy a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 2.2 kg (4.7 lb), the Russian Toy counts as a toy dog breed on this site (toy = under 5 kg (11 lb) on average). It is taller than 4% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does a Russian Toy live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, shorter than the 14.1-year average for toy breeds.

Can a Russian Toy fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually yes. At 1.4–2.9 kg (3.1–6.4 lb), it fits even the strictest airline cabin rule, where dog and carrier together may weigh 8 kg (17.6 lb). Check your airline's rules and carrier size limits before booking.
