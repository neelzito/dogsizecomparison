How big is an English Springer Spaniel?

The English Springer Spaniel is a medium-sized dog breed from England, in the Sporting group. Males stand 51 cm (20.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 23 kg (50 lb). Females stand 49 cm (19.1 in) and weigh 18 kg (40 lb). On average it is taller than 53% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 51%. At the shoulder, an average male English Springer Spaniel reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the English Springer Spaniel

The English Springer Spaniel is a British gundog bred to find game birds and "spring" them into the air for the hunter, then retrieve them after the shot. In the 19th century, spaniels from the same litter were sorted by size: the smaller ones became Cockers and the larger ones Springers. The Springer was recognised as a separate breed in 1902. Today it is a much-loved family dog, a top working gundog, and a common sniffer dog with police, customs and the military, thanks to its tireless nose.

What a Springer's size means at home

An English Springer stands about 51 cm at the shoulder (males) and 48 cm (females), roughly an adult's knee. The AKC standard gives one ideal height per sex. Males weigh around 22–23 kg (50 lb), females about 18 kg (40 lb). It is a medium-sized, athletic dog that needs space and lots of exercise. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Springers are too heavy for an airline cabin.

Working Springers vs show Springers

As with Cockers, Springers have split into two types. Show Springers are heavier, with more coat, longer ears and a more stately look. Working Springers are lighter, faster and usually smaller, with less feathering and huge energy and drive. A working-bred Springer can be a handful in a quiet home, so choose the type that suits your life.

Springer vs Cocker and Toller

The Springer is bigger and leggier than the English Cocker Spaniel, but the two have a similar happy, busy nature. The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever is a little smaller and lighter, a different type of gundog with a red coat.

Exercise and training

Springers need at least an hour or two of exercise a day, plus mental work: retrieving, scent games, training or dog sports. They love water and mud. A bored Springer can become noisy or destructive. They're eager to please and usually easy to train with rewards.

Coat and health

The medium-length, feathered coat needs brushing a few times a week, and the ears need regular checks to prevent infections. Health issues include hip and elbow dysplasia, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), and phosphofructokinase (PFK) deficiency, a blood disorder for which a DNA test exists. Springers often live 12–14 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; The Kennel Club breed standard; English Springer Spaniel Field Trial Association.

English Springer Spaniel size: common questions

How big does an English Springer Spaniel get?

A fully grown male English Springer Spaniel stands 51 cm (20.1 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 23 kg (50 lb). Females stand 49 cm (19.1 in) and weigh 18 kg (40 lb).

Is an English Springer Spaniel a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 20 kg (45 lb), the English Springer Spaniel counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 53% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does an English Springer Spaniel live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can an English Springer Spaniel fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A English Springer Spaniel weighs 18–23 kg (40–50 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
