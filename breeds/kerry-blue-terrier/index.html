How big is a Kerry Blue Terrier?

The Kerry Blue Terrier is a medium-sized dog breed from Ireland, in the Terrier group. Males stand 46–50 cm (17.9–19.5 in) at the shoulder and weigh 15–18 kg (33–40 lb). Females stand 45–49 cm (17.5–19.1 in) and weigh 14–16 kg (31–35 lb). On average it is taller than 45% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 41%. At the shoulder, an average male Kerry Blue Terrier reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Kerry Blue Terrier

The Kerry Blue Terrier comes from County Kerry in south-west Ireland, where it was used as an all-round farm dog: hunting rats, badgers and otters, herding livestock and guarding the home. It became a symbol of Irish nationalism in the early 20th century, and Michael Collins owned a famous Kerry Blue. The breed's most striking feature is its soft, wavy blue-grey coat, which is born black and gradually lightens.

What a Kerry Blue's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–19.5 in (45.5–49.5 cm) at the shoulder for males, with females slightly smaller. Males weigh about 15–18 kg (33–40 lb), and females a little less. A Kerry Blue reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's square, athletic and well-muscled. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Kerry Blue vs Soft Coated Wheaten

The Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier, another Irish terrier, is about the same size. The two have a similar soft, wavy coat, but the Wheaten is wheat-coloured and usually more easy-going with other dogs. The American Pit Bull Terrier is a little taller and clearly heavier.

Born black, turning blue

Kerry Blue puppies are born black. Their coat gradually clears to blue-grey, a process that can take up to 18 months or longer. Adult shades range from deep slate to light silver-blue.

Temperament and exercise

Kerry Blues are clever, lively and loyal, affectionate with their family and good watchdogs. Like many terriers, they can be strong-willed and may not get on with other dogs, especially of the same sex, so early socialisation is important. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation and enjoy agility, herding and earthdog trials.

Coat and health

The soft, single coat sheds very little but grows continuously. It needs brushing a couple of times a week and a trim every 4–8 weeks. Known health problems include eye problems, skin cysts, and cerebellar abiotrophy (a nerve disease) and factor XI deficiency (a bleeding disorder), which have DNA tests. Kerry Blues often live 12–15 years.

Kerry Blue Terrier size: common questions

How big does a Kerry Blue Terrier get?

A fully grown male Kerry Blue Terrier stands 46–50 cm (17.9–19.5 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 15–18 kg (33–40 lb). Females stand 45–49 cm (17.5–19.1 in) and weigh 14–16 kg (31–35 lb).

Is a Kerry Blue Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 16 kg (35 lb), the Kerry Blue Terrier counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 45% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Kerry Blue Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Kerry Blue Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Kerry Blue Terrier weighs 14–18 kg (31–40 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
