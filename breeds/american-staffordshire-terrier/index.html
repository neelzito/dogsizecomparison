How big is an American Staffordshire Terrier?

The American Staffordshire Terrier is a large-sized dog breed from the United States, in the Terrier group. Males stand 46–49 cm (17.9–19.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 25–32 kg (55–71 lb). Females stand 43–46 cm (16.9–17.9 in) and weigh 18–25 kg (40–55 lb). On average it is taller than 44% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 64%.

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male American Staffordshire Terrier stands 47 cm (18.5 in) at the shoulder: 27% of the person's height, reaching about knee height. An average female, at 44 cm (17.4 in), reaches just below the knee. In feet, males stand 1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 7 in tall at the shoulder and females 1 ft 5 in to 1 ft 6 in. About the American Staffordshire Terrier

The American Staffordshire Terrier ("AmStaff") shares its roots with the American Pit Bull Terrier: bulldog and terrier crosses brought to the US from Britain in the 19th century. In 1936 the AKC recognised these dogs as the Staffordshire Terrier, a show and companion line. In 1972 the name was changed to American Staffordshire Terrier to distinguish it from the smaller English Staffordshire Bull Terrier. A famous early example is Pete the Pup from the Our Gang films. Today a well-bred AmStaff is a confident, affectionate and people-loving dog.

What an AmStaff's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–19 in (45.5–48.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 17–18 in (43–45.5 cm) for females. AmStaffs weigh about 25–32 kg (55–70 lb) for males and 18–25 kg (40–55 lb) for females. Very muscular for its height, it reaches roughly an adult's knee but is heavy enough to put it in the large size class on this site. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

AmStaff vs Pit Bull and Staffie

The AmStaff is a little shorter than the American Pit Bull Terrier but stockier and heavier. The Staffordshire Bull Terrier from England is clearly smaller and lighter.

Laws and restrictions

In some countries, regions and cities, AmStaffs are covered by breed-specific laws, which may require a licence, insurance, a muzzle in public or neutering, or ban them outright. Rules differ widely and change over time. Check the current laws where you live, and where you plan to travel or move, before you get one. Some airlines and landlords also have rules for "strong" breeds.

Temperament and training

AmStaffs are usually very affectionate and loyal with their family, often good with children, and keen to please. Some are less tolerant of other dogs, so early socialisation and careful introductions matter. They're strong, energetic and athletic, and need daily exercise and consistent, reward-based training.

Care and health

The short, stiff coat needs very little grooming. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, skin allergies, hypothyroidism, and cerebellar ataxia, an inherited nerve disease for which a DNA test exists. AmStaffs often live 12–16 years.

American Staffordshire Terrier size: common questions

How big does an American Staffordshire Terrier get?

A fully grown male American Staffordshire Terrier stands 46–49 cm (17.9–19.1 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 25–32 kg (55–71 lb). Females stand 43–46 cm (16.9–17.9 in) and weigh 18–25 kg (40–55 lb).

How tall is an American Staffordshire Terrier in feet?

A male American Staffordshire Terrier stands 1 ft 6 in to 1 ft 7 in tall at the shoulder (46–49 cm) and a female 1 ft 5 in to 1 ft 6 in (43–46 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about knee height.

Is an American Staffordshire Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 25 kg (55 lb), the American Staffordshire Terrier counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 44% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does an American Staffordshire Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–16 years, longer than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

Can an American Staffordshire Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A American Staffordshire Terrier weighs 18–32 kg (40–71 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
