How big is a Keeshond?

The Keeshond is a medium-sized dog breed from the Netherlands, in the Non-Sporting group. Males stand 46 cm (17.9 in) at the shoulder and weigh 16–21 kg (35–45 lb). Females stand 43 cm (16.9 in) and weigh 16–21 kg (35–45 lb). On average it is taller than 41% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 45%. At the shoulder, an average male Keeshond reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Keeshond

The Keeshond is a Dutch spitz, a companion and watchdog on the barges and farms of the Netherlands in the 17th and 18th centuries. It takes its name from Cornelis (Kees) de Gyselaer, a leader of the Patriot party in the Dutch political struggles of the 1780s, whose dog became the party's symbol. When the Patriots lost, the breed fell out of favour, but it survived on barges and farms. Breeders in England revived it in the 20th century. It's known for its fox-like face, plush grey coat and the pale "spectacles" around its eyes.

What a Keeshond's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 18 in (45.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 17 in (43 cm) for females. Keeshonden weigh about 16–20.5 kg (35–45 lb). A Keeshond reaches roughly an adult's knee, and its thick coat makes it look much bigger than it is. It suits most homes and usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Keeshond or Wolfspitz?

The FCI treats the Keeshond as the largest variety of the German Spitz, called the Wolfspitz, which includes several sizes from the tiny Pomeranian upwards. The AKC and The Kennel Club treat the Keeshond as a separate breed. Either way, it is a close cousin of the German Spitz and the Pomeranian.

Temperament

Keeshonden are friendly, affectionate and lively, very attached to their families and usually good with children and other pets. They're alert watchdogs that bark at visitors, but they aren't aggressive. They need daily walks and play, and they enjoy learning, especially with rewards. They don't like being left alone for long.

Coat care

The Keeshond's thick double coat has a dense undercoat and a long, harsh outer coat. It sheds moderately all year and heavily once or twice a year. It needs a thorough brush, down to the skin, at least once or twice a week, and daily during moults. Don't shave it, as the coat insulates against heat as well as cold.

Health

Keeshonden are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, loose kneecaps, hypothyroidism, and primary hyperparathyroidism, a calcium disorder for which a DNA test exists. They often live 12–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 97 (German Spitz, Wolfspitz/Keeshond); Keeshond Club of America.

Keeshond size: common questions

How big does a Keeshond get?

A fully grown male Keeshond stands 46 cm (17.9 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 16–21 kg (35–45 lb). Females stand 43 cm (16.9 in) and weigh 16–21 kg (35–45 lb).

Is a Keeshond a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 18 kg (40 lb), the Keeshond counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 41% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Keeshond live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Keeshond fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Keeshond weighs 16–21 kg (35–45 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
