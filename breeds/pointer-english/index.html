How big is a Pointer (English)?

The Pointer (English) is a large-sized dog breed from England, in the Sporting group. Males stand 64–71 cm (25–28 in) at the shoulder and weigh 25–34 kg (55–75 lb). Females stand 59–66 cm (23–26 in) and weigh 21–30 kg (45–65 lb). On average it is taller than 84% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 71%. At the shoulder, an average male Pointer (English) reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

About the Pointer (English)

The Pointer, often called the English Pointer, was developed in Britain in the 17th and 18th centuries from Spanish pointing dogs, crossed with foxhounds, greyhounds and setters to add speed and stamina. Its job is to find game birds and then freeze in a classic "point": nose forward, body rigid, often with one front paw raised, showing the hunter where the birds are. The Pointer is one of the founding breeds of many modern gundogs and is still prized by field-trial and hunting enthusiasts.

What a Pointer's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 25–28 in (63.5–71 cm) at the shoulder and 55–75 lb (25–34 kg) for males, and 23–26 in (58.5–66 cm) and 45–65 lb (20.5–29.5 kg) for females. A Pointer reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's tall, lean and athletic, with long legs. Because of its height, the crate size calculator suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

Pointer vs German Shorthaired Pointer

The Pointer is a little taller and similar in weight to the German Shorthaired Pointer. It's a more specialised bird dog, built for speed and range, while the GSP is an all-round hunter that also retrieves and tracks. Pointers are usually white with liver, black, orange or lemon markings. The Large Munsterlander is another versatile gundog with a long coat.

Exercise: a lot of it

Pointers are bred to run and hunt for hours. They need at least one to two hours of hard exercise every day, ideally including free running in a large, safe area. Without it, they can become restless and destructive. Their urge to range and hunt birds is strong, so recall needs a lot of training. At home, a well-exercised Pointer is calm, gentle and affectionate.

Coat and health

The short, smooth coat needs very little grooming, but it offers little protection from cold, so a coat helps in winter. Pointers are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, eye conditions, deafness and skin allergies. They often live 12–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 1 (English Pointer); American Pointer Club.

Pointer (English) size: common questions

How big does a Pointer (English) get?

A fully grown male Pointer (English) stands 64–71 cm (25–28 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 25–34 kg (55–75 lb). Females stand 59–66 cm (23–26 in) and weigh 21–30 kg (45–65 lb).

Is a Pointer (English) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 27 kg (60 lb), the Pointer (English) counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 84% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Pointer (English) live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, longer than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

Can a Pointer (English) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Pointer (English) weighs 21–34 kg (45–75 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
