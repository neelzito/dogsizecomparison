How big is an Irish Red and White Setter?

The Irish Red and White Setter is a medium-sized dog breed from Ireland, in the Sporting group. Males stand 62–66 cm (24.4–26 in) at the shoulder and weigh 19–27 kg (42–60 lb). Females stand 57–61 cm (22.4–24 in) and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb). On average it is taller than 76% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 54%.

Irish Red and White Setter compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Irish Red and White Setter stands 64 cm (25.2 in) at the shoulder: 37% of the person's height, reaching about mid-thigh. An average female, at 59 cm (23.2 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 2 ft to 2 ft 2 in tall at the shoulder and females 1 ft 10 in to 2 ft. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about hip height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 11–15 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Irish Red and White Setter

The Irish Red and White Setter is the older of the two Irish setters. In the 18th century, Irish setters were usually red and white. In the 19th century, the solid red Irish Setter became fashionable, and the Red and White nearly vanished. In the 1920s, a few breeders revived it, and The Kennel Club recognised it again in the 1980s. It's a working gundog with a pearly white coat and red patches.

What a Red and White Setter's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 24.5–26 in (62–66 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22.5–24 in (57–61 cm) for females. The figures on this page show about 19–27 kg (42–60 lb) for males and 16–22.5 kg for females. A Red and White Setter reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean and athletic, so its weight puts it in the medium size class on this site. It usually needs a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Red and White vs Irish Setter

The Irish Setter is taller and clearly heavier, with a solid mahogany coat. The Wirehaired Pointing Griffon is shorter and heavier, with a wiry coat.

Temperament and exercise

Red and White Setters are friendly, affectionate and energetic, generally a little calmer and more biddable than the Irish Setter. They need at least an hour or two of exercise a day, ideally including free running. Recall needs work because of their bird instinct.

Coat and health

The silky, feathered coat needs brushing a couple of times a week. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, canine leukocyte adhesion deficiency (CLAD) and von Willebrand's disease, both of which have DNA tests. Red and White Setters often live 11–15 years.

Irish Red and White Setter size: common questions

How big does an Irish Red and White Setter get?

A fully grown male Irish Red and White Setter stands 62–66 cm (24.4–26 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 19–27 kg (42–60 lb). Females stand 57–61 cm (22.4–24 in) and weigh 16–23 kg (35–50 lb).

How tall is an Irish Red and White Setter in feet?

A male Irish Red and White Setter stands 2 ft to 2 ft 2 in tall at the shoulder (62–66 cm) and a female 1 ft 10 in to 2 ft (57–61 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about mid-thigh.

Is an Irish Red and White Setter a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 21 kg (47 lb), the Irish Red and White Setter counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 76% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does an Irish Red and White Setter live?

Typical life expectancy is 11–15 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can an Irish Red and White Setter fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Irish Red and White Setter weighs 16–27 kg (35–60 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
