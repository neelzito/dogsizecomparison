How big is a Cane Corso?

The Cane Corso is a giant-sized dog breed from Italy, in the Working group. Males stand 64–70 cm (25–27.6 in) at the shoulder and weigh 45–50 kg (99–110 lb). Females stand 60–66 cm (23.4–26 in) and weigh 40–45 kg (88–99 lb). On average it is taller than 84% of the 267 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 90%. At the shoulder, an average male Cane Corso reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 9–12 years, about average for giant breeds.

About the Cane Corso

The Cane Corso is an Italian mastiff, a descendant of the Roman war and guard dogs. For centuries, Italian farmers used it to guard property and livestock, hunt wild boar and drive cattle, especially in southern Italy. As farming changed in the 20th century, the breed nearly died out. Italian enthusiasts revived it from the few remaining dogs in the 1970s and 80s, and it was recognised by the FCI in 1996 and the AKC in 2010. The name roughly means "guardian dog". Today the Cane Corso is a popular, powerful and loyal guardian that needs an experienced owner.

What a Cane Corso's size means at home

A male Cane Corso stands about 64–70 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and females about 60–66 cm. The AKC standard only says weight should be in proportion to height. Typical weights are around 45–50 kg (99–110 lb) for males and 40–45 kg (88–99 lb) for females, and many dogs are heavier. That's heavy enough to put it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle and a 54″ (137 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Its strength on the lead is considerable, so training from puppyhood is essential.

Cane Corso vs Rottweiler, Mastiff and Dogo Argentino

The Cane Corso is a little taller than the Rottweiler but usually a little lighter, and it's more athletic and has a broader, square muzzle. The English Mastiff is much bigger and heavier. The white Dogo Argentino is slightly smaller and lighter.

Temperament and training

A well-bred Cane Corso is calm and affectionate with its family, gentle with its own children, and naturally suspicious of strangers. It was bred to guard, so it needs extensive socialisation from a young age and consistent, confident training. It isn't a good choice for first-time dog owners. Some countries and regions have special rules for large guarding breeds, such as licences, insurance or muzzle requirements. Check local laws first.

Ears, growth and health

Cane Corsos are often pictured with cropped ears. Cropping is banned in the UK and much of Europe, so there they keep their natural drop ears. Puppies grow very fast, so feed a large-breed puppy food, keep them lean and protect their joints from hard exercise. Hip and elbow dysplasia, eyelid problems (entropion, ectropion) and bloat occur in the breed. Life expectancy is around 9–12 years.

Cane Corso size: common questions

How big does a Cane Corso get?

A fully grown male Cane Corso stands 64–70 cm (25–27.6 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 45–50 kg (99–110 lb). Females stand 60–66 cm (23.4–26 in) and weigh 40–45 kg (88–99 lb).

Is a Cane Corso a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 45 kg (99 lb), the Cane Corso counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is taller than 84% of the 267 breeds listed.

How long does a Cane Corso live?

Typical life expectancy is 9–12 years, about average for giant breeds.

Can a Cane Corso fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Cane Corso weighs 40–50 kg (88–110 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
