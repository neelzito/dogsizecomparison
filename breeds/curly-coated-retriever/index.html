How big is a Curly-Coated Retriever?

The Curly-Coated Retriever is a large-sized dog breed from England, in the Sporting group. Males stand 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder and weigh 27–43 kg (60–95 lb). Females stand 59–64 cm (23–25 in) and weigh 27–43 kg (60–95 lb). On average it is taller than 80% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 83%. At the shoulder, an average male Curly-Coated Retriever reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

About the Curly-Coated Retriever

The Curly-Coated Retriever is one of the oldest retriever breeds, developed in England by the late 18th century, probably from the St. John's water dog, the old English Water Spaniel and possibly the Poodle. Gamekeepers and poachers alike prized it for retrieving birds from water and thick cover. It's covered in tight, crisp curls that shed water, and it's the tallest of the retrievers. It's less common than the Labrador and the Golden, but a loyal, capable working dog.

What a Curly's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 25–27 in (63.5–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 23–25 in (58.5–63.5 cm) for females. Curlies weigh about 27–43 kg (60–95 lb). A Curly reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's tall, athletic and elegant. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Curly vs Labrador and Weimaraner

The Curly is taller than a Labrador, with a longer leg and a more elegant outline. The Weimaraner is about the same height and weight. Curlies come in black or liver.

Temperament and exercise

Curlies are confident, intelligent and independent, more reserved with strangers than most retrievers and slow to mature. They need a lot of daily exercise, especially swimming and retrieving, and plenty of mental work. They're affectionate with their family but can be strong-willed, so consistent training matters.

Coat and health

The tight curls need little grooming: they shouldn't be brushed, which turns them into frizz. Occasional wetting and trimming keep the coat neat. The coat sheds seasonally. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, eye problems, and exercise-induced collapse and glycogen storage disease, both of which have DNA tests. Curlies often live 10–12 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 110 (Curly Coated Retriever); Curly-Coated Retriever Club of America.

Curly-Coated Retriever size: common questions

How big does a Curly-Coated Retriever get?

A fully grown male Curly-Coated Retriever stands 64–69 cm (25–27 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 27–43 kg (60–95 lb). Females stand 59–64 cm (23–25 in) and weigh 27–43 kg (60–95 lb).

Is a Curly-Coated Retriever a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 35 kg (77 lb), the Curly-Coated Retriever counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 80% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Curly-Coated Retriever live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a Curly-Coated Retriever fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Curly-Coated Retriever weighs 27–43 kg (60–95 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
