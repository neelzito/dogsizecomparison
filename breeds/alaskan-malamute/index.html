How big is an Alaskan Malamute?

The Alaskan Malamute is a large-sized dog breed from the United States (Alaska), in the Working group. Males stand 64 cm (25 in) at the shoulder and weigh 39 kg (85 lb). Females stand 59 cm (23 in) and weigh 34 kg (75 lb). On average it is taller than 72% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 85%. At the shoulder, an average male Alaskan Malamute reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–14 years, about average for large breeds.

About the Alaskan Malamute

The Alaskan Malamute is one of the oldest Arctic sled dogs. It is named after the Mahlemut, an Inuit people of north-western Alaska, who bred these strong, enduring dogs to haul heavy sleds, hunt seals and help with bears. During the Gold Rush of the late 1890s, Malamutes were much in demand, and later they went on polar expeditions and served in the Second World War. Today the Malamute is a friendly, dignified and powerful companion that still loves to pull.

What a Malamute's size means at home

The AKC standard gives a "desirable freighting size": about 64 cm (25 in) and 38.5 kg (85 lb) for males, and 58.5 cm (23 in) and 34 kg (75 lb) for females. Many Malamutes are larger, especially pet and show dogs. A male reaches roughly mid-thigh on an adult. It needs a big boot, a 48″ (122 cm) crate (check with the crate size calculator) and a strong person on the lead. A Malamute that decides to pull can easily take an adult with it.

Malamute vs Siberian Husky

The two are often confused. The Malamute is the freight dog: taller and much heavier than the Siberian Husky, with a broader head, brown eyes and a plumed tail carried over the back. The Husky is the lighter, faster racing dog and often has blue eyes. "Giant Malamutes" of 60 kg or more are sometimes advertised, but they aren't part of the breed standard, and size alone doesn't make a better dog.

Exercise and temperament

Malamutes need plenty of exercise and love pulling sports: sledding, canicross, bikejoring or carting. They're affectionate and friendly with people, so they make poor guard dogs, but they can be pushy with other dogs, especially of the same sex. Their prey drive is strong. They dig, howl (they rarely bark), and can be escape artists, so they need a secure garden.

Coat, heat and health

The Malamute's thick double coat sheds heavily, especially when it "blows" its undercoat once or twice a year. It copes poorly with heat, so exercise in the cool part of the day in summer, and never shave the coat. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, eye conditions, hypothyroidism and polyneuropathy, a nerve disease for which a DNA test exists. Malamutes often live 10–14 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 243 (Alaskan Malamute); Alaskan Malamute Club of America.

Alaskan Malamute size: common questions

How big does an Alaskan Malamute get?

A fully grown male Alaskan Malamute stands 64 cm (25 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 39 kg (85 lb). Females stand 59 cm (23 in) and weigh 34 kg (75 lb).

Is an Alaskan Malamute a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 36 kg (80 lb), the Alaskan Malamute counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 72% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does an Alaskan Malamute live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–14 years, about average for large breeds.

Can an Alaskan Malamute fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Alaskan Malamute weighs 34–39 kg (75–85 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
