How big is an Australian Terrier?

The Australian Terrier is a small-sized dog breed from Australia, in the Terrier group. Adults stand 26–28 cm (10–11 in) at the shoulder and weigh 6.8–9.1 kg (15–20 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 13% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 22%. At the shoulder, an average Australian Terrier reaches about mid-shin on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 11–15 years, shorter than the 13.7-year average for small breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Australian Terrier

The Australian Terrier is one of the smallest working terriers and one of the first breeds developed in Australia. In the 19th century, settlers crossed British terriers, including the Cairn, Skye, Yorkshire and Dandie Dinmont, to create a tough little dog that could kill rats and snakes, guard the homestead and cope with harsh conditions. It's a confident, spirited companion with a ruff around its neck and a bold, upright stance.

What an Aussie Terrier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 10–11 in (25.5–28 cm) at the shoulder, and Australian Terriers weigh about 7–9 kg (15–20 lb). One reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's sturdy and a little longer than tall. It suits flats and small homes and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Most adults are over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit for dog and carrier on many airlines.

Australian Terrier vs Westie and Silky

The West Highland White Terrier is about the same size. The Australian Terrier comes in blue and tan, or sandy or red, and has a softer topknot. The Silky Terrier, its close relative, is smaller and lighter, with a long, silky coat.

Temperament and exercise

Australian Terriers are alert, brave and affectionate, devoted to their family and good watchdogs. Like most terriers, they love to dig and chase, so a secure garden is essential, and they may not get on with small pets. They need daily walks and play. They're clever and fairly easy to train for a terrier, but they can be stubborn.

Coat and health

The harsh, weatherproof coat sheds little and needs weekly brushing and occasional hand-stripping. Known health problems include diabetes, loose kneecaps, allergies and Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (a hip condition). Australian Terriers often live 11–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 8 (Australian Terrier); Australian Terrier Club of America.

Australian Terrier size: common questions

How big does an Australian Terrier get?

An adult Australian Terrier stands 26–28 cm (10–11 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 6.8–9.1 kg (15–20 lb).

Is an Australian Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 8 kg (18 lb), the Australian Terrier counts as a small dog breed on this site (small = 5–10 kg (11–22 lb) on average). It is taller than 13% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an Australian Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 11–15 years, shorter than the 13.7-year average for small breeds.

Can an Australian Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Australian Terrier weighs 6.8–9.1 kg (15–20 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
