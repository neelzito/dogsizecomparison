How big is a Chow Chow?

The Chow Chow is a large-sized dog breed from China, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) at the shoulder and weigh 21–32 kg (45–71 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 46% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 66%. At the shoulder, an average Chow Chow reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 8–12 years, shorter than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Chow Chow

The Chow Chow is one of the oldest dog breeds, from northern China, where it was used for centuries as a guard dog, hunting dog and cart puller. Genetic studies place it among the breeds closest to the ancient ancestors of modern dogs. Chows arrived in Britain in the late 18th century, and Queen Victoria helped make them fashionable. The breed's lion-like mane, scowling expression, stilted gait and blue-black tongue make it instantly recognisable.

What a Chow's size means at home

A Chow Chow stands about 43–51 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's knee, and weighs about 20–32 kg (45–70 lb). Its heavy coat makes it look much bigger than it is. It is square and solid, with a broad head and straight, stiff hind legs. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Chows are too heavy for an airline cabin.

Chow Chow vs Shar-Pei

The Chow and the Chinese Shar-Pei are both ancient Chinese breeds, and both have a blue-black tongue. They're similar in height, but the Shar-Pei is a little lighter, with its famous wrinkles and a short, harsh coat instead of the Chow's mane.

Rough or smooth coat

Chows come in two coats: the familiar rough coat, long and dense with a mane, and the shorter smooth coat. Both have a thick double coat that sheds heavily, especially seasonally, and needs brushing several times a week. Chows struggle in the heat, so they need shade, cool floors and rest on warm days.

Temperament

Chows are dignified, independent and often aloof, more cat-like than most dogs. They're very loyal to their family but reserved with strangers and can be protective. They don't usually enjoy rough play or a lot of handling. Early socialisation and calm, consistent training are essential. A Chow isn't a dog that lives to please, and it suits owners who respect its independence. Chows need only moderate exercise and are usually clean and easy to house-train.

Health

Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, eyelid problems (entropion), and hypothyroidism. Because of the deep-set eyes and heavy face, eye checks are important. Chows often live 8–12 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 205 (Chow Chow); The Kennel Club breed standard.

Chow Chow size: common questions

How big does a Chow Chow get?

An adult Chow Chow stands 43–51 cm (16.9–20.1 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 21–32 kg (45–71 lb).

Is a Chow Chow a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 26 kg (58 lb), the Chow Chow counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 46% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Chow Chow live?

Typical life expectancy is 8–12 years, shorter than the 12.1-year average for large breeds.

Can a Chow Chow fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Chow Chow weighs 21–32 kg (45–71 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
