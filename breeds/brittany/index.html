How big is a Brittany?

The Brittany is a medium-sized dog breed from France, in the Sporting group. Adults stand 45–52 cm (17.5–20.5 in) at the shoulder and weigh 14–18 kg (30–40 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 50% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 41%. At the shoulder, an average Brittany reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Brittany

The Brittany comes from the region of Brittany in north-western France, where it has been used for centuries to find and point game birds and woodcock. It was long known as the Brittany Spaniel, but because it points game rather than flushing it like a spaniel, the AKC dropped "Spaniel" from its name in 1982. In Europe it is called the Épagneul Breton. Brittanies are prized by hunters for their nose and versatility, and the breed has produced more AKC dual champions (in both show and field) than any other.

What a Brittany's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 17.5–20.5 in (44.5–52 cm) at the shoulder. Brittanies weigh about 13.5–18 kg (30–40 lb). A Brittany reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's a compact, leggy and athletic gundog that fits well in most homes and cars, and the crate size calculator suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate for an average adult. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Brittany vs Toller

The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever is about the same height but heavier, with a fuller red coat. Both are compact, energetic gundogs that suit active families.

Exercise: a lot of it

Brittanies are bred to run and hunt all day. They need at least an hour or two of hard exercise daily, such as running, retrieving, hunting or dog sports, plus mental challenges. Without it, they can become restless and destructive. They're sensitive and eager to please, so gentle, reward-based training works best. Their hunting instinct is strong, so recall training takes time.

Coat and colours

The Brittany's coat is dense, flat or wavy, with light feathering. It needs only weekly brushing. In the US, the standard colours are orange and white or liver and white. European Brittanies may also be black and white or tricolour. Some are born with a naturally short tail, or none at all.

Health

Brittanies are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, epilepsy and some eye conditions. They often live 12–14 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 95 (Épagneul Breton); American Brittany Club.

Brittany size: common questions

How big does a Brittany get?

An adult Brittany stands 45–52 cm (17.5–20.5 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 14–18 kg (30–40 lb).

Is a Brittany a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 16 kg (35 lb), the Brittany counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 50% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Brittany live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–14 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Brittany fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Brittany weighs 14–18 kg (30–40 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
