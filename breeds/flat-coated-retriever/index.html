How big is a Flat-Coated Retriever?

The Flat-Coated Retriever is a large-sized dog breed from England, in the Sporting group. Males stand 59–62 cm (23–24.4 in) at the shoulder and weigh 27–32 kg (60–71 lb). Females stand 56–60 cm (22–23.4 in) and weigh 27–32 kg (60–71 lb). On average it is taller than 68% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 74%. At the shoulder, an average male Flat-Coated Retriever reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 8–10 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

About the Flat-Coated Retriever

The Flat-Coated Retriever was developed in England in the mid-19th century from crosses of the St. John's water dog (the ancestor of the Labrador) with setters and other retrievers. It quickly became the gamekeeper's favourite retriever and was one of the most popular gundogs in Britain until the Labrador and Golden Retriever overtook it after the First World War. Known as the "Peter Pan" of retrievers, the Flat-Coat keeps its puppyish, playful nature well into old age.

What a Flat-Coat's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 23–24.5 in (58.5–62 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22–23.5 in (56–59.5 cm) for females. Flat-Coats weigh about 27–32 kg (60–70 lb). A Flat-Coat reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean, elegant and athletic. It needs a big boot and usually a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Its wagging tail is at coffee-table height.

Flat-Coat vs Golden Retriever

The Golden Retriever is about the same height and usually a little heavier, with a fuller, golden coat. The Flat-Coat is leaner and usually black or liver, with a sleeker, flat-lying coat and a longer head.

Temperament and exercise

Flat-Coats are cheerful, friendly and enthusiastic, and they love people. They're excellent family dogs for active homes. They need plenty of daily exercise and love swimming and retrieving. Without enough activity, their exuberance can become chaos. They're intelligent and eager to please, but they mature slowly and can stay silly for years.

Coat care

The dense, flat, glossy coat with feathering on the legs and tail needs brushing a couple of times a week. It sheds moderately, more during seasonal moults. After swimming, dry the ears to prevent infections.

Health: cancer risk

Cancer is a serious problem in the breed. Studies have found it to be the cause of death in about half of Flat-Coats, especially histiocytic sarcoma and other aggressive cancers. As a result, the average life span is relatively short, about 8–10 years. Hip dysplasia, eye problems and kneecap problems are also known. Ask breeders how long their dogs' relatives lived.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 121 (Flat Coated Retriever); The Flat Coated Retriever Society health information.

Flat-Coated Retriever size: common questions

How big does a Flat-Coated Retriever get?

A fully grown male Flat-Coated Retriever stands 59–62 cm (23–24.4 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 27–32 kg (60–71 lb). Females stand 56–60 cm (22–23.4 in) and weigh 27–32 kg (60–71 lb).

Is a Flat-Coated Retriever a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 30 kg (65 lb), the Flat-Coated Retriever counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 68% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Flat-Coated Retriever live?

Typical life expectancy is 8–10 years, shorter than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a Flat-Coated Retriever fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Flat-Coated Retriever weighs 27–32 kg (60–71 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
