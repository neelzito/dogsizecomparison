How big is an Akita (American)?

The Akita (American) is a giant-sized dog breed from the United States/Japan, in the Working group. Males stand 66–71 cm (26–28 in) at the shoulder and weigh 46–59 kg (100–130 lb). Females stand 61–66 cm (24–26 in) and weigh 32–46 kg (71–100 lb). On average it is taller than 88% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 91%.

Akita (American) compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Akita (American) stands 69 cm (27 in) at the shoulder: 39% of the person's height, reaching about mid-thigh. An average female, at 64 cm (25 in), reaches about mid-thigh. In feet, males stand 2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 4 in tall at the shoulder and females 2 ft to 2 ft 2 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about waist height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 10–14 years, longer than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

About the Akita (American)

The American Akita descends from Japanese Akitas brought to the United States after the Second World War, notably by US servicemen. American breeders preferred a bigger, heavier, bear-headed type and accepted all colours, while breeders in Japan went back to a lighter, fox-like type. Over time the two drifted apart. The FCI and The Kennel Club now treat them as separate breeds: the American Akita and the Akita Inu (Japanese Akita). In the US, the AKC's "Akita" is the American type. It is a powerful, dignified and fiercely loyal guardian.

What an American Akita's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 26–28 in (66–71 cm) at the shoulder for males and 24–26 in (61–66 cm) for females. Typical weights are about 45.5–59 kg (100–130 lb) for males and 32–45.5 kg (70–100 lb) for females. An American Akita reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's massive and broad-chested, heavy enough to put it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle and, because of its height and bulk, the crate size calculator suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

American vs Japanese Akita

The Japanese Akita is about the same height but much lighter, with a fox-like head and a limited range of colours (red, white, sesame or brindle with white markings). The American Akita has a broad, bear-like head, often a black mask, and comes in almost any colour. Next to a Cane Corso, it's a little taller and heavier.

Temperament

American Akitas are calm, clean and deeply loyal to their family, but reserved and often aloof with strangers. They're naturally protective and frequently don't tolerate other dogs, especially of the same sex. They need extensive early socialisation, consistent, confident training and an experienced owner. In some countries and regions, Akitas are subject to breed-specific rules, so check local laws.

Coat and health

The thick double coat sheds heavily, with big moults once or twice a year, and needs regular brushing. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat, hypothyroidism, autoimmune diseases such as sebaceous adenitis and VKH-like syndrome, and eye diseases. American Akitas often live 10–14 years.

Akita (American) size: common questions

How big does an Akita (American) get?

A fully grown male Akita (American) stands 66–71 cm (26–28 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 46–59 kg (100–130 lb). Females stand 61–66 cm (24–26 in) and weigh 32–46 kg (71–100 lb).

How tall is an Akita (American) in feet?

A male Akita (American) stands 2 ft 2 in to 2 ft 4 in tall at the shoulder (66–71 cm) and a female 2 ft to 2 ft 2 in (61–66 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about mid-thigh.

Is an Akita (American) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 46 kg (100 lb), the Akita (American) counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is taller than 88% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does an Akita (American) live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–14 years, longer than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Can an Akita (American) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Akita (American) weighs 32–59 kg (71–130 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
