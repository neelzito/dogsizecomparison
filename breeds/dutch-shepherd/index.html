How big is a Dutch Shepherd?

The Dutch Shepherd is a large-sized dog breed from the Netherlands, in the Herding group. Adults stand 55–62 cm (21.5–24.4 in) at the shoulder and weigh 19–34 kg (42–75 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 65% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 66%. At the shoulder, an average Dutch Shepherd reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 11–14 years, about average for large breeds.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Dutch Shepherd

The Dutch Shepherd (Hollandse Herder) was developed in the Netherlands in the 19th century as an all-round farm dog. It herded sheep, kept cows out of crops, guarded the farm and pulled carts. As farming changed, the breed became rare, and it now works mainly as a police, military and search-and-rescue dog, especially in the Netherlands. It's closely related to the Belgian and German Shepherds and is famous for its brindle coat.

What a Dutch Shepherd's size means at home

The breed standard gives about 55–62 cm at the shoulder, and Dutch Shepherds weigh about 19–34 kg (42–75 lb). A Dutch Shepherd reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean, athletic and agile. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Dutch Shepherd vs Malinois, Berger Picard and Appenzeller

The Belgian Malinois is very similar in build and work, but usually a little taller and heavier, with a fawn coat and black mask. The Berger Picard is a little taller and similar in weight, with a rough coat. The Appenzeller Sennenhund is shorter and stockier.

Three coats

Dutch Shepherds come in three coat types, all brindle (gold or silver brindle):

  • Short-haired: the most common, and easy to groom.
  • Long-haired: straight and harsh, with feathering.
  • Rough-haired: wiry and tousled, the rarest.

Temperament and exercise

Dutch Shepherds are intelligent, alert, loyal and very driven. They need hours of exercise and mental work every day, such as herding, obedience, tracking, protection sports or agility. They're eager to work with their owner, but without enough to do they can become restless or destructive. They're watchful with strangers and need early socialisation. They suit experienced, active owners.

Health

Dutch Shepherds are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia and, in rough-coated dogs, goniodysgenesis, an eye condition linked to glaucoma. They often live 11–14 years.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 223 (Hollandse Herdershond); Dutch Shepherd Club (NHC).

Dutch Shepherd size: common questions

How big does a Dutch Shepherd get?

An adult Dutch Shepherd stands 55–62 cm (21.5–24.4 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 19–34 kg (42–75 lb).

Is a Dutch Shepherd a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 27 kg (58 lb), the Dutch Shepherd counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 65% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Dutch Shepherd live?

Typical life expectancy is 11–14 years, about average for large breeds.

Can a Dutch Shepherd fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Dutch Shepherd weighs 19–34 kg (42–75 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
