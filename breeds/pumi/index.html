How big is a Pumi?

The Pumi is a medium-sized dog breed from Hungary, in the Herding group. Males stand 41–47 cm (15.9–18.5 in) at the shoulder and weigh 12–13 kg (26–29 lb). Females stand 38–45 cm (15–17.5 in) and weigh 10–11 kg (22–24 lb). On average it is taller than 38% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 33%. At the shoulder, an average male Pumi reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–13 years, shorter than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Pumi size: common questions

How big does a Pumi get?

A fully grown male Pumi stands 41–47 cm (15.9–18.5 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 12–13 kg (26–29 lb). Females stand 38–45 cm (15–17.5 in) and weigh 10–11 kg (22–24 lb).

Is a Pumi a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 12 kg (25 lb), the Pumi counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 38% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Pumi live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–13 years, shorter than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Pumi fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Pumi weighs 10–13 kg (22–29 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
