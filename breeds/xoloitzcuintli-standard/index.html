How big is a Xoloitzcuintli (Standard)?

The Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) is a medium-sized dog breed from Mexico, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 46–59 cm (17.9–23 in) at the shoulder and weigh 14–25 kg (30–55 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 56% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 48%. At the shoulder, an average Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 13–18 years, longer than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Xoloitzcuintli (Standard)

The Xoloitzcuintli (pronounced "show-low-eets-QUEENT-lee"), or "Xolo", is the hairless dog of Mexico, with a history going back more than 3,000 years. The Aztecs linked it to Xolotl, the god of lightning and death, and believed it guided souls through the underworld. Xolos were also kept as companions and bed warmers, as their bare skin feels warm to the touch. Today the Xolo is Mexico's national dog, and one starred as Dante in the Pixar film Coco (2017).

Three sizes, two coats

The AKC recognises three sizes, measured at the shoulder:

  • Toy: 10–14 in (25.5–35.5 cm)
  • Miniature: over 14 in up to 18 in (35.5–45.5 cm)
  • Standard: over 18 in up to 23 in (45.5–58.5 cm)

This page shows the Standard Xolo, which weighs about 13.5–25 kg (30–55 lb) and reaches roughly an adult's knee. Xolos come in a hairless variety and a coated variety with a short, flat coat. Both can be born in the same litter. The crate size calculator suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average Standard Xolo.

Xolo vs Peruvian Hairless and Chinese Crested

The Xolo is one of several hairless breeds. The Peruvian Inca Orchid is a similar lean, hairless dog from South America. The Chinese Crested is much smaller and has silky hair on its head, feet and tail. Xolos are lean, elegant and athletic, a little like a hairless sighthound.

Caring for bare skin

Hairless Xolos need sunscreen in strong sun, a coat or jumper in cold weather, and regular baths and moisturising to keep their skin healthy. Young Xolos often get acne, which usually clears with age. Coated Xolos need only occasional brushing. Hairless Xolos often have missing teeth, which is linked to the hairless gene.

Temperament and health

Xolos are calm, loyal and attentive, often bonding closely with their family and wary of strangers, which makes them good watchdogs. They're intelligent and fairly easy to train, but they need daily exercise and company. The breed is generally healthy and long-lived, often 13–18 years. Known problems include skin conditions and dental issues in hairless dogs.

Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 234 (Perro sin pelo mexicano / Xoloitzcuintle); Xoloitzcuintli Club of America.

Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) size: common questions

How big does a Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) get?

An adult Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) stands 46–59 cm (17.9–23 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 14–25 kg (30–55 lb).

Is a Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 19 kg (42 lb), the Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 56% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) live?

Typical life expectancy is 13–18 years, longer than the 13.2-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Xoloitzcuintli (Standard) weighs 14–25 kg (30–55 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
