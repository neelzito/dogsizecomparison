How big is a Caucasian Shepherd Dog?

The Caucasian Shepherd Dog is a giant-sized dog breed from the Caucasus, in the Working group. Adults stand 59–76 cm (23–29.9 in) at the shoulder and weigh 45–77 kg (99–170 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 91% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is the 8th heaviest breed. At the shoulder, an average Caucasian Shepherd Dog reaches about mid-thigh on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, longer than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Caucasian Shepherd Dog

The Caucasian Shepherd Dog (Kavkazskaya Ovcharka) is a large livestock guardian from the Caucasus Mountains, in today's Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and southern Russia. For centuries, shepherds used it to protect flocks from wolves, bears and thieves. In the Soviet era it was bred as a guard dog for military sites, prisons and borders, including the Berlin Wall. It's a massive, powerful and fiercely protective dog, and it needs a very experienced owner.

What a Caucasian Shepherd's size means at home

The AKC standard gives typical heights of about 23–30 in (58.5–76 cm) at the shoulder and weights of about 45–77 kg (100–170 lb), and many males are heavier. A Caucasian Shepherd reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. With its thick coat and broad head, it looks enormous, and its weight puts it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle, a very secure property and a 54″ (137 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator.

Caucasian vs Tibetan Mastiff, Neapolitan Mastiff and Tosa

The Tibetan Mastiff is a little taller on average but usually lighter. The Neapolitan Mastiff and the Tosa are taller and a little heavier on average, but short-coated.

Laws and restrictions

The Caucasian Shepherd is banned or restricted in a number of countries, regions and cities, and some insurers and landlords have rules for it. Check the current laws where you live, and where you plan to travel or move, before getting one.

A serious guardian

Caucasian Shepherds are devoted to their family but deeply suspicious of strangers, and they will defend their territory without hesitation. They're independent and strong-willed, bred to make their own decisions. They need extensive socialisation from puppyhood, a very experienced and confident owner, and a large, securely fenced property. They aren't suitable for first-time owners, busy households or homes with frequent visitors.

Coat and health

The thick double coat comes in short, medium and long varieties. It sheds heavily once or twice a year and needs regular brushing. Caucasian Shepherds cope well with cold but struggle with heat. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, obesity and heart problems. They often live 10–12 years.

Sources: AKC breed information; FCI standard No. 328 (Caucasian Shepherd Dog); Russian Kennel Club (RKF).

Caucasian Shepherd Dog size: common questions

How big does a Caucasian Shepherd Dog get?

An adult Caucasian Shepherd Dog stands 59–76 cm (23–29.9 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 45–77 kg (99–170 lb).

Is a Caucasian Shepherd Dog a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 61 kg (134 lb), the Caucasian Shepherd Dog counts as a giant dog breed on this site (giant = 45 kg (99 lb) and over on average). It is taller than 91% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Caucasian Shepherd Dog live?

Typical life expectancy is 10–12 years, longer than the 10.1-year average for giant breeds.

Can a Caucasian Shepherd Dog fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Caucasian Shepherd Dog weighs 45–77 kg (99–170 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
