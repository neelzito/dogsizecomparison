How big is a Silken Windhound?

The Silken Windhound is a medium-sized dog breed from the United States, in the Hound group. Males stand 47–60 cm (18.5–23.4 in) at the shoulder and weigh 15–25 kg (33–55 lb). Females stand 47–60 cm (18.5–23.4 in) and weigh 10–20 kg (22–44 lb). On average it is taller than 57% of the 268 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 44%.

Silken Windhound compared to a human

Next to a 175 cm (5′9″) adult, an average male Silken Windhound stands 53 cm (21 in) at the shoulder: 30% of the person's height, reaching about knee height. An average female, at 53 cm (21 in), reaches about knee height. In feet, males stand 1 ft 7 in to 1 ft 11 in tall at the shoulder and females 1 ft 7 in to 1 ft 11 in. Next to a 120 cm (3′11″) child, about seven years old, the shoulder comes to about hip height. The head sits higher than the shoulder, so the dog looks taller than these numbers suggest.

Typical life expectancy is 14–18 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Silken Windhound

The Silken Windhound is a young American breed. Francie Stull began developing it in Kentucky in the 1980s, crossing Borzois with smaller sighthounds and lurchers. The aim was a dog with the Borzoi's looks and gentle nature in a size that's easier to live with. The International Silken Windhound Society was founded in 1999, and the United Kennel Club recognised the breed in 2011. Silkens are still rare, but they have a devoted following among lure coursing and racing enthusiasts.

What a Silken Windhound's size means at home

The breed standard allows 18½–23½ in (47–59.5 cm) at the shoulder for both sexes, roughly an adult's knee to mid-thigh. Males usually weigh about 15–25 kg (33–55 lb) and females about 10–20 kg (22–44 lb), though the standard itself sets no weight. That puts a Silken at about the height of a Whippet, but with a long, silky coat that makes it look like a Borzoi in miniature. A big male Borzoi stands about 25 cm taller and weighs more than twice as much.

Temperament and exercise

Silkens are affectionate, gentle and sociable, usually friendly with people and other dogs, and calm indoors. They're more biddable than many sighthounds and respond well to kind, reward-based training. They still have a strong urge to chase and can sprint very fast, so most owners only let them run off the lead in safe, enclosed areas. Daily walks plus regular chances to gallop keep them happy.

Coat and health

The coat is long and silky but has little undercoat, so it's easier to keep than it looks: a brush once or twice a week usually prevents tangles. Silkens are generally healthy and long-lived, often reaching 14 years or more. Many carry the MDR1 gene mutation, which makes them sensitive to some common drugs such as ivermectin, so ask your breeder about testing and tell your vet. Like other sighthounds, they can also be sensitive to some anaesthetics.

Silken Windhound size: common questions

How big does a Silken Windhound get?

A fully grown male Silken Windhound stands 47–60 cm (18.5–23.4 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 15–25 kg (33–55 lb). Females stand 47–60 cm (18.5–23.4 in) and weigh 10–20 kg (22–44 lb).

How tall is a Silken Windhound in feet?

A male Silken Windhound stands 1 ft 7 in to 1 ft 11 in tall at the shoulder (47–60 cm) and a female 1 ft 7 in to 1 ft 11 in (47–60 cm). Next to a 5′9″ adult, an average male reaches about knee height.

Is a Silken Windhound a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 18 kg (39 lb), the Silken Windhound counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 57% of the 268 breeds listed.

How long does a Silken Windhound live?

Typical life expectancy is 14–18 years, longer than the 13.3-year average for medium breeds.

Can a Silken Windhound fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Silken Windhound weighs 10–25 kg (22–55 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).
