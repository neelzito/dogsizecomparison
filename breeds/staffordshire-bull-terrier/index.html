How big is a Staffordshire Bull Terrier?

The Staffordshire Bull Terrier is a medium-sized dog breed from England, in the Terrier group. Males stand 36–41 cm (14–15.9 in) at the shoulder and weigh 13–17 kg (28–37 lb). Females stand 36–41 cm (14–15.9 in) and weigh 11–16 kg (24–34 lb). On average it is taller than 33% of the 266 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 38%. At the shoulder, an average male Staffordshire Bull Terrier reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Staffordshire Bull Terrier

The Staffordshire Bull Terrier comes from the Black Country around Staffordshire in the English Midlands. In the 19th century, bulldogs were crossed with terriers to create a fast, strong and tenacious dog for the cruel blood sports of the time, including dog fighting, which continued illegally after bull-baiting was banned in 1835. Breed enthusiasts later turned it into a show dog and companion, and it was recognised by The Kennel Club in 1935. Today the "Staffie" is known in the UK as one of the most affectionate family dogs: people-loving, playful and very loyal.

What a Staffie's size means at home

A Staffie stands about 36–41 cm at the shoulder, just below an adult's knee, but it is heavily muscled for its height: males weigh about 13–17 kg (28–37 lb), females about 11–15.5 kg (24–34 lb). It suits most homes, including flats, as long as it gets daily exercise and company. It usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the crate size calculator. Staffies are strong pullers, so lead training and a well-fitting harness help.

Staffie vs American Pit Bull Terrier

People often confuse the two, but the American Pit Bull Terrier is clearly taller and heavier. The American Staffordshire Terrier is also bigger than the Staffie. In the UK, Staffordshire Bull Terriers are a legal, recognised breed and are not covered by the Dangerous Dogs Act, which bans the pit bull type. Rules differ in other countries and regions, so check local laws if you plan to move or travel with your dog.

Temperament

A well-raised Staffie is famously good with people, including children, and the breed standard describes it as "totally reliable" with people. With other dogs it can be less tolerant, especially dogs of the same sex, so early socialisation and supervision matter. Staffies are clever, energetic and sometimes stubborn. Reward-based training works best.

Rescue Staffies

Staffies are among the most common breeds in UK rescue centres, often through no fault of their own. If you're thinking of a Staffie, adoption is a great option. Many rescue Staffies have already been assessed with children, cats and other dogs.

Care and health

The short, smooth coat needs very little grooming. Staffies feel the cold, so a coat helps in winter. Health issues include hip dysplasia, skin allergies and some inherited eye and metabolic diseases. DNA tests exist for L-2-HGA, a metabolic disease, and hereditary cataracts, so ask breeders for the parents' results. Staffies often live 12–15 years.

Staffordshire Bull Terrier size: common questions

How big does a Staffordshire Bull Terrier get?

A fully grown male Staffordshire Bull Terrier stands 36–41 cm (14–15.9 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 13–17 kg (28–37 lb). Females stand 36–41 cm (14–15.9 in) and weigh 11–16 kg (24–34 lb).

Is a Staffordshire Bull Terrier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 14 kg (31 lb), the Staffordshire Bull Terrier counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 33% of the 266 breeds listed.

How long does a Staffordshire Bull Terrier live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can a Staffordshire Bull Terrier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Staffordshire Bull Terrier weighs 11–17 kg (24–37 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
