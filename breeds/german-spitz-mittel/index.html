How big is a German Spitz (Mittel)?

The German Spitz (Mittel) is a small-sized dog breed from Germany, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 31–38 cm (12–15 in) at the shoulder and weigh 6.8–12 kg (15–25 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 25% of the 247 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 26%. At the shoulder, an average German Spitz (Mittel) reaches just below the knee on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, about average for small breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

German Spitz (Mittel) size: common questions

How big does a German Spitz (Mittel) get?

An adult German Spitz (Mittel) stands 31–38 cm (12–15 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 6.8–12 kg (15–25 lb).

Is a German Spitz (Mittel) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 9.2 kg (20 lb), the German Spitz (Mittel) counts as a small dog breed on this site (small = 5–10 kg (11–22 lb) on average). It is taller than 25% of the 247 breeds listed.

How long does a German Spitz (Mittel) live?

Typical life expectancy is 13–15 years, about average for small breeds.

Can a German Spitz (Mittel) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A German Spitz (Mittel) weighs 6.8–12 kg (15–25 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
