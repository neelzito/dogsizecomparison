How big is an Aussiedoodle (Standard)?

The Aussiedoodle (Standard) is a medium-sized dog breed from the United States, in the designer & mixed category. Males stand 46–58 cm (18.1–22.8 in) at the shoulder and weigh 18–32 kg (40–71 lb). Females stand 43–53 cm (16.9–20.9 in) and weigh 16–27 kg (35–60 lb). On average it is taller than 53% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 56%. At the shoulder, an average male Aussiedoodle (Standard) reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds. It is too heavy for airlines that cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb), so it usually flies in the hold unless the airline only checks the carrier size.

About the Aussiedoodle (Standard)

The Aussiedoodle is a cross between an Australian Shepherd and a Poodle. It became popular in North America in the 2000s and 2010s as part of the wider doodle trend. People are drawn to its striking merle and tricolour coats, often with blue eyes, and its clever, energetic personality. It's a high-energy cross that suits active homes. As a crossbreed, it has no official standard, so size and coat vary.

How big does an Aussiedoodle get?

Size depends mainly on the Poodle parent:

  • Standard Aussiedoodle (Standard Poodle parent): about 46–58 cm at the shoulder and 18–32 kg (40–70 lb) for males
  • Mini Aussiedoodle: about 33–46 cm
  • Toy Aussiedoodle: smaller still

These are informal breeder terms. This page shows the Standard Aussiedoodle, which reaches roughly an adult's knee. A Labradoodle is a little taller on average and similar in weight. Ask breeders about both parents' size, and use the puppy weight calculator as a rough guide.

What an Aussiedoodle's size means at home

A Standard Aussiedoodle needs a medium-to-large car boot, and the crate size calculator suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

Energy: lots of it

With two very clever, energetic parents, Aussiedoodles need plenty of daily exercise and mental work, such as long walks, running, agility, trick training and puzzle games. Without it, many become restless, noisy or destructive, and some try to herd children or other pets. They're affectionate, eager to please and quick to learn, and they suit active owners who enjoy training.

Coat and colours

Coats range from curly to wavy to straighter. Curly and wavy coats shed less but mat easily, so brush several times a week and book a groom every 6–8 weeks. Merle Aussiedoodles are popular, but two merle parents should never be bred together, because "double merle" puppies are at high risk of deafness and blindness.

Health

Aussiedoodles can inherit problems from both parents, including hip dysplasia, eye diseases and the MDR1 gene variant from the Aussie side, which makes some dogs sensitive to common medicines. Choose a breeder who health-tests both parents and can show DNA results. Aussiedoodles often live 12–15 years.

Sources: AKC breed standards for the Australian Shepherd and the Poodle; Washington State University Veterinary Clinical Pharmacology Lab (MDR1).

Aussiedoodle (Standard) size: common questions

How big does an Aussiedoodle (Standard) get?

A fully grown male Aussiedoodle (Standard) stands 46–58 cm (18.1–22.8 in) at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 18–32 kg (40–71 lb). Females stand 43–53 cm (16.9–20.9 in) and weigh 16–27 kg (35–60 lb).

Is an Aussiedoodle (Standard) a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 23 kg (51 lb), the Aussiedoodle (Standard) counts as a medium dog breed on this site (medium = 10–25 kg (22–55 lb) on average). It is taller than 53% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does an Aussiedoodle (Standard) live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–15 years, about average for medium breeds.

Can an Aussiedoodle (Standard) fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Aussiedoodle (Standard) weighs 16–32 kg (35–71 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
