How big is a Eurasier?

The Eurasier is a large-sized dog breed from Germany, in the Non-Sporting group. Adults stand 49–61 cm (19.1–24 in) at the shoulder and weigh 18–32 kg (40–71 lb); the breed standard gives the same range for males and females. On average it is taller than 58% of the 256 breeds on this site, and it is heavier than 62%. At the shoulder, an average Eurasier reaches about knee height on a 175 cm (5′9″) adult.

Typical life expectancy is 12–16 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Sizes follow the American Kennel Club (AKC) breed standard.

About the Eurasier

The Eurasier was developed in Germany in the 1960s by Julius Wipfel. His aim was a calm, family-friendly spitz-type companion. He crossed the German Wolfspitz with the Chow Chow, producing what was first called the "Wolf-Chow", and later added the Samoyed. The FCI recognised the breed in 1973 as the Eurasier, reflecting its European and Asian roots. It's a balanced, gentle and devoted family dog, still more common in Germany and Switzerland than elsewhere.

What a Eurasier's size means at home

The breed standard gives about 52–60 cm at the shoulder for males and 48–56 cm for females. The figures on this page, about 48.5–61 cm and 18–32 kg (40–70 lb), cover both sexes. A Eurasier reaches roughly an adult's knee, and its thick coat makes it look larger. Its weight puts it at the lower end of the large size class on this site. It needs a medium-to-large car boot, and the crate size calculator suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult.

Eurasier vs Chow Chow, Keeshond and Samoyed

The Eurasier is a little taller than the Chow Chow and less heavy-set, and usually more relaxed with strangers. Next to a Standard Poodle or a Dalmatian, it's about the same height, with a thick spitz coat and a fox-like head. Eurasiers come in many colours, including red, fawn, wolf-grey, black and black and tan, and some have a blue-black or spotted tongue from the Chow.

Temperament and exercise

Eurasiers are calm, even-tempered and very attached to their family. They're often reserved with strangers but rarely aggressive. They're sensitive and don't do well when left alone for long or kept outside away from their people. They need daily walks and play but are moderate in energy, and they respond best to gentle, consistent training.

Coat and health

The thick, medium-length double coat sheds a lot, especially once or twice a year, and needs brushing at least weekly, more during moults. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, kneecap problems, hypothyroidism and eyelid problems (entropion). Eurasiers often live 12–16 years.

Sources: FCI standard No. 291 (Eurasier); AKC breed information; Eurasier Klub (Germany).

Eurasier size: common questions

How big does a Eurasier get?

An adult Eurasier stands 49–61 cm (19.1–24 in) tall at the shoulder (withers) and weighs 18–32 kg (40–71 lb).

Is a Eurasier a small, medium or large dog?

With an average weight of about 25 kg (55 lb), the Eurasier counts as a large dog breed on this site (large = 25–45 kg (55–99 lb) on average). It is taller than 58% of the 256 breeds listed.

How long does a Eurasier live?

Typical life expectancy is 12–16 years, longer than the 12.2-year average for large breeds.

Can a Eurasier fly in the airplane cabin?

Usually not. A Eurasier weighs 18–32 kg (40–71 lb), well over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, and is too big for an under-seat carrier. Most airlines fly dogs this size in the hold (cargo).

Sizes are breed-standard ranges for adult dogs; individual dogs, working lines and dogs from different countries can be bigger or smaller. Airline notes are a general guide only. Check with your airline and your vet. Disclaimer.
