Dog size classes explained

What "small", "medium" and "large" actually mean, and why two sources can put the same breed in different classes.

Updated 2026-11-20

There is no official definition of a "small" or "large" dog. Kennel clubs group breeds by their original job, not size, and vets, insurers, airlines and pet-food brands each draw their own lines. This site sorts every breed by its average adult weight, males and females together, into five classes.

Weight works better than height for this because it reflects the whole body. A Basset Hound is only 33–38 cm (13–15 in) tall but weighs as much as a Border Collie, and it needs a crate, bed and food portion to match.

Why other sources disagree

  • Pet-food and insurance companies often use only three or four classes, with "large" starting anywhere from 23 kg (50 lb) to 30 kg (66 lb).
  • Vets sometimes classify by expected adult weight to plan growth diets: "large-breed puppy food" is usually for dogs expected to exceed about 25 kg (55 lb).
  • Kennel clubs have a Toy group, but it includes breeds by tradition as much as size. The Miniature Dachshund is classed as a hound, and some toy breeds outweigh small terriers.

A breed near a boundary, like the 7–11 kg Shetland Sheepdog, can reasonably be called small or medium.

Breed groups

The American Kennel Club (AKC) sorts breeds into seven groups by original purpose; this site adds an eighth for designer crosses. Within a group, sizes can range from tiny to giant.

Height at the withers

All heights here are measured at the withers, the top of the shoulder blades. A dog's head usually stands 20–40% higher. See how to measure your dog.
