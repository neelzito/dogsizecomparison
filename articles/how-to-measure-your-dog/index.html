How to measure your dog

Height at the withers, length and weight: the measurements behind every breed standard, crate chart and carrier limit.

Updated 2026-11-19

Breed standards, crate charts and airline rules all use a handful of measurements. You need a tape measure, a spirit level or a straight book, and a calm dog standing squarely on a flat floor.

Height at the withers

The withers are the highest point of the shoulder blades, where the neck meets the back. Dog heights, including every figure on this site, are measured here and not at the head, because the head moves.

  1. Stand your dog on a flat, hard floor with all four legs straight under the body.
  2. Feel for the top of the shoulder blades just behind the neck.
  3. Lay a level or a stiff book flat across the withers, parallel to the floor.
  4. Measure from the floor straight up to the underside of the level.

Measure two or three times and take the average. A wriggly dog is easier with a helper and a treat held at nose height.

Length

For crates and carriers you need the length from the tip of the nose to the base of the tail (where the tail joins the body). Don't include the tail itself. Breed standards sometimes use a different length, from the point of the shoulder to the point of the buttock, to describe body proportions.

Head height

For a crate, also measure from the floor to the top of the head (or the tips of erect ears) with your dog standing naturally. The crate's inside height should be about 5 cm (2 in) more. The crate size calculator does the maths.

Weight

  • Small dogs: weigh yourself, then weigh yourself holding the dog, and subtract.
  • Large dogs: most vet practices let you use their walk-on scale for free.
  • Puppies: weigh weekly at the same time of day; growth comes in spurts. The puppy weight calculator estimates adult weight from age and current weight.

Neck and chest, for collars and harnesses

Measure the neck where the collar sits and add two fingers' width. For a harness, measure the chest girth around the widest part of the rib cage, just behind the front legs.
