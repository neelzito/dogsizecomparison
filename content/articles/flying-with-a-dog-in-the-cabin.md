> **Please check before you fly.** Airline pet policies change often and can differ by route, aircraft and cabin class. This guide is general information, not travel advice, and we can't guarantee that the rules below are current. Always confirm with your airline, and check your destination's entry requirements, before booking.

Most airlines that accept pets in the cabin have two limits: a **maximum weight**, usually for dog and carrier together, and a **maximum carrier size** that fits under the seat in front of you. Your dog stays in the carrier for the whole flight and counts as (part of) your hand luggage on many airlines.

## The 8 kg rule

Most European airlines, including Lufthansa, Swiss, KLM, Air France and Turkish Airlines, cap dog plus carrier at **8 kg (17.6 lb)**. A light soft-sided carrier weighs 1–2 kg, which leaves roughly **6.5 kg (14 lb) for the dog**. That's the rule this site uses for its ✈ cabin labels, because a dog that meets it can fly with almost any airline that allows pets in the cabin.

US airlines are often more relaxed about weight. Delta, United and Alaska set no weight limit at all, but the dog must fit in a carrier that slides under the seat, and it must be able to stand up and turn around inside. In practice that still means a dog under about 9 kg (20 lb).

## Before you book

- **Book the pet early.** Most airlines allow only a few pets per flight, often 1–2 per cabin class.
- **Check the carrier size for your aircraft.** Under-seat space differs between planes and seat rows. Bulkhead and some exit-row seats have no under-seat storage.
- **Check entry rules for your destination.** International trips usually need a microchip, rabies vaccination and a pet passport or health certificate, sometimes weeks in advance.
- **Practise with the carrier.** Let your dog sleep in it at home for a few weeks before the trip.
- **Skip the big meal.** Feed a light meal about four hours before departure and offer water until boarding.

## Health first

Ask your vet whether your dog is fit to fly, especially if it is very young, old, pregnant, unwell or a snub-nosed breed. Don't give sedatives unless your vet prescribes them; many airlines refuse sedated animals.

## Too big for the cabin?

Larger dogs fly in a heated, pressurised part of the hold, booked as checked baggage or as cargo. Some airlines don't carry snub-nosed (brachycephalic) breeds such as Pugs, French Bulldogs and Bulldogs in the hold at all, because they are prone to breathing problems. If your dog is close to the limit, a trip by car, train or ferry may be kinder.
