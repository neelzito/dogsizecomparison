The Spanish Mastiff (*Mastín Español*) is one of the largest dog breeds in the world. For centuries, it protected the huge flocks of Merino sheep that walked hundreds of kilometres between summer and winter pastures across Spain, from wolves and bears. One Mastín guarded around a hundred sheep, and each dog traditionally wore a spiked collar. Today it's Spain's national dog and is still used as a livestock guardian, as well as a calm family guardian.

### What a Spanish Mastiff's size means at home

The FCI standard sets minimum heights of 77 cm for males and 72 cm for females, and many dogs are taller. Spanish Mastiffs typically weigh about 63.5–90.5 kg (140–200 lb), and some males weigh more. A Spanish Mastiff reaches roughly an adult's hip. It's massive, with a broad head, loose skin and heavy bone. It needs a large vehicle, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=spanish-mastiff)) and a large, secure property.

### Spanish Mastiff vs English Mastiff

The [English Mastiff](/compare/mastiff-english-vs-spanish-mastiff/) is about the same height and usually a little heavier, with a shorter coat and a flatter face. The Pyrenean Mastiff is a close relative from northern Spain, usually white with patches.

### Temperament

Spanish Mastiffs are calm, noble and devoted to their family, but naturally protective and wary of strangers. They're independent, bred to guard without commands, so training takes patience. They need early socialisation, an experienced owner and plenty of space. They often bark at night.

### Care and health

The medium-length double coat sheds seasonally. Puppies grow very fast, so feed a giant-breed puppy food and protect their joints. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat and eyelid problems. Spanish Mastiffs often live 10–12 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 91 (Mastín Español); Real Sociedad Canina de España (RSCE); AKC breed information.*
