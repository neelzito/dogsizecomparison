The Bedlington Terrier comes from the mining town of Bedlington in Northumberland, in north-east England. Miners and Romany travellers bred it in the 18th and 19th centuries as a fast, tough terrier for catching rats, rabbits and badgers. Some greyhound or whippet ancestry gave it speed and an arched back. Its soft, curly coat and pear-shaped head make it look like a lamb, but underneath it's a true terrier.

### What a Bedlington's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 16.5 in (42 cm) at the shoulder for males and 15.5 in (39.5 cm) for females, and about 7.5–10.5 kg (17–23 lb). A Bedlington reaches just below an adult's knee. It's lean, graceful and leggy, with an arched back. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bedlington-terrier) suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate for an average adult because of its leg length. It's too heavy for most airline cabins.

### Bedlington vs Welsh Terrier and Whippet

The [Welsh Terrier](/compare/bedlington-terrier-vs-welsh-terrier/) is a little shorter and about the same weight, with a wiry black-and-tan coat. With its arched back and fast gallop, the Bedlington is also reminiscent of a small Whippet, and the two are sometimes crossed to make "Bedlington lurchers".

### Temperament and exercise

Bedlingtons are gentle and affectionate at home, often calm and cuddly, but fast and determined outdoors. They need daily exercise and a chance to run in a safe area, and their chase instinct means recall can be unreliable. They can be scrappy with other dogs if challenged.

### Coat and health

The soft, curly coat sheds very little but needs brushing a couple of times a week and clipping every 6–8 weeks. Bedlingtons are born dark (blue or liver) and lighten as they grow. The breed is known for copper toxicosis, an inherited liver disease caused by copper build-up, for which a DNA test exists. Eye problems and kidney disease also occur. Bedlingtons often live 11–16 years.
