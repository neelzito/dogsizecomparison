The Landseer is a large black-and-white water dog, named after the English painter Sir Edwin Landseer, who painted these dogs in the 19th century. In Britain and the US, black-and-white dogs of this type are accepted as Newfoundlands. In continental Europe, the FCI recognises the Landseer (European Continental Type) as a separate breed, taller and leggier than the Newfoundland. It's a calm, gentle and powerful dog that loves water.

### What a Landseer's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 72–80 cm at the shoulder for males and 67–72 cm for females, with weights of about 50–70 kg for males and 45–60 kg for females. A Landseer reaches roughly an adult's hip, and its weight puts it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (see the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=landseer-european)) and plenty of space.

### Landseer vs Leonberger and Newfoundland

The [Leonberger](/compare/landseer-european-vs-leonberger/) is about the same size. The Newfoundland is a little shorter and more heavily built.

### Temperament and care

Landseers are gentle, friendly and patient, good with children and other dogs. They love swimming. The long double coat sheds heavily and needs regular brushing. Expect drool. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, heart disease and bloat. Landseers often live 10–12 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 226 (Landseer, European Continental Type); Deutscher Landseer Club.*
