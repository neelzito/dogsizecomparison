The Kai Ken comes from the mountains of Yamanashi Prefecture (formerly Kai province) in Japan, where it was used to hunt deer, boar and other game. It's one of Japan's six native spitz breeds and was declared a natural monument in 1934. It's known as the "tiger dog" (*Tora Inu*) because of its brindle coat. It's rare outside Japan.

### What a Kai Ken's size means at home

The breed standard calls for about 18.5–19.5 in (47–49.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 15.5–17 in (39.5–43 cm) for females. Kai Ken weigh about 13.5–18 kg (30–40 lb) for males and 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb) for females. A Kai reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's lean, agile and athletic. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=kai-ken). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Kai Ken vs Shiba, Shikoku and Klee Kai

The Kai Ken is larger than the Shiba Inu and similar in height to the [Shikoku](/breeds/shikoku/), but lighter. The [Alaskan Klee Kai](/compare/alaskan-klee-kai-vs-kai-ken/), despite the similar name, is unrelated and much smaller. Next to a [Standard Schnauzer](/compare/kai-ken-vs-standard-schnauzer/), a Kai is about the same height and lighter.

### Temperament and exercise

Kai Ken are loyal, intelligent and alert, often bonding closely with their family and reserved with strangers. They're agile and can climb, so they need a secure garden, and their prey drive is strong. They need daily exercise and patient training.

### Care and health

The double coat sheds seasonally. Kai Ken are generally healthy and often live 12–15 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 317 (Kai); Kai Ken Society of America; Nihon Ken Hozonkai.*
