The Catahoula Leopard Dog is named after Catahoula Parish in Louisiana, where it was developed to hunt wild hogs and round up semi-wild cattle in the swamps and woods. Its ancestors probably included dogs kept by Native Americans and dogs brought by Spanish and French settlers. It's the state dog of Louisiana. The breed is known for its striking merle "leopard" coat and its often pale or mismatched "glass" eyes.

### What a Catahoula's size means at home

Catahoulas usually stand about 56–61 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weigh about 22.5–43 kg (50–95 lb). Their size varies a lot, because they were bred for working ability rather than looks. A Catahoula is muscular and athletic. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=catahoula-leopard-dog) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult.

### Catahoula vs Old English Sheepdog and Bergamasco

The [Old English Sheepdog](/compare/catahoula-leopard-dog-vs-old-english-sheepdog/) and the [Bergamasco](/compare/bergamasco-sheepdog-vs-catahoula-leopard-dog/) are similar in size, but both have heavy, long coats. The Catahoula has a short, flat coat in merle, solid or brindle colours.

### Temperament and exercise

Catahoulas are intelligent, energetic and independent, loyal to their family and often wary of strangers. They were bred to work hard all day, so they need a lot of exercise and a job: herding, tracking, agility or long, active days outdoors. They can be strong-willed and need consistent, confident training and early socialisation.

### Merle and health

As in other merle breeds, two merle dogs should never be bred together, because "double merle" puppies have a high risk of deafness and blindness. Other known problems include hip dysplasia and eye conditions. Catahoulas often live 10–14 years.
