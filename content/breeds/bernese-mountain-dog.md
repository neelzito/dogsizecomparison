The Bernese Mountain Dog (*Berner Sennenhund*) is a farm dog from the canton of Bern in Switzerland. Farmers used these big, calm, tricoloured dogs to guard the farm, drive cattle and pull carts of milk and cheese to the dairy. As farming modernised, the breed declined. Around 1900, Swiss dog lovers, including the geologist Albert Heim, began collecting the best remaining dogs, many from the area around Dürrbach, and the breed club was founded in 1907. The Bernese is the only one of the four Swiss mountain dog breeds with a long coat. Today it is loved as a gentle, good-natured family dog.

### What a Bernese's size means at home

A male Bernese stands about 64–70 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 36–52 kg (80–115 lb). Females stand about 59–66 cm and weigh 32–43 kg. Its thick coat makes it look even bigger. It needs plenty of space, an estate car or van, and the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bernese-mountain-dog) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male. A Bernese feels the heat much more than the cold, so it loves snow but needs shade and rest on hot days.

### Bernese vs Swissy, Saint Bernard and Bernedoodle

The [Greater Swiss Mountain Dog](/compare/bernese-mountain-dog-vs-greater-swiss-mountain-dog/) has the same colours but a short coat, and it's a little taller and clearly heavier. The [Saint Bernard](/compare/bernese-mountain-dog-vs-saint-bernard/), another Swiss breed, is much heavier. The [Bernedoodle](/compare/bernese-mountain-dog-vs-bernedoodle-standard/), a Bernese crossed with a Poodle, varies more in size and coat.

### Growth and care

Bernese puppies grow fast, so feed a large-breed puppy food and keep them lean. Avoid long runs, lots of jumping and stairs while the joints are developing. They reach adult height at around 15–18 months but often don't fill out until 2–3 years. The coat sheds heavily, especially in spring and autumn, and needs brushing at least once or twice a week.

### Health and life span

The Bernese has one of the shortest life spans of any breed, usually only 7–10 years. Cancer is the main reason: studies in several European countries have found cancer to be the cause of death in roughly a third to more than half of Bernese dogs, much more often than in dogs overall. Histiocytic sarcoma, an aggressive cancer, is especially common in the breed. Hip and elbow dysplasia and bloat also occur. If you're buying a puppy, ask about the parents' hip and elbow scores and how long their relatives lived.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 45 (Berner Sennenhund); Danish and Swiss studies on causes of death in Bernese Mountain Dogs.*
