The Eurasier was developed in Germany in the 1960s by Julius Wipfel. His aim was a calm, family-friendly spitz-type companion. He crossed the German Wolfspitz with the Chow Chow, producing what was first called the "Wolf-Chow", and later added the Samoyed. The FCI recognised the breed in 1973 as the Eurasier, reflecting its European and Asian roots. It's a balanced, gentle and devoted family dog, still more common in Germany and Switzerland than elsewhere.

### What a Eurasier's size means at home

The breed standard gives about 52–60 cm at the shoulder for males and 48–56 cm for females. The figures on this page, about 48.5–61 cm and 18–32 kg (40–70 lb), cover both sexes. A Eurasier reaches roughly an adult's knee, and its thick coat makes it look larger. Its weight puts it at the lower end of the large size class on this site. It needs a medium-to-large car boot, and the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=eurasier) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult.

### Eurasier vs Chow Chow, Keeshond and Samoyed

The Eurasier is a little taller than the Chow Chow and less heavy-set, and usually more relaxed with strangers. Next to a [Standard Poodle](/compare/eurasier-vs-standard-poodle/) or a [Dalmatian](/compare/dalmatian-vs-eurasier/), it's about the same height, with a thick spitz coat and a fox-like head. Eurasiers come in many colours, including red, fawn, wolf-grey, black and black and tan, and some have a blue-black or spotted tongue from the Chow.

### Temperament and exercise

Eurasiers are calm, even-tempered and very attached to their family. They're often reserved with strangers but rarely aggressive. They're sensitive and don't do well when left alone for long or kept outside away from their people. They need daily walks and play but are moderate in energy, and they respond best to gentle, consistent training.

### Coat and health

The thick, medium-length double coat sheds a lot, especially once or twice a year, and needs brushing at least weekly, more during moults. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, kneecap problems, hypothyroidism and eyelid problems (entropion). Eurasiers often live 12–16 years.
