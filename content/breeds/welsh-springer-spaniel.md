The Welsh Springer Spaniel is a gundog from Wales, used for centuries to find game birds and "spring" them into the air for hunters. Red-and-white spaniels like it appear in old Welsh records, and it was recognised as a separate breed in the early 20th century. It's always red and white, and it's smaller and lighter than the English Springer Spaniel. Today it's a loyal, cheerful family dog that is still used as a working gundog.

### What a Welshie's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–19 in (45.5–48.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 17–18 in (43–45.5 cm) for females. Welshies weigh about 18–25 kg (40–55 lb) for males and 16–22.5 kg (35–50 lb) for females. A Welshie reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's compact, athletic and sturdy. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=welsh-springer-spaniel). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Welsh vs English Springer and Field Spaniel

The [English Springer Spaniel](/breeds/english-springer-spaniel/) is a little taller and heavier, and it comes in liver or black and white. The [Field Spaniel](/compare/field-spaniel-vs-welsh-springer-spaniel/) is about the same height and a little lighter, usually solid black or liver.

### Temperament and exercise

Welsh Springers are friendly, loyal and affectionate, very attached to their family, and sometimes more reserved with strangers than English Springers. They need plenty of daily exercise, such as running, retrieving, swimming or long walks, plus mental work. They're keen hunters, so recall needs work. They're sensitive and respond best to gentle, reward-based training. They often don't like being left alone for long.

### Coat and health

The silky, flat coat with feathering needs brushing a couple of times a week, and the ears need regular checks to prevent infections. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, eye conditions (glaucoma, cataracts), hypothyroidism and epilepsy. Welsh Springers often live 12–15 years.
