The English Foxhound was developed in Britain from the 16th century onwards to hunt foxes in packs, followed by riders on horseback. Hunts kept careful stud books, so many packs can trace their pedigrees back to the 18th century. Hunting foxes with hounds was banned in England and Wales in 2005 and in Scotland in 2002, and today packs follow artificial trails. English Foxhounds are rarely kept as pets, but they're friendly, sociable dogs.

### What an English Foxhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 24 in (61 cm) at the shoulder, and English Foxhounds weigh about 27–34 kg (60–75 lb). An English Foxhound reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's solid, heavy-boned and athletic. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=english-foxhound) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult.

### English vs American Foxhound and Greyhound

The [American Foxhound](/compare/american-foxhound-vs-english-foxhound/) is about the same height and weight but lighter-boned and leggier. The [Greyhound](/compare/english-foxhound-vs-greyhound/) is much taller and about the same weight.

### Temperament and exercise

English Foxhounds are friendly, gentle and very sociable, bred to live and work in packs, so they get on well with other dogs. They need a lot of exercise, ideally long runs, and their nose leads them, so recall is often unreliable. They bay loudly and don't like being alone.

### Care and health

The short, hard coat needs only weekly brushing. English Foxhounds are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia and kidney disease. They often live 10–13 years.
