The Coton de Tuléar is a small white companion dog from Madagascar, named after the port city of Tuléar (today Toliara) and its cotton-like coat. It descends from Bichon-type dogs that reached Madagascar with traders and sailors several centuries ago. For a time it was associated with the Merina nobility, and it has been called the "Royal Dog of Madagascar". The breed was little known abroad until the 1970s, when it was exported to Europe and the US. Today it's a cheerful, affectionate and sociable companion.

### What a Coton's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 10–11 in (25.5–28 cm) at the shoulder and 9–15 lb (4–7 kg) for males, and 9–10 in (23–25.5 cm) and 8–13 lb (3.5–6 kg) for females. A Coton reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's small, sturdy and a little longer than tall. It suits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=coton-de-tulear).

### Can a Coton fly in the cabin?

Usually, yes. Most Cotons plus a light carrier stay within the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set, though the heaviest males may not. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=coton-de-tulear).

### Coton vs Shih Tzu and Bichon

The Coton is [similar in size to a Shih Tzu](/compare/coton-de-tulear-vs-shih-tzu/), but it has a longer muzzle and a soft white coat. It's closely related to the Bichon Frise and the Maltese. The Bichon's coat is curly and trimmed round, the Maltese's is silky and straight, and the Coton's is soft and fluffy, like cotton wool.

### Coat care

The Coton's long, soft coat sheds very little but mats easily, especially during the change from puppy to adult coat, at around 8–14 months. It needs brushing down to the skin several times a week, or daily for a full coat. Many owners keep it shorter. Puppies are sometimes born with light tan or grey patches that usually fade.

### Temperament and health

Cotons are happy, playful and devoted to their family, usually good with children, other dogs and cats. They're clever and fairly easy to train. Many love to "talk" and do clownish tricks. They don't like being left alone for long. The breed is generally healthy. Known problems include loose kneecaps, hip dysplasia, eye problems and allergies. Cotons are long-lived, often 15–19 years.
