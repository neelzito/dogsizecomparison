The Otterhound is a large, rough-coated British scent hound, bred to hunt otters along rivers. It has webbed feet, an oily, waterproof double coat and a powerful nose that can follow a scent over water. When otter hunting was banned in the UK in 1978, the breed lost its job, and it's now one of the rarest breeds in the world, with only a few hundred dogs. The Kennel Club lists it as a vulnerable native breed.

### What an Otterhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 27 in (68.5 cm) at the shoulder and about 115 lb (52 kg) for males, and about 24 in (61 cm) and 80 lb (36.5 kg) for females. An Otterhound reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's big, shaggy and strong. It needs a large vehicle and, because of its height, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=otterhound) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

### Otterhound vs Bloodhound and Airedale

The [Bloodhound](/compare/bloodhound-vs-otterhound/) is a little shorter and lighter, with a short coat and loose skin. The Airedale Terrier was developed partly from the Otterhound and is much smaller.

### Temperament and exercise

Otterhounds are friendly, boisterous and affectionate, good with children and other dogs. They need a lot of exercise and love water. Their nose leads them, so recall is unreliable. They have a loud, deep bay.

### Care and health

The rough coat needs weekly brushing and gets very muddy. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat and a bleeding disorder (Glanzmann's thrombasthenia), for which a DNA test exists. Otterhounds often live 10–13 years.
