The Norwegian Buhund is an ancient Nordic farm dog. Dogs of this type have been found in Viking graves, including the Gokstad ship burial from about 900 AD. For centuries, Norwegian farmers used it to herd sheep, cattle and horses and to guard the farm. Its name comes from *bu*, the Norwegian word for farm or homestead. It's a cheerful, energetic spitz with a curled tail.

### What a Buhund's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 17–18.5 in (43–47 cm) at the shoulder for males and 16–17.5 in (40.5–44.5 cm) for females. Buhunds weigh about 14–18 kg (31–40 lb) for males and 12–16 kg for females. A Buhund reaches just below an adult's knee. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=norwegian-buhund). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Buhund vs Puli and Icelandic Sheepdog

The [Puli](/compare/norwegian-buhund-vs-puli/) is a little shorter and lighter, with a corded coat. The [Icelandic Sheepdog](/breeds/icelandic-sheepdog/) is a close relative and similar in size. Buhunds are wheaten or black.

### Temperament and exercise

Buhunds are friendly, affectionate and very energetic, good with children and other pets. They're vocal and make alert watchdogs. They need a lot of daily exercise and mental work, and they excel at herding and agility.

### Care and health

The thick double coat sheds heavily once or twice a year. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and eye conditions. Buhunds often live 12–15 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 237 (Norsk Buhund); Norwegian Buhund Club of America.*
