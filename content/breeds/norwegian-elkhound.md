The Norwegian Elkhound (*Norsk Elghund Grå*) is an ancient Nordic spitz used for thousands of years in the forests of Norway. Its main job was hunting moose, called "elk" in Europe. The dog finds a moose, holds it at bay by barking and dodging around it, and keeps it there until the hunter arrives. Elkhound-type dogs have been found in Viking-era graves, and the breed is Norway's national dog. It's a hardy, bold and friendly companion, still used for hunting in Scandinavia.

### What an Elkhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 20.5 in (52 cm) at the shoulder and 55 lb (25 kg) for males, and 19.5 in (49.5 cm) and 48 lb (22 kg) for females. An Elkhound reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's square, compact and sturdy, with a tightly curled tail. It suits most homes, and the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=norwegian-elkhound) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Elkhound vs Harrier

The [Harrier](/compare/harrier-vs-norwegian-elkhound/), an English pack hound, is about the same height and weight, but a very different type of dog. The Elkhound is a spitz with a thick coat and curled tail, the Harrier a short-coated scent hound. The [Bavarian Mountain Scent Hound](/compare/bavarian-mountain-scent-hound-vs-norwegian-elkhound/) is a little shorter.

### Temperament and exercise

Elkhounds are friendly, confident and independent, very loyal to their family and usually good with children. They were bred to work independently and bark at game, so they're vocal and can be stubborn. Patient, consistent training works best, and recall can be unreliable. They need daily exercise and enjoy hiking, especially in cold weather. They love food, so watch their weight.

### Coat and health

The thick, weatherproof double coat sheds a lot, with heavy moults once or twice a year. It needs brushing a couple of times a week and daily during moults. The coat insulates well against cold but makes Elkhounds sensitive to heat. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and Fanconi syndrome, a kidney disease. Elkhounds often live 12–15 years.
