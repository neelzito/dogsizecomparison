The Chesapeake Bay Retriever is an American breed, from the shores of Chesapeake Bay in Maryland. According to the breed's history, it began in 1807 with two Newfoundland-type puppies, Sailor and Canton, rescued from a sinking English ship. They were bred with local retrievers and hounds to create a tough dog that could retrieve ducks from icy, rough water all day. It is the state dog of Maryland. With its oily, wavy coat and strong will, the "Chessie" is one of the hardiest retrievers.

### What a Chessie's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 23–26 in (58.5–66 cm) and 65–80 lb (29.5–36.5 kg) for males, and 21–24 in (53.5–61 cm) and 55–70 lb (25–32 kg) for females. A Chessie reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's powerful and solidly built. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=chesapeake-bay-retriever).

### Chessie vs Labrador

The Chessie is [similar in size to a Labrador](/compare/chesapeake-bay-retriever-vs-labrador-retriever/), a little taller on average and about the same weight. But it looks and behaves differently: its coat is wavy and oily, in shades of brown, sedge or "deadgrass" (straw to tan), and its eyes are often yellow or amber. It's also more independent and protective than the typically easy-going Labrador.

### The coat

The Chessie's double coat has a dense, woolly undercoat and a short, harsh, wavy outer coat with natural oils that make it nearly waterproof. It needs only weekly brushing and occasional baths, as too much washing strips the oils. It sheds seasonally and has a distinctive doggy smell.

### Temperament and exercise

Chessies are intelligent, loyal and hard-working, but more strong-willed and reserved with strangers than other retrievers. They're often protective of their family and property. They need plenty of daily exercise and love swimming and retrieving, even in cold water. Early socialisation and consistent, confident training are important.

### Health

Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), degenerative myelopathy and exercise-induced collapse. DNA tests exist for several of these. Chessies often live 10–13 years.
