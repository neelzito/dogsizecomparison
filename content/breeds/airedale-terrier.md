The Airedale Terrier is the largest of the terriers, often called "the King of Terriers". It was developed in the 19th century in the valley of the River Aire in Yorkshire, England, where working men crossed local terriers with the Otterhound to create a dog that could hunt otters and rats along the riverbanks. Its size, courage and intelligence later made it a messenger, guard and Red Cross dog in the First World War, and one of the first breeds used by police in Britain and Germany.

### What an Airedale's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 23 in (58.5 cm) at the shoulder for males, with females slightly smaller. An Airedale reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh and weighs about 22.5–32 kg (50–70 lb). It's square, muscular and athletic. It needs a big boot and usually a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=airedale-terrier).

### Airedale vs Bull Terrier

Next to a [Bull Terrier](/compare/airedale-terrier-vs-bull-terrier/), the Airedale is a little taller and similar in weight, with a long, flat head and a wiry black-and-tan coat instead of the Bull Terrier's smooth coat and egg-shaped head. It looks like a giant version of the Welsh or Lakeland Terrier.

### Temperament and exercise

Airedales are clever, confident, playful and full of energy, with a real terrier's independence and sense of humour. They're loyal to their family and good watchdogs. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental challenges, and they enjoy running, swimming, tracking and dog sports. They can be stubborn, so training should be consistent and fun. Many have a strong prey drive towards cats and small animals.

### Coat care

The wiry double coat sheds very little. To keep its harsh texture and rich colour, show dogs are hand-stripped (dead hair is plucked out). Most pet Airedales are clipped every 6–8 weeks instead, which softens the coat. The beard needs regular cleaning after meals and drinks.

### Health

Airedales are generally hardy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism, skin allergies and some eye conditions. They often live 11–14 years.
