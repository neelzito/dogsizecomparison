The Scottish Deerhound is an ancient sighthound from the Scottish Highlands, bred to course and bring down red deer. For centuries, it was the dog of Highland chiefs, and according to tradition no one below the rank of earl was allowed to own one. The breed declined when deer hunting with guns replaced coursing and the clan system collapsed after 1745, but enthusiasts revived it in the 19th century. Sir Walter Scott called his Deerhound, Maida, "the most perfect creature of heaven". Today it's a gentle, quiet and dignified giant.

### What a Deerhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 30–32 in (76–81.5 cm) and above at the shoulder and 85–110 lb (38.5–50 kg) for males, and from 28 in (71 cm) and 75–95 lb (34–43 kg) for females. A Deerhound reaches roughly an adult's hip. It's very tall but slim and light for its height. It needs a big vehicle and, because of its height, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=scottish-deerhound) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male. A Deerhound's head is easily level with a kitchen worktop, so keep food out of reach.

### Deerhound vs Irish Wolfhound and Borzoi

The [Irish Wolfhound](/compare/irish-wolfhound-vs-scottish-deerhound/) looks like a bigger, heavier Deerhound: taller and much more solid. The [Borzoi](/compare/borzoi-vs-scottish-deerhound/) is about the same height and a little lighter, with a silky coat. The Deerhound's coat is harsh and wiry, usually blue-grey, grey or brindle.

### Temperament and exercise

Deerhounds are gentle, friendly and calm, quiet at home and good with children and other dogs. They're too friendly to make good guard dogs. Like all sighthounds, they love to run and have a strong prey drive, so they need regular chances to sprint in a large, secure area. They can't hear you at full speed. They're sensitive and respond best to gentle training.

### Care and health

The rough, wiry coat needs only weekly brushing and occasional tidying. Deerhounds need soft bedding, as their bony joints are prone to calluses. Known health problems include bloat, heart disease (cardiomyopathy), bone cancer and cystinuria, a bladder stone disorder. Like many giant breeds, Deerhounds have a short life, usually 8–11 years.
