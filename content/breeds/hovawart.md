The Hovawart is a German guardian breed whose name comes from Middle High German *hova* (farm or court) and *wart* (watchman). Dogs of this type guarded farms and estates in medieval Germany, but by the early 20th century the old type had disappeared. In the 1920s, Kurt Friedrich König and others recreated the Hovawart from farm dogs of the Black Forest and Harz region, crossed with breeds such as the Kuvasz, Newfoundland and German Shepherd. Today it's a versatile working and family dog, popular in Germany and Scandinavia.

### What a Hovawart's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 63–70 cm at the shoulder for males and 58–65 cm for females. The figures on this page, about 58.5–71 cm and 29.5–41 kg (65–90 lb), cover both sexes. A Hovawart reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's strong, athletic and a little longer than tall. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=hovawart).

### Hovawart vs Giant Schnauzer, Doberman and Malamute

The [Giant Schnauzer](/compare/giant-schnauzer-vs-hovawart/) and the [Alaskan Malamute](/compare/alaskan-malamute-vs-hovawart/) are similar in size. The [Doberman](/compare/doberman-pinscher-vs-hovawart/) is a little taller and heavier. A blond Hovawart is often mistaken for a Golden Retriever, but it's leaner, longer-legged and has a guardian's temperament.

### Three colours

Hovawarts come in three colours: **black**, **black and gold** (black with gold markings) and **blond**. All have a medium-long, slightly wavy coat with a light undercoat.

### Temperament and training

Hovawarts are loyal, confident and protective, affectionate with their family and naturally wary of strangers. They mature slowly, often not until 2–3 years. They're intelligent but independent, so they need consistent, fair training and early socialisation. They need plenty of exercise and mental work, and they excel at tracking, search and rescue, obedience and protection sports.

### Coat and health

The coat sheds seasonally and needs weekly brushing. Hovawarts are generally healthy, thanks partly to strict German breeding rules. Known problems include hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism and degenerative myelopathy. They often live 10–14 years.
