The Clumber Spaniel is the heaviest of the spaniels, named after Clumber Park, the Nottinghamshire estate of the Dukes of Newcastle, where it was developed in the 18th and 19th centuries. It was bred to work slowly and quietly through thick cover, flushing game birds for hunters on foot. Prince Albert and King Edward VII were among its royal fans. It's a calm, dignified and affectionate dog, and it's now rare.

### What a Clumber's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–20 in (45.5–51 cm) at the shoulder and 70–85 lb (32–38.5 kg) for males, and 17–19 in (43–48.5 cm) and 55–70 lb (25–32 kg) for females. A Clumber reaches roughly an adult's knee, but it's long, low and very heavily built, which puts it in the large size class on this site. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=clumber-spaniel) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male.

### Clumber vs Labrador

A [Labrador Retriever](/compare/clumber-spaniel-vs-labrador-retriever/) is much taller but about the same weight. The Clumber is long and low, with a massive head and heavy bone, and it's always white with lemon or orange markings.

### Temperament and exercise

Clumbers are gentle, easy-going and affectionate, usually good with children and other dogs. They need daily walks and enjoy retrieving and sniffing, but they're less energetic than most spaniels. They can be stubborn. Expect drool and shedding.

### Care and health

The dense, straight coat sheds a lot and needs brushing a few times a week. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, eyelid problems (entropion and ectropion), back problems, and pyruvate dehydrogenase phosphatase deficiency (PDP1), for which a DNA test exists. Keep your Clumber lean. Clumbers often live 10–12 years.
