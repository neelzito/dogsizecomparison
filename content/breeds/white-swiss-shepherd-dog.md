The White Swiss Shepherd Dog (*Berger Blanc Suisse*) descends from white German Shepherd Dogs. White coats appeared in early German Shepherd lines, but from the 1930s German breeders excluded white dogs from the breed. In North America, however, white Shepherds stayed popular and were bred as "American-Canadian White Shepherds". In the 1970s, some were imported to Switzerland, where breeders developed them into a separate breed. It was fully recognised by the FCI in 2011, with Switzerland as its country of origin. Today it's a gentle, intelligent and versatile family and working dog.

### What a White Swiss Shepherd's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 60–66 cm at the shoulder and about 30–40 kg for males, and 55–61 cm and about 25–35 kg for females. A White Swiss Shepherd reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's well-muscled and elegant, a little longer than tall. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=white-swiss-shepherd-dog).

### White Swiss Shepherd vs German Shepherd

The White Swiss Shepherd is [about the same size as a German Shepherd](/compare/german-shepherd-dog-vs-white-swiss-shepherd-dog/), but it's always pure white, and its back is straighter rather than sloping. Most White Swiss Shepherds are also a little softer and calmer in temperament than working-line German Shepherds. The French [Briard](/compare/briard-vs-white-swiss-shepherd-dog/) is a similar size, with a long, shaggy coat.

### Temperament and exercise

White Swiss Shepherds are friendly, affectionate and eager to please, devoted to their family and usually good with children and other dogs. They're alert but not aggressive, and sometimes reserved with strangers. They're intelligent and easy to train, and they do well in obedience, agility, tracking and as therapy dogs. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they don't like being left alone for long.

### Coat and health

White Swiss Shepherds come with a medium-length (stock) coat or a long coat. Both have a dense undercoat that sheds heavily, especially seasonally, so expect white hair everywhere and brush several times a week. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, degenerative myelopathy and the MDR1 drug sensitivity, for which DNA tests exist. White Swiss Shepherds often live 12–14 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 347 (Berger Blanc Suisse); Swiss Kennel Club (SKG); United Kennel Club breed standard.*
