The Pumi is a Hungarian herding dog, developed in the 17th and 18th centuries by crossing the Puli with terrier-type dogs from France and Germany. It worked cattle, sheep and pigs on Hungarian farms, and also caught rats. It's known for its curly coat, its whimsical expression and its upright ears with tipped-over ends. The AKC recognised it in 2016.

### What a Pumi's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 16–18.5 in (40.5–47 cm) at the shoulder for males and 15–17.5 in (38–44.5 cm) for females, with weights of about 12–13 kg (27–29 lb) for males and 10–11 kg for females. A Pumi reaches just below an adult's knee. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=pumi). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Pumi vs Mudi, Puli and Icelandic Sheepdog

The [Mudi](/compare/mudi-vs-pumi/) is a little shorter and lighter. The Puli has a corded coat. The [Icelandic Sheepdog](/compare/icelandic-sheepdog-vs-pumi/) is a little taller and heavier.

### Temperament and exercise

Pumik are lively, clever and vocal, devoted to their family and wary of strangers. They need a lot of exercise and mental work, and they excel at herding and agility.

### Coat and health

The curly coat sheds little and needs combing and trimming every few weeks. It's never brushed out. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and degenerative myelopathy. Pumik often live 12–13 years.
