The Canaan Dog is the national dog of Israel. Its ancestors were semi-wild pariah dogs that lived in the deserts of the Middle East for thousands of years. In the 1930s, Dr Rudolphina Menzel, a dog expert working with the Haganah defence force, captured and bred some of these dogs to create a hardy, intelligent working dog. Canaan Dogs served as guard, messenger and mine-detection dogs. Today Bedouin people still keep similar dogs to guard their camps and herds.

### What a Canaan Dog's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 20–24 in (51–61 cm) at the shoulder and 45–55 lb (20.5–25 kg) for males, and 19–23 in (48.5–58.5 cm) and 35–45 lb (16–20.5 kg) for females. A Canaan Dog reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's lean, square and agile. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=canaan-dog) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Canaan Dog vs Bearded Collie

The [Bearded Collie](/compare/bearded-collie-vs-canaan-dog/) is about the same size but has a long, shaggy coat. The Canaan Dog has a short-to-medium double coat, pricked ears and a bushy tail that curls over its back when it's alert.

### Temperament and exercise

Canaan Dogs are alert, loyal and devoted to their family, but naturally wary of strangers and quick to bark at anything unusual. They're intelligent and independent, a legacy of surviving on their own, so they need patient, consistent training and early socialisation. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and some dig.

### Care and health

The double coat sheds heavily once or twice a year but otherwise needs little care. Canaan Dogs are generally hardy and healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism and epilepsy. They often live 12–15 years.
