The Polish Lowland Sheepdog (*Polski Owczarek Nizinny*, or "PON") is a herding dog from the plains of Poland, used for centuries to herd and guard sheep. It's thought to be an ancestor of the Bearded Collie. The breed nearly vanished during the Second World War, and Dr Danuta Hryniewicz rebuilt it from a few surviving dogs, especially a male called Smok. Its long, shaggy coat covers its eyes.

### What a PON's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–20 in (45.5–51 cm) at the shoulder for males and 17–19 in (43–48 cm) for females. PONs weigh about 13.5–22.5 kg (30–50 lb). A PON reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's compact, muscular and a little longer than tall. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=polish-lowland-sheepdog). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### PON vs Spanish Water Dog and Bearded Collie

The [Spanish Water Dog](/compare/polish-lowland-sheepdog-vs-spanish-water-dog/) is about the same size, with a curly coat. The Bearded Collie, its likely descendant, is taller.

### Temperament and exercise

PONs are clever, confident and loyal, devoted to their family and wary of strangers. They have a good memory and can be strong-willed, so they need consistent training. They need daily exercise and mental work.

### Coat and health

The long, shaggy double coat needs thorough brushing a few times a week to prevent mats. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, eye conditions and hypothyroidism. PONs often live 12–14 years.
