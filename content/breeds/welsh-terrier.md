The Welsh Terrier is one of the oldest terrier breeds, from the hills of Wales, where it was used to hunt foxes, badgers and otters, often running with packs of hounds. It's black and tan, square and wiry, and looks like a small Airedale Terrier. President John F. Kennedy's family had a Welsh Terrier called Charlie at the White House.

### What a Welsh Terrier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 15 in (38 cm) at the shoulder and about 20 lb (9 kg) for males, with females a little smaller. A Welsh Terrier reaches just below an adult's knee. It's square, sturdy and athletic. It usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=welsh-terrier). It's too heavy for most airline cabins.

### Welsh Terrier vs Airedale and Bedlington

The Airedale is much bigger, with the same colouring. The Lakeland Terrier is a little smaller and comes in more colours. The [Bedlington Terrier](/compare/bedlington-terrier-vs-welsh-terrier/) is a little taller and about the same weight, with a lamb-like curly coat.

### Temperament and exercise

Welsh Terriers are friendly, spirited and confident, usually less fiery than some terriers but still independent. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and they love to dig and chase. They're clever but can be stubborn.

### Coat and health

The wiry coat sheds little and is traditionally hand-stripped a few times a year, or clipped for pets. Welsh Terriers are generally healthy. Known problems include eye conditions such as lens luxation and glaucoma, and allergies. They often live 12–15 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 78 (Welsh Terrier); Welsh Terrier Club of America.*
