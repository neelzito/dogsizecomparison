Despite its name, the American Eskimo Dog has nothing to do with the Arctic. It descends from white German Spitz dogs brought to the US by German immigrants. During and after the First World War, anti-German feeling led owners to rename the breed the "American Eskimo". In the early 20th century, "Eskies" were stars of travelling circuses, where their intelligence and striking white coats made them crowd favourites. Today the Eskie is a bright, lively and affectionate companion.

### Three sizes

The AKC recognises three sizes, measured at the shoulder:

- **Toy:** 9–12 in (23–30.5 cm)
- **Miniature:** over 12 in up to 15 in (30.5–38 cm)
- **Standard:** over 15 in up to 19 in (38–48.5 cm)

This page shows the Standard size, which weighs about 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb) and reaches just below an adult's knee. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=american-eskimo-dog) suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate for an average Standard Eskie. Toys and Miniatures need smaller crates, and many Toys fit an airline cabin carrier.

### Eskie vs Finnish Spitz and German Spitz

The [Finnish Spitz](/compare/american-eskimo-dog-vs-finnish-spitz/) is a little taller and similar in weight, with a fox-red coat. The Eskie is closely related to the German Spitz and the Japanese Spitz, which look very similar but are recognised separately by different kennel clubs.

### Temperament

Eskies are clever, eager to please and quick to learn, and they love to show off tricks. They're affectionate with their family and alert watchdogs that bark at anything unusual. They can be reserved with strangers. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and they don't like being left alone for long.

### Coat and health

The thick white double coat sheds a lot, with heavy moults once or twice a year. It needs brushing several times a week, more during moults. Surprisingly, the coat tends to shed dirt well. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, loose kneecaps, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), for which a DNA test exists, and diabetes. Eskies often live 13–15 years.
