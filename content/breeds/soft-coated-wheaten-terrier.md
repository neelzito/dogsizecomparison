The Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier is an Irish farm dog with a history going back centuries. Poor farmers kept it as an all-rounder: it hunted rats and badgers, guarded the farm and even helped herd livestock. It was recognised by the Irish Kennel Club in 1937. Its most distinctive feature is its soft, silky, wheat-coloured coat, unusual among terriers, which usually have a harsh coat. Today the Wheaten is a cheerful, affectionate family dog.

### What a Wheaten's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–19 in (45.5–48.5 cm) at the shoulder and 35–40 lb (16–18 kg) for males, and 17–18 in (43–45.5 cm) and 30–35 lb (13.5–16 kg) for females. A Wheaten reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's square, medium-sized and athletic. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=soft-coated-wheaten-terrier). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Wheaten vs Kerry Blue

The [Kerry Blue Terrier](/compare/kerry-blue-terrier-vs-soft-coated-wheaten-terrier/), another Irish terrier, is about the same size, but its soft, wavy coat is blue-grey. Both are thought to share ancestry with the Irish Terrier.

### The coat and the colour change

Wheaten puppies are born dark, reddish-brown or mahogany, and their coat gradually lightens to wheaten over the first two years or so. The adult coat is soft and gently waving, and it sheds very little. But it mats easily, so it needs brushing down to the skin several times a week and a trim every 6–8 weeks.

### Temperament

Wheatens are friendly, playful and full of energy, and they're known for their exuberant greeting, the "Wheaten greetin'", which often involves jumping up. They're usually good with children and other dogs, but they have a terrier's urge to chase small animals. They're clever but can be stubborn, so training should be consistent and fun. They need daily exercise and like to be involved in family life.

### Health

The breed is prone to two protein-losing diseases, protein-losing nephropathy (PLN), affecting the kidneys, and protein-losing enteropathy (PLE), affecting the gut. A DNA test can identify dogs at risk of PLN, and regular screening is recommended. Addison's disease, kidney dysplasia and hip dysplasia also occur. Wheatens often live 12–14 years.
