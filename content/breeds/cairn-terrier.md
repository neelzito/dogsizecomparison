The Cairn Terrier is one of the oldest Scottish terriers, from the Highlands and the Isle of Skye. Farmers used these small, tough dogs to hunt foxes, otters and vermin that hid in cairns, the piles of stones that mark fields and boundaries in Scotland. The breed's most famous dog is Terry, who played Toto in *The Wizard of Oz* (1939). Today the Cairn is a cheerful, bold and affectionate companion that still has plenty of terrier spirit.

### What a Cairn's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 10 in (25.5 cm) and 14 lb (6.4 kg) for males, and 9.5 in (24 cm) and 13 lb (5.9 kg) for females. A Cairn reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's compact and sturdy with short legs, and it suits flats and small homes. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=cairn-terrier).

### Flying with a Cairn

A Cairn Terrier usually fits the cabin. With a light carrier, it stays within the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dog and carrier. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=cairn-terrier).

### Cairn vs Westie and Norwich

The Cairn and the [West Highland White Terrier](/compare/west-highland-white-terrier-vs-cairn-terrier/) are close relatives of similar height. The Westie is a little heavier and always white, while the Cairn has a shaggier coat in shades of wheaten, red, grey or brindle. The [Norwich Terrier](/compare/cairn-terrier-vs-norwich-terrier/) is about the same height and a little lighter, with prick ears.

### Temperament and exercise

Cairns are curious, lively and independent. They love to dig, explore and chase, so a secure garden is a must, and recall can be unreliable near small animals. They're affectionate with their family and usually good with children. They need daily walks and play, and they're clever enough to learn quickly with reward-based training, though they can be stubborn.

### Coat and health

The harsh, weatherproof double coat sheds little and needs weekly brushing. It is traditionally hand-stripped rather than clipped, to keep its texture. Known health problems include loose kneecaps, eye conditions, liver shunts and a few rare inherited diseases. Cairns are hardy and long-lived, often 13–15 years.
