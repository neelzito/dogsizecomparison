The Xoloitzcuintli (pronounced "show-low-eets-QUEENT-lee"), or "Xolo", is the hairless dog of Mexico, with a history going back more than 3,000 years. The Aztecs linked it to Xolotl, the god of lightning and death, and believed it guided souls through the underworld. Xolos were also kept as companions and bed warmers, as their bare skin feels warm to the touch. Today the Xolo is Mexico's national dog, and one starred as Dante in the Pixar film *Coco* (2017).

### Three sizes, two coats

The AKC recognises three sizes, measured at the shoulder:

- **Toy:** 10–14 in (25.5–35.5 cm)
- **Miniature:** over 14 in up to 18 in (35.5–45.5 cm)
- **Standard:** over 18 in up to 23 in (45.5–58.5 cm)

This page shows the Standard Xolo, which weighs about 13.5–25 kg (30–55 lb) and reaches roughly an adult's knee. Xolos come in a **hairless** variety and a **coated** variety with a short, flat coat. Both can be born in the same litter. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=xoloitzcuintli-standard) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average Standard Xolo.

### Xolo vs Peruvian Hairless and Chinese Crested

The Xolo is one of several hairless breeds. The Peruvian Inca Orchid is a similar lean, hairless dog from South America. The Chinese Crested is much smaller and has silky hair on its head, feet and tail. Xolos are lean, elegant and athletic, a little like a hairless sighthound.

### Caring for bare skin

Hairless Xolos need sunscreen in strong sun, a coat or jumper in cold weather, and regular baths and moisturising to keep their skin healthy. Young Xolos often get acne, which usually clears with age. Coated Xolos need only occasional brushing. Hairless Xolos often have missing teeth, which is linked to the hairless gene.

### Temperament and health

Xolos are calm, loyal and attentive, often bonding closely with their family and wary of strangers, which makes them good watchdogs. They're intelligent and fairly easy to train, but they need daily exercise and company. The breed is generally healthy and long-lived, often 13–18 years. Known problems include skin conditions and dental issues in hairless dogs.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 234 (Perro sin pelo mexicano / Xoloitzcuintle); Xoloitzcuintli Club of America.*
