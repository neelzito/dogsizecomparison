The Basenji is an ancient hunting dog from Central Africa, where people in the Congo basin used it to drive small game into nets. Genetic studies place it among the breeds closest to the earliest domestic dogs. European travellers brought Basenjis to Britain in the 1930s. The breed is famous as the "barkless dog": because of the shape of its larynx, it doesn't bark, but it yodels, howls and makes a sound often called a "baroo".

### What a Basenji's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 17 in (43 cm) and 24 lb (11 kg) for males, and 16 in (40.5 cm) and 22 lb (10 kg) for females. A Basenji reaches just below an adult's knee. It's light, elegant and square, with a curled tail and a wrinkled forehead. It suits flats and houses, as long as it gets enough exercise, and the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=basenji) suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate because of its leg length. A Basenji is too heavy for most airline cabins.

### Basenji vs Cirneco and Podengo

The Basenji shares its "primitive" look with other ancient hunting breeds. The [Cirneco dell'Etna](/compare/basenji-vs-cirneco-dell-etna/) from Sicily is a little taller and similar in weight, with large upright ears. The [Portuguese Podengo (Medium)](/compare/portuguese-podengo-medium-vs-basenji/) is taller and heavier.

### Cat-like and independent

Basenjis are often described as cat-like: they're clean, groom themselves, dislike water and can be aloof with strangers. They're clever but independent, and training takes patience and creativity. Their prey drive is strong, so recall is often unreliable and small pets may not be safe. They're also skilled escape artists and climbers. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and they can be destructive if bored.

### Care and health

The short, fine coat sheds very little and has almost no doggy smell. Basenjis feel the cold. Like wild dogs, females usually come into season only once a year. Known health problems include Fanconi syndrome, a kidney disease for which a DNA test exists, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and hypothyroidism. Basenjis often live 13–14 years.
