The Sealyham Terrier was developed in the mid-19th century by Captain John Edwardes at Sealyham House in Pembrokeshire, Wales. He wanted a white, short-legged, fearless terrier to hunt otters, badgers and foxes. The breed was very fashionable in the early 20th century, with owners including Alfred Hitchcock, who walked his two Sealyhams in his cameo in *The Birds* (1963). Today it's rare, and The Kennel Club lists it as a vulnerable native breed.

### What a Sealyham's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 10.5 in (26.5 cm) at the shoulder and about 23–24 lb (10.5–11 kg) for males, with females slightly lighter. A Sealyham reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's low, sturdy and heavy for its size. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=sealyham-terrier). It's too heavy for the airline cabin.

### Sealyham vs Scottish Terrier and Mini Bull Terrier

The [Scottish Terrier](/compare/scottish-terrier-vs-sealyham-terrier/) is about the same height and a little lighter, usually black. The [Miniature Bull Terrier](/compare/miniature-bull-terrier-vs-sealyham-terrier/) is a little taller and about the same weight.

### Temperament and exercise

Sealyhams are calmer than many terriers but still brave, independent and alert. They're devoted to their family and good watchdogs. They need daily walks and play, and they can be stubborn.

### Coat and health

The wiry white coat needs regular brushing and hand-stripping or clipping. Known health problems include eye diseases (lens luxation, retinal dysplasia), deafness and back problems. Sealyhams often live 12–14 years.
