The Italian Greyhound is a miniature sighthound kept as a companion for more than two thousand years. Small greyhound-type dogs appear in ancient Mediterranean art, and in Renaissance Italy they were favourites of nobles and royalty. Famous owners included Catherine the Great, Frederick the Great of Prussia and Queen Victoria. Despite its tiny size, it is a true sighthound: fast, graceful and built to chase.

### What an Italian Greyhound's size means at home

An Italian Greyhound stands about 33–38 cm at the shoulder, just below an adult's knee, but it weighs only about 3–6.5 kg (7–14 lb), very light for its height. It's slim and long-legged, so the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=italian-greyhound) suggests a 30″ (76 cm) crate because of its leg length. It suits flats and small homes, and loves a soft sofa and a blanket.

### Flying with an Italian Greyhound

An Italian Greyhound usually fits in the cabin. Even with a carrier, it stays under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set. Check carrier sizes with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=italian-greyhound), and look for a carrier tall enough for its long legs.

### Italian Greyhound vs Whippet

The [Whippet](/compare/whippet-vs-italian-greyhound/) is the next size up: about 15 cm taller and roughly three times as heavy. The Greyhound is much bigger still. All three share the same shape, gentle nature and need to sprint. The [Chinese Crested](/compare/chinese-crested-vs-italian-greyhound/) is a little smaller, and a very different, mostly hairless dog.

### Fragile legs

Italian Greyhounds' fine leg bones break easily, especially in young dogs between about 4 and 18 months old. Common causes are jumping off furniture or from someone's arms, or landing badly when running. Use ramps or pet steps, discourage big jumps, and teach children to sit down when holding one.

### Living with an Italian Greyhound

Italian Greyhounds are affectionate, sensitive and playful, and very attached to their people. They need a daily chance to run in a safe, enclosed area, but they also sleep a lot. They feel the cold, so a coat or jumper is essential in winter. Many hate rain, which can make house-training slow. Dental disease is common, so brush their teeth regularly. They often live 14–15 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 200 (Piccolo Levriero Italiano); The Kennel Club breed standard.*
