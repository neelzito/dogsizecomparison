The Boykin Spaniel is a small American gundog from South Carolina, developed in the early 20th century to hunt wild turkeys and ducks along the Wateree River. According to tradition, the breed began with a small brown stray spaniel found near a church in Spartanburg, later bred by Whit Boykin. Hunters needed a dog small enough to fit in a narrow boat. It's the state dog of South Carolina, and the AKC recognised it in 2009.

### What a Boykin's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 15.5–18 in (39.5–45.5 cm) at the shoulder and 30–40 lb (13.5–18 kg) for males, and 14–16.5 in (35.5–42 cm) and 25–35 lb (11.5–16 kg) for females. A Boykin reaches just below an adult's knee. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=boykin-spaniel). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Boykin vs American Water Spaniel and English Cocker

The [American Water Spaniel](/compare/american-water-spaniel-vs-boykin-spaniel/) is about the same size and a little heavier, with tighter curls. The [English Cocker Spaniel](/compare/boykin-spaniel-vs-english-cocker-spaniel/) is about the same height and a little lighter. Boykins are always solid liver or brown.

### Temperament and exercise

Boykins are friendly, eager and energetic, great with children and other dogs. They need plenty of daily exercise and love swimming and retrieving. They're keen to please and easy to train.

### Coat and health

The medium-length wavy coat needs weekly brushing, and the ears need regular checks. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, eye conditions, and exercise-induced collapse, for which a DNA test exists. Boykins often live 10–15 years.
