The Miniature Dachshund is the small version of the German "badger dog". Smaller Dachshunds were bred from the 19th century onwards to go after smaller quarry, such as rabbits and stoats, in narrower burrows. Today the Miniature Dachshund is a hugely popular companion: brave, loyal, playful and full of character. Like the Standard Dachshund, it comes in three coats: smooth, wire-haired and long-haired.

### How small is a Miniature Dachshund?

Sizing depends on the kennel club:

- **AKC (US):** a Miniature weighs 11 lb (5 kg) or less at 12 months or older.
- **The Kennel Club (UK):** an ideal weight of about 4.5 kg (10 lb).
- **FCI (Europe):** sized by chest circumference instead. A Miniature (*Zwergteckel*) measures 30–35 cm around the chest, and the even smaller Rabbit Dachshund (*Kaninchenteckel*) up to 30 cm.

A Miniature typically stands only about 13–15 cm at the shoulder, around ankle height on an adult. Next to a [Standard Dachshund](/compare/dachshund-miniature-vs-dachshund-standard/), it is about 8 cm shorter and well under half the weight.

### What a Miniature Dachshund's size means at home

A Miniature Dachshund fits any home and usually needs an 18″ (46 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=dachshund-miniature). Because it's long rather than tall, a carrier needs enough length for it to lie stretched out.

### Flying with a Miniature Dachshund

Most Miniature Dachshunds fit in the cabin, staying under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set even with a carrier. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=dachshund-miniature).

### Protect the back

Miniature Dachshunds share the breed's high risk of intervertebral disc disease (IVDD), where a disc in the spine bulges or bursts. To lower the risk, keep your dog lean, use ramps or steps instead of letting it jump on and off furniture, carry it on stairs where possible, and support its back and bottom when you lift it. If it suddenly cries out, refuses to move, or wobbles or drags its back legs, see a vet straight away.

### Temperament

Miniature Dachshunds are bold, curious and often stubborn, with a big bark for their size. They need patient, reward-based training and daily walks to sniff and explore. Some dig and chase small animals. They often live 12–16 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard; The Kennel Club breed standard; FCI standard No. 148 (Dachshund); Dachshund Breed Council (UK).*
