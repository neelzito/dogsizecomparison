The Redbone Coonhound is an American scent hound bred in the southern states to hunt raccoons and, sometimes, larger game. It descends from red foxhounds brought to America by Scottish and Irish immigrants. It's known for its solid red coat, its sweet, pleading expression and its melodious bay. It was made famous by Wilson Rawls's novel *Where the Red Fern Grows* (1961), about two Redbones.

### What a Redbone's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 22–27 in (56–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 21–26 in (53.5–66 cm) for females. Redbones weigh about 20.5–32 kg (45–70 lb). A Redbone reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean, athletic and fast. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. ### Redbone vs Treeing Walker and Bluetick

The [Treeing Walker Coonhound](/compare/redbone-coonhound-vs-treeing-walker-coonhound/) is about the same size, tricoloured. The Bluetick is similar in height but heavier.

### Temperament and exercise

Redbones are easy-going and affectionate at home, and determined hunters outdoors. They need plenty of daily exercise, and their nose leads them, so recall is unreliable. They bay loudly and love food.

### Care and health

The short, smooth coat needs only weekly brushing, and the ears need regular cleaning. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and ear infections. Redbones often live 12–15 years.
