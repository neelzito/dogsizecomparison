The Irish Wolfhound is the tallest dog breed on this site. Giant hounds were prized in ancient Ireland for hunting wolves, deer and boar, and for war, and were given as gifts to kings across Europe. When the last wolves in Ireland were killed in the late 18th century, the breed almost died out. In the 19th century, Captain George Augustus Graham, a Scottish army officer, rebuilt it from the few remaining dogs, crossed with Scottish Deerhounds, Great Danes and other breeds. Its old motto sums up its nature: "gentle when stroked, fierce when provoked".

### What a Wolfhound's size means at home

The AKC standard sets minimums of 32 in (81 cm) and 120 lb (54.5 kg) for males, and 30 in (76 cm) and 105 lb (47.5 kg) for females, and calls for great size. Most males stand about 81–90 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's hip, and their head is easily level with a kitchen worktop. Standing on its hind legs, a Wolfhound is taller than most people. An average male is too tall for standard crates, as the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=irish-wolfhound) shows, so it needs an extra-large giant crate or a custom-built one. It also needs a van-sized vehicle and a lot of floor space. A wagging Wolfhound tail clears coffee tables.

### Wolfhound vs Great Dane and Scottish Deerhound

The Wolfhound is [taller than the Great Dane](/compare/great-dane-vs-irish-wolfhound/) on average, but the Dane is usually heavier. The [Scottish Deerhound](/compare/irish-wolfhound-vs-scottish-deerhound/) looks like a lighter, slimmer Wolfhound: a little shorter and much lighter. All three are gentle, quiet giants.

### Temperament and care

Wolfhounds are calm, friendly and dignified, and usually good with children and other dogs. They're too friendly to make good guard dogs. Like all sighthounds, they love to run and can chase, so they need a secure area. The wiry, rough coat needs only weekly brushing and occasional hand-stripping.

### Growth and a short life

Wolfhound puppies grow extremely fast, often gaining several kilos a month. Feed a giant-breed puppy food, keep them lean and avoid forced exercise or jumping until their growth plates close. Sadly, the Wolfhound has one of the shortest life spans of any breed, usually only 6–8 years. Heart disease (dilated cardiomyopathy), bone cancer and bloat are the main health risks. Ask breeders about heart screening and how long the parents' relatives lived.
