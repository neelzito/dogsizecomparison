The Akita Inu is a large Japanese spitz breed from the mountains of Akita Prefecture in northern Japan, where it was used to hunt bear, boar and deer. It was declared a natural monument of Japan in 1931. The breed's most famous dog is Hachikō, who waited at Shibuya Station in Tokyo for his owner every day for almost ten years after the owner died. His statue still stands there. In Japan the Akita symbolises loyalty, good health and happiness.

### Japanese Akita or American Akita?

After the Second World War, Akitas taken to the US were bred towards a bigger, heavier type with more varied colours and a bear-like head. In Japan, breeders went back to the original, more fox-like type. Most kennel clubs now treat them as two breeds:

- **Akita Inu (Japanese Akita):** lighter and more fox-like, in red, white, sesame or brindle, always with white markings (*urajiro*). This page shows this type.
- **[American Akita](/compare/akita-inu-japanese-vs-akita-american/):** about the same height but much heavier and broader, in almost any colour, often with a black mask.

### What an Akita's size means at home

A male Japanese Akita stands about 64–70 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 30–34 kg (65–75 lb). Females are about 57–64 cm and 25–30 kg. It needs space, a big boot and, because of its height and deep chest, a large crate: the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=akita-inu-japanese) suggests 54″ (137 cm) for an average male. Next to a [Shiba Inu](/compare/shiba-inu-vs-akita-inu-japanese/), its small cousin, an Akita is about 25 cm taller and three times as heavy.

### Temperament

Akitas are loyal, dignified and calm at home, devoted to their family but reserved, even aloof, with strangers. They're naturally protective and often don't tolerate other dogs, especially of the same sex. An Akita needs early socialisation, a confident, consistent owner and a secure garden. It isn't the best choice for a first dog.

### Coat and health

The thick double coat sheds heavily, with one or two big moults a year. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, eye diseases, hypothyroidism, and sebaceous adenitis, an inflammatory skin disease. Akitas often live 10–12 years.
