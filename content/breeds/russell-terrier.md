The Russell Terrier is the AKC's name for the short-legged Russell-type terrier, which much of the world, including the FCI and The Kennel Club, calls the Jack Russell Terrier. It descends from the fox-hunting terriers of the Reverend John Russell in 19th-century Devon, and was refined in Australia as a smaller, slightly longer-bodied type. The AKC recognised it in 2012.

### What a Russell Terrier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 10–12 in (25.5–30.5 cm) at the shoulder, and Russell Terriers weigh about 4–7 kg (9–15 lb). One reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. ### Russell Terrier vs Jack Russell and Parson Russell

The [Jack Russell Terrier](/compare/jack-russell-terrier-vs-russell-terrier/) (FCI) is essentially the same dog. The Parson Russell Terrier is the taller, square type.

### Temperament and exercise

Russell Terriers are bold, energetic and clever, real working terriers. They need a lot of exercise, love to dig and chase, and need a secure garden. Bored Russells can be noisy and destructive.

### Coat and health

The smooth, broken or rough coat needs weekly brushing. Known health problems include lens luxation, deafness and loose kneecaps. Russell Terriers often live 12–14 years.
