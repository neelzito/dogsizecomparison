The Treeing Walker Coonhound descends from the Walker foxhounds developed in Kentucky by John W. Walker and George Washington Maupin in the 19th century, crossed with a fast hound called Tennessee Lead. Hunters used it to track raccoons and "tree" them, baying at the base of the tree. It's fast, athletic and tricoloured, and it's sometimes called "the people's choice" among coon hunters. The AKC recognised it in 2012.

### What a Treeing Walker's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 22–27 in (56–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 20–25 in (51–63.5 cm) for females. Treeing Walkers typically weigh about 22.5–32 kg (50–70 lb). A Treeing Walker reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean and fast. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=treeing-walker-coonhound).

### Treeing Walker vs Bluetick and Redbone

The [Bluetick Coonhound](/compare/bluetick-coonhound-vs-treeing-walker-coonhound/) is about the same height but heavier and slower. The [Redbone](/compare/redbone-coonhound-vs-treeing-walker-coonhound/) is about the same size, solid red.

### Temperament and exercise

Treeing Walkers are friendly, easy-going and affectionate, usually good with children and other dogs. They need a lot of exercise, and their nose leads them, so recall is unreliable. They have a loud, ringing bay.

### Care and health

The short coat needs only weekly brushing, and the ears need regular cleaning. Treeing Walkers are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia. They often live 12–13 years.
