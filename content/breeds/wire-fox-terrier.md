The Wire Fox Terrier was developed in England in the 19th century to go to ground and bolt foxes for mounted fox hunts. It had to be small enough to follow a fox into its den, fast enough to keep up with the hounds, and mostly white so it wasn't mistaken for the fox. The Wire and Smooth Fox Terriers were shown as one breed for years before being separated. The Wire Fox Terrier has won Best in Show at Westminster more times than any other breed, and Snowy, Tintin's companion, is one.

### What a Wire Fox Terrier's size means at home

The AKC standard says males should not exceed 15.5 in (39.5 cm) at the shoulder, and calls for about 18 lb (8 kg) for males and 15–17 lb (7–7.5 kg) for females. A Wire reaches just below an adult's knee. It's square, leggy and athletic. It suits most homes and usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=wire-fox-terrier). Most adults are over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit for dog and carrier on many airlines.

### Wire vs Smooth Fox Terrier

The [Smooth Fox Terrier](/compare/smooth-fox-terrier-vs-wire-fox-terrier/) is the same size and build, with a short, flat coat instead of the Wire's dense, wiry one.

### Temperament and exercise

Wire Fox Terriers are lively, bold, curious and full of energy, real terriers in every sense. They love to dig, chase and explore, so they need a secure garden, and recall can be unreliable around small animals. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental stimulation, or they'll find their own entertainment. They can be noisy and may not tolerate other dogs, so early socialisation matters. With the right owner, they're funny, affectionate and loyal.

### Coat care

The wiry coat sheds very little. Show dogs are hand-stripped to keep the coat harsh and the colours crisp. Pet dogs are usually clipped every 6–8 weeks, which softens the coat. The beard and leg furnishings need regular brushing.

### Health

Wire Fox Terriers are generally healthy. Known problems include eye conditions such as lens luxation (a displaced lens in the eye), deafness, loose kneecaps, skin allergies and Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (a hip condition). They often live 12–15 years.
