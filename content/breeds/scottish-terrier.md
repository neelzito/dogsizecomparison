The Scottish Terrier, or "Scottie", comes from the Scottish Highlands, where it hunted foxes, badgers and vermin among rocks and in burrows. Short legs, a powerful build and a bold nature suited the job. The breed's nickname, "the Diehard", reflects its determination. Scotties became fashionable in the 20th century: President Franklin D. Roosevelt's Scottie, Fala, was famous across the US, and a Scottie has long been one of the playing pieces in Monopoly.

### What a Scottie's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 10 in (25.5 cm) at the shoulder, and 19–22 lb (8.5–10 kg) for males and 18–21 lb (8–9.5 kg) for females. A Scottie reaches around mid-shin on an adult, but it's heavy for its height: low, strong and solid. It suits flats and small homes and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=scottish-terrier). Most adult Scotties are over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit for dog and carrier on many airlines.

### Scottie vs Westie and other Scottish terriers

The Scottie is about [the same height as a West Highland White Terrier](/compare/west-highland-white-terrier-vs-scottish-terrier/) but heavier, with a long head, a big beard and usually a black coat (wheaten and brindle also occur). The [Dandie Dinmont](/compare/dandie-dinmont-terrier-vs-scottish-terrier/) and the [Sealyham](/compare/scottish-terrier-vs-sealyham-terrier/) are similar in size but very different in shape and coat.

### Temperament

Scotties are independent, dignified and confident, sometimes called "the most cat-like of terriers". They're loyal to their family but often reserved with strangers, and they make alert watchdogs. They can be stubborn and sensitive, so patient, reward-based training works best. Many don't tolerate other dogs well, and their prey drive is strong. They need daily walks and enjoy digging.

### Coat and health

The wiry, weather-resistant double coat sheds little. It needs regular brushing, and either hand-stripping or clipping every couple of months. The distinctive beard and eyebrows need trimming. Scotties have a much higher risk of bladder cancer than most breeds, and they also suffer from Scottie cramp (a movement disorder), von Willebrand's disease (a bleeding disorder) and craniomandibular osteopathy (a jaw bone disease). They typically live around 12 years.
