The Black Russian Terrier was created by the Soviet army in the 1940s and 50s at the Red Star Kennel near Moscow. After the Second World War, the Soviet Union needed a large, tough, trainable guard dog that could cope with the Russian climate. Breeders crossed the Giant Schnauzer, Airedale Terrier, Rottweiler, Newfoundland and other breeds. Despite its name, it's not really a terrier, but a large working and guard dog. The FCI recognised it in 1984 and the AKC in 2004.

### What a Black Russian's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 27–30 in (68.5–76 cm) at the shoulder for males and 26–29 in (66–73.5 cm) for females. Black Russians weigh about 36.5–59 kg (80–130 lb), heavy enough to put them in the giant size class on this site. A Black Russian reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and its thick black coat makes it look even bigger. It needs a large vehicle and, because of its height, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=black-russian-terrier) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

### Black Russian vs Komondor and Kuvasz

The Hungarian [Komondor](/compare/black-russian-terrier-vs-komondor/) is a little taller and heavier on average, with its unique white cords. The [Kuvasz](/compare/black-russian-terrier-vs-kuvasz/), another white Hungarian guardian, is about the same size. Compared with the Giant Schnauzer, one of its ancestors, the Black Russian is taller and more heavily built.

### Temperament and training

Black Russian Terriers are calm, confident and very loyal, often gentle with their own family and children, but naturally protective and wary of strangers. They're intelligent but can be strong-willed, so they need early socialisation and consistent, confident training. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they want to be close to their people. Left alone in a garden, they can become bored and destructive.

### Coat and health

The thick, wavy, weather-resistant coat sheds little but needs brushing at least twice a week and a trim every couple of months. The beard collects food and water and needs regular cleaning. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, eye conditions, and juvenile laryngeal paralysis with polyneuropathy, for which a DNA test exists. Black Russians often live 10–12 years.
