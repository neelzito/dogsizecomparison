The Czechoslovakian Wolfdog began as a military experiment. In 1955 the Czechoslovak army, under Karel Hartl, crossed German Shepherd Dogs with Carpathian wolves. They hoped to combine the wolf's strength, stamina and senses with the German Shepherd's trainability for border patrol work. The breed was officially recognised in Czechoslovakia in 1982 and by the FCI in 1999. With its wolf-like looks, amber eyes and grey coat, it is striking, but it's very demanding to live with.

### What a Czechoslovakian Wolfdog's size means at home

The FCI standard sets minimum heights: at least 65 cm for males and 60 cm for females, with minimum weights of 26 kg and 20 kg. Most males stand about 65–75 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean, long-legged and athletic, lighter than it looks for its height. Because of its height, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=czechoslovakian-wolfdog) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

### Wolfdog vs Malinois and German Shepherd

The Czechoslovakian Wolfdog is [taller than a Belgian Malinois](/compare/belgian-malinois-vs-czechoslovakian-wolfdog/) and similar in weight. It's leaner and longer-legged than a German Shepherd, with a narrower chest and a more wolf-like head and movement.

### Laws and restrictions

Because of its wolf ancestry, some countries, regions or cities treat the Czechoslovakian Wolfdog as a wolf hybrid, which may mean special permits, restrictions or outright bans. Check the current rules where you live, and where you plan to travel or move, before getting one.

### Not a dog for most homes

Czechoslovakian Wolfdogs are intelligent, loyal and very attached to their family, but they keep many wolf-like traits. They can be independent, wary or shy with strangers, highly reactive to movement and prey, and prone to howling rather than barking. They need a huge amount of exercise and mental stimulation, extensive socialisation from puppyhood, and patient, experienced handling. Many don't cope well alone and can become destructive, and they're skilled escape artists. They suit very experienced, active owners with secure, spacious properties.

### Care and health

The thick double coat sheds heavily, especially in spring and autumn. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, degenerative myelopathy and dwarfism (pituitary dwarfism), for which DNA tests exist. Czechoslovakian Wolfdogs often live 12–16 years.
