The French Bulldog is a small, muscular companion with bat ears, a flat face and a big personality. Its story starts in England: in the 19th century, lace workers from Nottingham brought small "toy" bulldogs with them when they moved to northern France for work. In France the little bulldogs became fashionable in Paris, developed their upright bat ears, and later returned to England and America as the French Bulldog. Today it is hugely popular. In 2022 it became the most registered breed in the United States, overtaking the Labrador Retriever.

### What a French Bulldog's size means at home

A Frenchie is short but heavy for its height. At about 30 cm at the shoulder it reaches roughly mid-shin on an adult, but it weighs 8–12.5 kg (18–28 lb), about as much as a [Shiba Inu](/breeds/shiba-inu/) that stands almost 10 cm taller. That compact build makes it a good flat and city dog: it doesn't need a big garden, and it fits on a sofa or in a small car. A 24″ (61 cm) crate usually fits. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=french-bulldog).

### Can a French Bulldog fly in the cabin?

Usually not in Europe. Most European airlines that allow dogs in the cabin cap dog and carrier together at 8 kg (17.6 lb), and an adult Frenchie alone is usually heavier than that. Some US airlines have no weight limit as long as the carrier fits under the seat. The hold is a problem too: because of their short noses, many airlines don't accept French Bulldogs and other snub-nosed (brachycephalic) breeds in the hold at all. Check your airline's rules before you book, and see our [guide to flying with a dog in the cabin](/articles/flying-with-a-dog-in-the-cabin/).

### Breathing, heat and exercise

The flat face that makes Frenchies so appealing also shortens their airways. Many French Bulldogs struggle to cool down, can overheat quickly in warm weather, and snore. They enjoy short walks and play, but long runs and hot days aren't for them. If you're choosing a puppy, look for parents with open nostrils that breathe quietly, and ask the breeder about breathing tests. Some kennel clubs now offer a breathing assessment for brachycephalic breeds.

### Size variations

The breed standard sets an upper weight limit: the AKC disqualifies dogs over 28 lb (12.7 kg). There is no official "mini" or "teacup" French Bulldog. Very small dogs sold under those names are simply undersized, and are often less healthy.
