The Bergamasco Sheepdog comes from the Alps around Bergamo in northern Italy, where it herded and guarded sheep for centuries. It's famous for its unique coat, which forms flat, felted mats called "flocks" that cover its body and protect it from weather and predators. The breed nearly vanished after the Second World War, when sheep farming declined, but Italian breeders saved it.

### What a Bergamasco's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 23.5 in (59.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22 in (56 cm) for females, with weights of about 32–38 kg (70–84 lb) for males and 26–32 kg (57–71 lb) for females. A Bergamasco reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's strong and solidly built. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bergamasco-sheepdog) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male.

### Bergamasco vs Catahoula and Komondor

The [Catahoula Leopard Dog](/compare/bergamasco-sheepdog-vs-catahoula-leopard-dog/) is about the same size, with a short coat. The Komondor also has a matted coat, but in cords, and it's much bigger.

### The flocks

The coat forms mats between about 1 and 2 years of age. The mats need separating by hand while they form, but after that the coat needs surprisingly little care and isn't brushed. It takes a long time to dry after bathing. The coat sheds very little.

### Temperament and health

Bergamascos are calm, patient and intelligent, devoted to their family and reserved with strangers. They need daily exercise. They're generally healthy and often live 13–15 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 194 (Cane da Pastore Bergamasco).*
