The Neapolitan Mastiff (*Mastino Napoletano*) descends from the massive molosser dogs of ancient Rome, used in war, in arenas and as guardians. For centuries, its ancestors guarded farms and estates around Naples in southern Italy. The breed was little known until the 1940s, when the Italian journalist Piero Scanziani saw them at a show in Naples and began a breeding programme. Its huge size and loose, wrinkled skin made it a natural choice to play Fang, Hagrid's dog, in the *Harry Potter* films.

### What a Neo's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 26–31 in (66–78.5 cm) at the shoulder and about 150 lb (68 kg) for males, and 24–29 in (61–73.5 cm) and about 110 lb (50 kg) for females. Many are heavier. A Neo reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. With its massive head, heavy bone and folds of loose skin, it looks even bigger than it is. It needs a large vehicle, a ramp, and a 54″ (137 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=neapolitan-mastiff). Expect a lot of drool.

### Neo vs Newfoundland and other giants

A Neapolitan Mastiff is [about the same height as a Newfoundland](/compare/neapolitan-mastiff-vs-newfoundland/) and similar in weight, but short-coated and much more wrinkled. The [Caucasian Shepherd](/compare/caucasian-shepherd-dog-vs-neapolitan-mastiff/) and the [Central Asian Shepherd](/compare/central-asian-shepherd-dog-vs-neapolitan-mastiff/) are similar in size, but they're thick-coated livestock guardians.

### Temperament

Neos are calm, devoted and gentle with their own family, and deeply protective. They tend to be suspicious of strangers. They're strong-willed and very powerful, so early socialisation and calm, consistent training from puppyhood are essential. They need moderate exercise rather than long runs, and they struggle in heat. A Neo isn't a breed for first-time owners or small homes.

### Skin, eyes and health

The heavy folds of skin need regular cleaning to prevent infections, and many Neos have eyelid problems such as cherry eye, entropion or ectropion. Other known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, heart disease and demodectic mange. Like many giant breeds, Neos have a short life, often only 7–9 years.
