The Berger Picard (say "bare-ZHAY pee-CAR") is one of France's oldest herding dogs, from the Picardy region in the north. It herded sheep and cattle for centuries, but both World Wars, fought across Picardy, nearly wiped it out. It's still rare, but it became better known after a Berger Picard starred in the film *Because of Winn-Dixie* (2005). It's known for its tousled coat, upright ears and big personality.

### What a Picard's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 23.5–25.5 in (60–65 cm) at the shoulder for males and 21.5–23.5 in (55–60 cm) for females. Picards weigh about 22.5–32 kg (50–70 lb). A Picard reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean, athletic and a little longer than tall. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=berger-picard).

### Picard vs Malinois and Dutch Shepherd

The [Belgian Malinois](/compare/belgian-malinois-vs-berger-picard/) is a little taller and heavier. The [Dutch Shepherd](/compare/berger-picard-vs-dutch-shepherd/) is a little shorter and about the same weight. The Picard's rough, shaggy coat in fawn or brindle makes it look more relaxed than either.

### Temperament and exercise

Berger Picards are loyal, lively and intelligent, with a good sense of humour. They're devoted to their family, often reserved with strangers, and can be stubborn and sensitive. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental work, and they enjoy herding, agility, tracking and long hikes. They're happiest as part of family life.

### Coat and health

The harsh, wiry coat sheds moderately and needs only occasional brushing. It shouldn't be trimmed into a neat shape. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and progressive retinal atrophy (PRA). Picards often live 12–13 years.
