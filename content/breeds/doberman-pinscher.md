The Doberman (Dobermann in Europe) was created in the 1890s by Karl Friedrich Louis Dobermann, a tax collector in Apolda, Thuringia, in Germany. He also ran the local dog pound. Collecting taxes in rough areas, he wanted a fast, alert and protective dog to accompany him. He probably crossed old German shepherd-type dogs, Rottweilers, pinschers and other breeds. The result was a sleek, athletic guard dog that later served as a police and military dog. Today's well-bred Doberman is loyal, sensitive and very attached to its family, much softer than its reputation suggests.

### What a Doberman's size means at home

A male Doberman stands about 66–71 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs 34–45 kg (75–100 lb). Females stand about 61–66 cm and weigh less. It is tall, muscular and elegant, with a short coat that needs very little grooming but offers little warmth, so a coat helps in cold weather. With its long legs and deep chest, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=doberman-pinscher) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

### Doberman vs Rottweiler and German Shepherd

The Doberman is the [tallest of these three German working breeds](/compare/rottweiler-vs-doberman-pinscher/), but it's leaner than the Rottweiler, which is shorter and much heavier. Next to a [German Shepherd](/compare/doberman-pinscher-vs-german-shepherd-dog/), the Doberman is a few centimetres taller, squarer and short-coated.

### Ears and tail

Dobermans are often pictured with cropped ears and a docked tail. Both are banned in the UK, much of Europe and Australia, so there you'll see Dobermans with their natural floppy ears and long tail. In the US, cropping and docking are still common but increasingly questioned.

### Training and exercise

Dobermans are intelligent, quick to learn and eager to work with their owners. They need daily exercise and mental challenges, and they do well in obedience, tracking and dog sports. They're sensitive, so calm, consistent, reward-based training works best. Good socialisation from puppyhood helps them become confident rather than suspicious.

### Health: the heart

The Doberman's biggest health risk is dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), a disease of the heart muscle that can cause sudden death without warning. A large European study found DCM in 58% of Dobermans that were followed into old age. Annual screening with a 24-hour ECG (Holter) and a heart ultrasound from about age two can catch it early. If you're buying a puppy, ask about the parents' heart screening results. Von Willebrand's disease, a bleeding disorder, is also found in the breed, and a DNA test exists for it.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 143 (Dobermann); Wess et al., Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine (2010) on DCM prevalence in Dobermans.*
