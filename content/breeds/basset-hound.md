The Basset Hound comes from France, where the word *bas* means "low". Short-legged hounds were bred so that hunters on foot could keep up with them while they followed the scent of rabbits and hares. The breed is thought to descend from the St Hubert hound, the ancestor of the Bloodhound, which explains its superb nose. Bassets came to Britain in the 1860s and became popular across the English-speaking world, helped by the "Hush Puppies" shoe adverts. Today it is a gentle, easy-going and famously stubborn family dog.

### What a Basset's size means at home

The AKC standard says a Basset should not be over 14 in (35.5 cm) at the shoulder. Most stand about 28–38 cm, just below an adult's knee, but they're very heavy for their height: about 18–29.5 kg (40–65 lb). That's more than a Border Collie, which stands almost 20 cm taller. Bassets are long, deep-chested and heavy-boned, so the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=basset-hound) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate because of their length. Lifting a Basset into a car takes effort, so a ramp helps, and it's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Basset vs Beagle

The [Beagle](/compare/beagle-vs-basset-hound/) is about the same height, but a Basset is much longer and roughly twice as heavy. Both are friendly, food-loving scent hounds that like to follow their nose.

### Nose first

A Basset's nose rules its life. On a scent, it may ignore you completely, so many can't be let off the lead in open areas. They are famously slow to obey, more from stubbornness than lack of brains. Patient, food-based training works best. They bay loudly, which neighbours may not appreciate.

### Ears, eyes and weight

Those long ears trail on the ground, so check and clean them weekly to prevent infections. The droopy lower eyelids can collect dirt and become inflamed. Bassets love food, and extra weight strains their long back and short legs, so keep yours lean. Use ramps instead of letting it jump, and support its back and chest when lifting.

### Health

Known health problems include ear infections, eye problems (ectropion, glaucoma), back problems, elbow dysplasia and bloat, as in other deep-chested breeds. Bassets often live 12–13 years.
