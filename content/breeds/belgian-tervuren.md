The Belgian Tervuren takes its name from the village of Tervuren near Brussels, where an early breeder, M. F. Corbeel, kept some of the foundation dogs in the late 19th century. It is one of four varieties of Belgian Shepherd, along with the Malinois, the Groenendael and the Laekenois, which differ mainly in coat. The Tervuren has a long, rich coat in fawn to mahogany with a black overlay and a black mask. Like the other Belgian Shepherds, it's an intelligent, sensitive and very capable working dog.

### One breed or four?

The FCI treats the Belgian Shepherd as one breed with four coat varieties. The AKC and some other clubs treat the Tervuren as a separate breed. Either way, the Tervuren is the long-haired fawn variety, the [Groenendael](/compare/belgian-sheepdog-groenendael-vs-belgian-tervuren/) the long-haired black one, and the Malinois the short-haired one. All are about the same size.

### What a Tervuren's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24–26 in (61–66 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22–24 in (56–61 cm) for females. Tervurens weigh about 25–34 kg (55–75 lb) for males and 20.5–27 kg (45–60 lb) for females. A Tervuren reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's elegant, square and athletic, and its coat makes it look bigger. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=belgian-tervuren).

### Temperament and exercise

Tervurens are intelligent, alert and devoted to their family, often bonding closely with one person. They're watchful with strangers. They're slightly less intense than many Malinois, but they still need a lot of daily exercise and mental work. They excel at herding, obedience, agility and tracking. They're sensitive, so they respond best to gentle, consistent, reward-based training. Without enough to do, they can become anxious or destructive.

### Coat and health

The long double coat sheds heavily, especially seasonally, and needs brushing a few times a week. Tervurens are generally healthy. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, epilepsy, eye diseases and hypothyroidism. They often live 12–14 years.
