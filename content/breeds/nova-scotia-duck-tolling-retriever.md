The Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, or "Toller", was developed in the 19th century in Yarmouth County, Nova Scotia, in eastern Canada. It has an unusual hunting technique called "tolling": the dog plays and frolics along the shoreline, which makes curious ducks swim closer, within range of the hidden hunter. The dog then retrieves the shot birds from the water. The behaviour is thought to copy the way foxes lure waterfowl. The Toller is the smallest of the retrievers and the provincial dog of Nova Scotia.

### What a Toller's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–21 in (45.5–53.5 cm) for males and 17–20 in (43–51 cm) for females, and about 16–22.5 kg (35–50 lb). A Toller reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's compact, muscular and agile. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=nova-scotia-duck-tolling-retriever) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult, and a smaller Toller may fit a 36″ crate. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Toller vs Golden Retriever, Brittany and Springer

People often mistake a Toller for a small Golden Retriever. It's much smaller, with a redder coat and usually white markings on the chest, feet and tail tip. The [Brittany](/compare/brittany-vs-nova-scotia-duck-tolling-retriever/) is about the same height but lighter. The [English Springer Spaniel](/compare/english-springer-spaniel-vs-nova-scotia-duck-tolling-retriever/) is a little bigger.

### Energy and the "Toller scream"

Tollers are intelligent, energetic and playful, with a strong desire to retrieve. They need plenty of exercise every day, ideally with swimming and fetch, plus mental work. They excel at agility, obedience and dock diving. When excited, many make a high-pitched shriek known as the "Toller scream". They're affectionate with their family, sometimes reserved with strangers, and can be sensitive.

### Coat and health

The medium-length, water-repellent double coat sheds heavily, especially seasonally, and needs brushing a few times a week. Known health problems include Addison's disease, autoimmune conditions, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), hip dysplasia and cleft palate. DNA tests exist for some of these. Tollers often live 12–14 years.
