The Brittany comes from the region of Brittany in north-western France, where it has been used for centuries to find and point game birds and woodcock. It was long known as the Brittany Spaniel, but because it points game rather than flushing it like a spaniel, the AKC dropped "Spaniel" from its name in 1982. In Europe it is called the Épagneul Breton. Brittanies are prized by hunters for their nose and versatility, and the breed has produced more AKC dual champions (in both show and field) than any other.

### What a Brittany's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 17.5–20.5 in (44.5–52 cm) at the shoulder. Brittanies weigh about 13.5–18 kg (30–40 lb). A Brittany reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's a compact, leggy and athletic gundog that fits well in most homes and cars, and the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=brittany) suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate for an average adult. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Brittany vs Toller

The [Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever](/compare/brittany-vs-nova-scotia-duck-tolling-retriever/) is about the same height but heavier, with a fuller red coat. Both are compact, energetic gundogs that suit active families.

### Exercise: a lot of it

Brittanies are bred to run and hunt all day. They need at least an hour or two of hard exercise daily, such as running, retrieving, hunting or dog sports, plus mental challenges. Without it, they can become restless and destructive. They're sensitive and eager to please, so gentle, reward-based training works best. Their hunting instinct is strong, so recall training takes time.

### Coat and colours

The Brittany's coat is dense, flat or wavy, with light feathering. It needs only weekly brushing. In the US, the standard colours are orange and white or liver and white. European Brittanies may also be black and white or tricolour. Some are born with a naturally short tail, or none at all.

### Health

Brittanies are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, epilepsy and some eye conditions. They often live 12–14 years.
