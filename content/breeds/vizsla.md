The Vizsla, also called the Magyar Vizsla or Hungarian Pointer, is Hungary's national pointing dog. Its ancestors were hunting dogs of the Magyar tribes who settled in the Carpathian Basin over a thousand years ago. Later, Hungarian nobles refined it into an all-round gundog that points, flushes and retrieves birds and hares, on land and in water. The breed nearly vanished after the World Wars, but Hungarian emigrants took dogs abroad and helped save it. Today the Vizsla is admired for its golden-rust coat, elegance and gentle, devoted nature.

### What a Vizsla's size means at home

A male Vizsla stands about 56–61 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 25–27 kg (55–60 lb). Females stand about 53–59 cm and weigh 20–25 kg. It is lean and athletic, lighter than it looks for its height. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate (check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=vizsla)) and a home with space and access to open countryside.

### The "Velcro dog"

Vizslas are known as "Velcro dogs" because they love to be physically close to their people, following them from room to room and leaning against their legs. They don't do well left alone for long hours and can become anxious or destructive. If you work away from home all day, a Vizsla may not be the right choice.

### Vizsla vs Weimaraner, GSP and Rhodesian Ridgeback

- The [Weimaraner](/compare/weimaraner-vs-vizsla/) is bigger and heavier, with a grey coat.
- The [German Shorthaired Pointer](/compare/german-shorthaired-pointer-vs-vizsla/) is a little taller and heavier, usually liver and white.
- The [Rhodesian Ridgeback](/compare/vizsla-vs-rhodesian-ridgeback/) is taller and much heavier, with its typical ridge of hair along the back.
- The [Wirehaired Vizsla](/compare/vizsla-vs-wirehaired-vizsla/) is a separate breed with the same colour but a wiry coat and beard.

### Exercise and care

A Vizsla needs a lot of exercise: at least an hour or two a day of running, swimming or retrieving, plus mental work. It's an excellent partner for runners and cyclists once fully grown. The short coat needs very little grooming and has little odour, but offers little protection against cold, so a coat helps in winter.

### Health

Vizslas are generally healthy. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, epilepsy, allergies and some eye conditions, so ask breeders about health tests. They often live 12–14 years.
