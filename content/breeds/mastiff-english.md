The Mastiff (often called the English Mastiff, or the Old English Mastiff) is one of the largest and heaviest dog breeds in the world. Massive mastiff-type dogs were kept in Britain for over two thousand years as guard dogs, war dogs and, sadly, for blood sports. By the end of the Second World War, the breed had almost died out in Britain and was rebuilt with dogs from North America. A Mastiff called Zorba held the Guinness record for the heaviest dog in 1989, at about 155 kg (343 lb). Today's Mastiff is a calm, dignified and gentle family guardian.

### What a Mastiff's size means at home

The AKC standard sets minimum heights of 30 in (76 cm) for males and 27.5 in (70 cm) for females and gives no maximum. Most males stand about 76–91 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's hip, and weigh about 73–105 kg (160–230 lb). Females weigh about 55–77 kg (120–170 lb). That's as heavy as a grown man. An average male is too big for standard crates, as the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=mastiff-english) shows. It needs a van or very large car, a ramp, and a home with plenty of space. Food, vet and medicine bills are giant-sized too.

### Mastiff vs Great Dane, Saint Bernard and Cane Corso

The [Great Dane](/compare/great-dane-vs-mastiff-english/) is a little shorter on average and much lighter and leaner. The [Saint Bernard](/compare/mastiff-english-vs-saint-bernard/) is shorter and lighter. The [Cane Corso](/compare/cane-corso-vs-mastiff-english/) is much smaller and more athletic. The [Spanish Mastiff](/compare/mastiff-english-vs-spanish-mastiff/) is similar in size, with a longer coat.

### Temperament

Mastiffs are calm, good-natured and very affectionate with their families. They're naturally protective but not usually aggressive, and they tend to deter intruders by size alone. They're happy with moderate exercise and spend much of the day asleep. Early training and socialisation are essential, because an untrained 80 kg dog that pulls or jumps up is hard to manage. Expect drool and loud snoring.

### Growth and health

Mastiff puppies grow very fast and need a giant-breed puppy food, a lean body and protection from hard exercise and stairs until about 18–24 months. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, heart disease, eyelid problems and bone cancer. Like many giant breeds, Mastiffs have a short life, usually 6–10 years.
