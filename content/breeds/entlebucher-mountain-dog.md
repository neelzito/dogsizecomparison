The Entlebucher Mountain Dog (*Entlebucher Sennenhund*) is the smallest of the four Swiss mountain dog breeds. It's named after the Entlebuch valley in the canton of Lucerne, where farmers used it to drive cattle to the high pastures and guard the farm. The breed was nearly lost by the early 20th century, but Swiss enthusiasts revived it, and the first breed club was founded in 1926. It shares the tricolour coat of the Bernese and Greater Swiss Mountain Dogs, in a compact, energetic package.

### What an Entlebucher's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 17–21 in (44.5–52 cm) at the shoulder for males and 16–20 in (42–49.5 cm) for females. Entlebuchers weigh about 22.5–29.5 kg (50–65 lb) for males and 18–25 kg (40–55 lb) for females. An Entlebucher reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's compact, muscular and a little longer than tall. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=entlebucher-mountain-dog). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### The four Swiss mountain dogs

From smallest to largest: the Entlebucher, the Appenzeller, the [Bernese Mountain Dog](/breeds/bernese-mountain-dog/) and the [Greater Swiss Mountain Dog](/breeds/greater-swiss-mountain-dog/). The Entlebucher is much smaller than the Bernese and the Swissy and more of a herder. Next to an [Australian Shepherd](/compare/australian-shepherd-vs-entlebucher-mountain-dog/), it's a little shorter but about as heavy.

### Temperament and exercise

Entlebuchers are lively, clever, confident and very loyal to their family, often wary of strangers. They're true herding dogs, with lots of energy and a tendency to nip at heels. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental work, such as herding, agility, obedience or long walks. They can be strong-willed, so consistent, positive training and early socialisation are important.

### Coat and health

The short, dense, shiny double coat sheds seasonally and needs only weekly brushing. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), for which a DNA test exists, and Entlebucher urinary syndrome, which involves abnormalities of the urinary tract. Entlebuchers often live 11–13 years.
