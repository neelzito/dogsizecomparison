The Bearded Collie is a herding dog from the Scottish Highlands and Borders, used for centuries to work sheep and cattle in rough terrain. It is probably descended from Polish Lowland Sheepdogs brought to Scotland by traders, crossed with local herding dogs. As a working dog it was never standardised, and by the Second World War show "Beardies" had almost died out. In the 1940s Mrs G. Olive Willison began breeding from a pair of Beardies, and today's breed descends largely from her dogs. Known for its bouncing energy and sense of humour, the Beardie is a lively, affectionate companion.

### What a Beardie's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 21–22 in (53.5–56 cm) at the shoulder for males and 20–21 in (51–53.5 cm) for females. Beardies weigh about 20.5–25 kg (45–55 lb). A Beardie reaches roughly an adult's knee, and its long coat makes it look bigger. It needs a medium to large car boot, and the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bearded-collie) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Beardie vs Australian Shepherd and Old English Sheepdog

The Beardie is [about the same height as an Australian Shepherd](/compare/australian-shepherd-vs-bearded-collie/) but usually lighter, with a much longer coat and a long tail. The Old English Sheepdog looks similar but is bigger and heavier, with a stockier build and a naturally short or docked tail.

### Temperament and exercise

Beardies are cheerful, enthusiastic and full of energy, with a bouncy gait and a playful personality. They're affectionate with their family and usually good with children and other dogs. They're clever but can be independent and stubborn, so training should be fun and consistent. They need plenty of daily exercise and enjoy herding, agility and long walks.

### Coat care

The Beardie's long, harsh double coat is beautiful but high-maintenance. It needs a thorough brush, down to the skin, at least once or twice a week, more during moults, to prevent mats. Puppies are born black, brown, blue or fawn, and many lighten as they grow, sometimes dramatically, before darkening again in adulthood.

### Health

Beardies are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism, Addison's disease and autoimmune conditions. They often live 12–14 years.
