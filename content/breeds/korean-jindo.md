The Korean Jindo comes from Jindo Island off the south-west coast of South Korea, where it was used for centuries to hunt deer, boar and smaller game, often working in small packs. It was designated a natural monument of Korea in 1962. The breed is famous for its loyalty and homing instinct. In 1993, a Jindo called Baekgu made headlines after finding her way home to Jindo Island from about 300 km away. It's a clean, independent and very devoted dog.

### What a Jindo's size means at home

The breed standard calls for 19.5–22 in (49.5–56 cm) at the shoulder for males and 18–20.5 in (45.5–52 cm) for females. Jindos weigh about 18–22.5 kg (40–50 lb) for males and 13.5–18 kg (30–40 lb) for females. A Jindo reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's a medium-sized, athletic spitz. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=korean-jindo) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male.

### Jindo vs Shiba and Standard Schnauzer

A Jindo is taller and heavier than a Shiba Inu and looks a little like a small Akita. Next to a [Standard Schnauzer](/compare/korean-jindo-vs-standard-schnauzer/), it's a little taller and about the same weight.

### Temperament and exercise

Jindos are loyal, intelligent and very independent, often bonding with one person. They're reserved with strangers, may not tolerate other dogs, and have a strong prey drive. They're also escape artists, so they need a high, secure fence. They need daily exercise and patient, consistent training.

### Coat and health

The double coat sheds heavily once or twice a year. Jindos are known for being clean and easy to house-train. They're generally healthy. Known problems include hypothyroidism and allergies. Jindos often live 14–15 years.

*Sources: AKC breed information; FCI standard No. 334 (Korea Jindo Dog); Korean Jindo Dog Association.*
