The Appenzeller Sennenhund comes from the Appenzell region of north-eastern Switzerland. It's one of the four Swiss mountain dog breeds, along with the Entlebucher, the Bernese and the Greater Swiss. Farmers used it to drive cattle, guard the farm and pull carts. It's the most energetic of the four, and it's recognisable by its tightly curled tail, carried over the back.

### What an Appenzeller's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 52–56 cm at the shoulder for males and 50–54 cm for females, and Appenzellers weigh about 22–32 kg (48–70 lb). An Appenzeller reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's square, muscular and agile. Its weight puts it at the lower end of the large size class on this site. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=appenzeller-sennenhund) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult.

### Appenzeller vs other Swiss mountain dogs

The Appenzeller is a little bigger than the [Entlebucher](/breeds/entlebucher-mountain-dog/) and much smaller than the [Bernese](/breeds/bernese-mountain-dog/) and the [Greater Swiss](/breeds/greater-swiss-mountain-dog/). Next to a [Dutch Shepherd](/compare/appenzeller-sennenhund-vs-dutch-shepherd/), it's shorter and about the same weight.

### Temperament and exercise

Appenzellers are lively, confident and loyal, very attached to their family and wary of strangers. They're vocal watchdogs and true herders, with lots of energy. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental work, and consistent training and early socialisation.

### Care and health

The short, dense double coat sheds seasonally and needs weekly brushing. Appenzellers are generally healthy. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia. They often live 13–15 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 46 (Appenzeller Sennenhund); AKC breed information; Appenzeller Sennenhunde Club (Switzerland).*
