The Goldendoodle is a cross between a Golden Retriever and a Poodle. Breeders in the US and Australia began producing them in the 1990s, following the success of the Labradoodle, and the cross quickly became one of the most popular family dogs. People love its friendly, gentle Golden Retriever temperament combined with a curly or wavy coat that often sheds less. Like other doodles, it is a crossbreed without an official breed standard, so size and coat vary.

### How big does a Goldendoodle get?

The size depends mostly on the Poodle parent:

- **Standard Goldendoodle** (Standard Poodle parent): males about 55–61 cm at the shoulder and 23–34 kg (50–75 lb)
- **Medium:** about 43–53 cm
- **Miniature:** about 33–43 cm

Breeders also use terms like "petite" or "toy" for very small ones, but these aren't standardised. This page shows the Standard Goldendoodle, which reaches roughly mid-thigh on an adult. It is close to a [Golden Retriever](/compare/golden-retriever-vs-goldendoodle-standard/) in size and a little taller than a [Labradoodle](/compare/goldendoodle-standard-vs-labradoodle-standard/) on average. The [puppy weight calculator](/calculators/puppy-weight/?breed=goldendoodle-standard) gives a rough estimate, but ask the breeder about both parents' size too.

### What a Goldendoodle's size means at home

A Standard Goldendoodle is a large, bouncy dog. It needs a big boot, a 42″ (107 cm) crate (check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=goldendoodle-standard)) and plenty of daily exercise. Goldendoodles are friendly with almost everyone and don't make good guard dogs. They are too heavy for an airline cabin.

### F1, F1B and multigen

You'll see these labels in breeder adverts:

- **F1:** a first-generation cross, 50% Golden Retriever and 50% Poodle. Coats vary most, and many shed.
- **F1B:** an F1 crossed back to a Poodle (about 75% Poodle). Coats are more often curly and low-shedding.
- **Multigen:** doodle bred to doodle over several generations, usually more consistent in size and coat.

### Coat care

Curly and wavy Goldendoodle coats shed little but mat easily, especially behind the ears, under the legs and around the collar. Plan on brushing down to the skin several times a week and a professional groom every 6–8 weeks. No dog is truly hypoallergenic, so people with allergies should meet the puppy's parents first.

### Health

A Goldendoodle can inherit problems from both parent breeds, including hip and elbow dysplasia and eye diseases. Golden Retrievers also have a high cancer risk. Look for breeders who health-test both parents, not just "doodle" breeders who promise hybrid vigour.

*Sources: Goldendoodle Association of North America; AKC breed standards for the Golden Retriever and the Poodle.*
