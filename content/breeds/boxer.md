The Boxer was developed in Munich, Germany, in the 1890s from the *Bullenbeisser* ("bull-biter"), a sturdy hunting dog used to hold boar, bear and deer until the hunters arrived. Breeders crossed it with English bulldog-type dogs and created a strong, athletic dog that was easier to live with. The first Boxer club was founded in Munich in 1895. Boxers served as messenger and guard dogs in both world wars, and soldiers who brought them home helped make the breed popular in the US and UK. Today the Boxer is known as a playful, clownish family dog that stays puppyish for years.

### What a Boxer's size means at home

A male Boxer stands about 58–64 cm at the shoulder, roughly mid-thigh on an adult, and weighs about 30–36 kg (65–80 lb). Females are a little smaller. It has a short coat but a big, muscular body and a lot of bounce: Boxers jump up to greet people and can knock over small children without meaning to. Plan on a large car boot and a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=boxer). It is far too heavy for an airline cabin, and as a short-nosed breed, some airlines don't accept it in the hold either.

### Boxer vs Labrador

The two are [close in size](/compare/boxer-vs-labrador-retriever/), but they're built differently. A Boxer is squarer and leaner, with a deep chest, a tucked-up belly and a shorter back. The Labrador is longer and heavier in the body. The Boxer's short coat needs very little grooming but offers little protection against cold.

### Heat, exercise and training

The Boxer's short muzzle makes it less able to cool down than long-nosed breeds. Exercise it in the cooler part of the day in summer, and watch for heavy panting. Boxers have lots of energy and need daily runs and games, and they're happiest with company: they don't like being left alone for long. Early, consistent and positive training helps channel all that enthusiasm. Boxers are clever but can be stubborn, and they get bored by repetition.

### Health

Boxers grow quickly and usually reach adult height at about 12–18 months but often don't mature mentally until 2–3 years. Heart disease is a known concern: Boxer cardiomyopathy (ARVC) can cause irregular heartbeats and fainting. Breeders can screen their dogs with heart tests, including a 24-hour ECG (Holter). Cancer is also more common in Boxers than in many breeds. Life expectancy is around 10–12 years.
