The Labrador Retriever is the classic family dog: friendly, eager to please and built to work all day. Its ancestor is the St. John's water dog, a working dog that Newfoundland fishermen used to haul nets and fetch lines from icy water. In the 1800s, British estate owners such as the Earls of Malmesbury imported these dogs and bred them into gundogs that retrieve shot game from land and water. The thick, water-shedding coat, webbed feet and strong "otter" tail all come from that job. In the United States the Labrador was the most registered breed for 31 years in a row, until the French Bulldog overtook it.

### What a Labrador's size means at home

A Labrador is a solidly built large dog. At about 60 cm at the shoulder, its back is roughly level with an adult's mid-thigh, and its head easily reaches a kitchen counter, so food left at the edge isn't safe. Most Labradors need an estate car or SUV boot, or a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Use the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=labrador-retriever) to check against your own dog. It's far too heavy for an airline cabin, so it flies in the hold.

### Show lines and working lines

Labradors come in two fairly different types. **Show-bred** Labradors (often called "English" Labs) are stockier, with a broader head, a thicker otter tail and a heavier bone structure. **Working or field-bred** Labradors (often called "American" Labs) are leaner, longer in the leg and lighter, and often have more energy. Both are the same breed and are registered together, but two Labradors of the same height can differ by several kilos depending on their lines. The ranges on this page follow the breed standard. A lean working dog may sit at the lower end of the weight range and a show dog at the top.

### How fast a Labrador puppy grows

Labradors grow fast in their first six months and usually reach their adult height at about 12 months. They then fill out in chest and muscle until about 18 months to 2 years. The [puppy weight calculator](/calculators/puppy-weight/?breed=labrador-retriever) gives an estimate from your puppy's current weight.

### Weight and health

Labradors love food, and many carry a common gene variant (in POMC) that makes them hungrier than other dogs, so overweight Labradors are very common. Extra weight matters more than in most breeds because hip and elbow dysplasia are known problems in the breed. A healthy adult Labrador should have a visible waist and ribs you can feel easily. Keeping your dog lean is one of the best things you can do for its joints and life span.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; The Kennel Club breed standard; Raffan et al., Cell Metabolism (2016) on the POMC gene variant.*
