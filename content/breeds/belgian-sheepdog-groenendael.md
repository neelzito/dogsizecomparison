The Groenendael is the long-haired black variety of the Belgian Shepherd. It was developed in the 1890s by Nicholas Rose, a breeder who lived at the Château de Groenendael near Brussels and bred from a pair of black long-haired herding dogs. Groenendaels served as messenger and ambulance dogs in the First World War and later as police dogs. In the US, the AKC calls the breed the Belgian Sheepdog. It's an elegant, intelligent and very attached companion.

### One breed or four?

The FCI treats the Belgian Shepherd as one breed with four coat varieties: the Groenendael (long, black), the [Tervuren](/compare/belgian-sheepdog-groenendael-vs-belgian-tervuren/) (long, fawn to mahogany), the [Malinois](/compare/belgian-malinois-vs-belgian-sheepdog-groenendael/) (short, fawn) and the Laekenois (rough). The AKC treats the Groenendael, Tervuren and Malinois as separate breeds. All are about the same size and build.

### What a Groenendael's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24–26 in (61–66 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22–24 in (56–61 cm) for females. Groenendaels weigh about 25–34 kg (55–75 lb) for males and 20.5–27 kg (45–60 lb) for females. A Groenendael reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and its long coat makes it look bigger. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=belgian-sheepdog-groenendael).

### Temperament and exercise

Groenendaels are intelligent, alert and very loyal, often deeply attached to their family and watchful with strangers. They're sensitive and respond best to gentle, consistent training. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental work, and they excel at herding, obedience, agility and tracking. Without enough to do, they can become anxious or destructive. They're generally a little less intense than working-line Malinois.

### Coat and health

The long, black double coat sheds heavily, especially seasonally, and needs brushing a couple of times a week. Groenendaels are generally healthy. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, epilepsy, eye diseases and hypothyroidism. They often live 12–14 years.
