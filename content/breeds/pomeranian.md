The Pomeranian is the smallest of the spitz family, and its ancestors were much bigger. It takes its name from Pomerania, a region on the Baltic coast now divided between Germany and Poland, where larger white spitz dogs worked as herders and watchdogs. In the 18th century, British royalty brought these dogs to England. Queen Victoria made the breed fashionable in the late 19th century, especially smaller dogs, and breeders reduced the size quickly. Within a few decades, the Pomeranian had shrunk to the tiny, fluffy companion it is today: lively, bold and very alert.

### What a Pomeranian's size means at home

A Pomeranian stands only about 15–18 cm at the shoulder, around ankle height on an adult, and weighs 1.4–3.2 kg (3–7 lb). Its thick coat makes it look much bigger than it is. It suits any home, including small flats, and it usually needs an 18″ (46 cm) crate or carrier. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=pomeranian). Being so small, it can be hurt by a jump or fall from furniture or arms, or by rough play with bigger dogs or young children.

### Flying with a Pomeranian

A Pomeranian is one of the easiest breeds to fly in the cabin. Even with a carrier, it stays well under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set. Check the rules for your airline with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=pomeranian).

### Pomeranian vs Chihuahua and Pomsky

A Pomeranian and a [Chihuahua](/compare/chihuahua-vs-pomeranian/) weigh about the same, but they look very different: the Pom is fluffy and fox-faced, the Chihuahua sleek with large ears. The [Pomsky](/compare/pomsky-vs-pomeranian/), a cross between a Pomeranian and a Siberian Husky, is much bigger, at around 25–38 cm and 7–14 kg.

### Coat and voice

The Pomeranian's double coat has a soft, dense undercoat and a long, straight outer coat. It sheds, especially seasonally, and needs brushing a few times a week to prevent mats. Don't shave a Pomeranian: the coat may grow back patchy or not at all. Poms make alert little watchdogs and can be very vocal, so start gentle training against excessive barking early.

### Health

Common problems include loose kneecaps (luxating patella), dental disease from crowded teeth, and a weak windpipe (tracheal collapse), so a harness is safer than a collar. Some Pomeranians develop alopecia X, a hair-loss condition sometimes called "black skin disease". Poms often live 12–16 years.
