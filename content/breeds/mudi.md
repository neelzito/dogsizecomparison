The Mudi (say "MOO-dee") is a Hungarian herding dog, one of three Hungarian herders along with the Puli and the Pumi. It developed naturally on farms from crosses of herding dogs and was described as a breed only in the 1930s. It works sheep and cattle with energy and independence, and it has also been used to hunt and to guard. It's rare, but its curly coat, upright ears and merle colours make it striking.

### What a Mudi's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 15–18.5 in (38–47 cm) at the shoulder, and Mudik weigh about 8–13 kg (18–29 lb). A Mudi reaches just below an adult's knee. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=mudi). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Mudi vs Pumi, Pyrenean Shepherd and Sheltie

The [Pumi](/compare/mudi-vs-pumi/) is a little taller and heavier, with corkscrew curls. The [Pyrenean Shepherd](/compare/mudi-vs-pyrenean-shepherd/) is about the same size. The [Shetland Sheepdog](/compare/mudi-vs-shetland-sheepdog/) is smaller.

### Temperament and exercise

Mudik are intelligent, energetic and loyal, devoted to their family and wary of strangers. They need a lot of exercise and mental work, and they excel at herding, agility and obedience. They're vocal.

### Coat and health

The wavy or curly coat is easy to care for. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and epilepsy. Mudik often live 12–14 years.
