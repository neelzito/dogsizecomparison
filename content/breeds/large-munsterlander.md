The Large Munsterlander comes from the Münster region of north-west Germany. It descends from long-haired German pointing dogs. When the German Longhaired Pointer club accepted only brown dogs in the early 20th century, breeders of the black-and-white dogs founded a separate breed in 1919. It's a versatile hunting dog that points, retrieves and tracks, with a long, feathered black-and-white coat.

### What a Large Munsterlander's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 60–65 cm at the shoulder for males and 58–63 cm for females, and Large Munsterlanders weigh about 22.5–32 kg (50–70 lb). One reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=large-munsterlander).

### Large Munsterlander vs GWP and Pointer

The [German Wirehaired Pointer](/compare/german-wirehaired-pointer-vs-large-munsterlander/) is about the same size, with a wiry coat. The [Pointer](/compare/large-munsterlander-vs-pointer-english/) is a little taller and heavier. Despite the name, the Small Munsterlander is a separate breed with a different history.

### Temperament and exercise

Large Munsterlanders are friendly, gentle and eager to work, good with children. They need a lot of exercise and mental work, ideally hunting, tracking or retrieving.

### Coat and health

The long, feathered coat needs brushing a couple of times a week. Known health problems include hip dysplasia. They often live 12–14 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 118 (Grosser Münsterländer); AKC breed information.*
