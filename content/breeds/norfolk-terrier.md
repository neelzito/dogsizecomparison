The Norfolk Terrier is one of the smallest working terriers, from East Anglia in England. It was shown together with the Norwich Terrier until 1964, when The Kennel Club separated them. The AKC followed in 1979. The main difference is the ears: the Norfolk's fold forward, while the Norwich's stand upright. Bred to hunt rats and bolt foxes, the Norfolk is a fearless, cheerful little dog.

### What a Norfolk's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 9–10 in (23–25.5 cm) at the shoulder and about 11–12 lb (5–5.5 kg). A Norfolk reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's compact, short-legged and sturdy. It suits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=norfolk-terrier). It usually fits the airline cabin limit. ### Norfolk vs Norwich

The [Norwich Terrier](/compare/norfolk-terrier-vs-norwich-terrier/) is the same size and build, with pricked ears. Both come in red, wheaten, black and tan, or grizzle.

### Temperament and exercise

Norfolks are affectionate, bold and full of energy, and generally more sociable with other dogs than many terriers. They need daily walks and play. They love to dig and chase small animals, so a secure garden is essential, and recall can be unreliable.

### Coat and health

The harsh, wiry coat sheds little and is traditionally hand-stripped a couple of times a year. Known health problems include heart conditions (mitral valve disease), hip dysplasia and loose kneecaps. Norfolks often live 12–16 years.
