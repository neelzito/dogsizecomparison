The Tibetan Terrier isn't really a terrier. It's an ancient herding and companion dog from the monasteries and villages of Tibet, where it was kept as a good-luck charm, a watchdog and a herder. Tibetan Terriers were never sold, but were given as gifts. In the 1920s, a British doctor working in India, Agnes Greig, was given one as thanks for treating a patient, and she brought the breed to Britain. The name "terrier" came from its size and shaggy look.

### What a Tibetan Terrier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 15–16 in (38–40.5 cm) at the shoulder, with weights of about 8–13.5 kg (18–30 lb). The figures on this page cover about 35.5–43 cm. A Tibetan Terrier reaches just below an adult's knee. It's square and sturdy, with large, flat, round feet that act like snowshoes. It usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=tibetan-terrier). It's too heavy for most airline cabins.

### Tibetan Terrier vs Lhasa Apso and Shiba

The Lhasa Apso, another Tibetan breed, is smaller, lower and longer. The Tibetan Terrier is taller and squarer, with longer legs. It's about [the same size as a Shiba Inu](/compare/shiba-inu-vs-tibetan-terrier/), but a very different dog in coat and character.

### Temperament and exercise

Tibetan Terriers are affectionate, lively and loyal, devoted to their family and often reserved with strangers. They're clever and sensitive, so gentle, consistent training works best. They need daily walks and play, and many enjoy agility. They're adaptable and can live in flats or houses, but they don't like being left alone for long.

### Coat and health

The long, double coat is fine and profuse. It sheds little but mats easily, so it needs thorough brushing, down to the skin, several times a week, or a shorter clip. Known health problems include eye diseases (primary lens luxation, progressive retinal atrophy), hip dysplasia and neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis (a nerve disease), and DNA tests exist for several of these. Tibetan Terriers are long-lived, often 15–16 years.
