The Harrier is an English pack hound bred to hunt hares, followed by hunters on foot or horseback. Harrier packs have been recorded in England since at least the 13th century. In size and type, it sits between the Beagle and the English Foxhound, and it's sometimes described as "a Beagle on steroids". It's rare as a pet, even in the UK and US.

### What a Harrier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 19–21 in (48.5–53.5 cm) at the shoulder, and Harriers weigh about 20.5–27 kg (45–60 lb). A Harrier reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's sturdy, muscular and athletic. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=harrier) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average adult. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Harrier vs Beagle and Elkhound

A [Beagle](/compare/beagle-vs-harrier/) is much smaller and about half the weight, but looks very similar. The [Norwegian Elkhound](/compare/harrier-vs-norwegian-elkhound/) is about the same size, but a thick-coated spitz.

### Temperament and exercise

Harriers are cheerful, friendly and outgoing, great with other dogs and children. They need a lot of exercise, and their nose leads them, so recall is often unreliable. They bay loudly and don't like being left alone.

### Care and health

The short coat needs only weekly brushing. Harriers are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia and epilepsy. They often live 12–15 years.
