The Giant Schnauzer was developed in Bavaria in southern Germany from the Standard Schnauzer, probably crossed with larger breeds such as the Great Dane, the Bouvier des Flandres and black herding dogs. Farmers and cattle drovers used it to drive cattle to market and guard farms and butcher's shops. When cattle droving ended, the breed found new work as a police and military dog in Germany. Today it is a powerful, intelligent and loyal working dog and companion for experienced owners.

### What a Giant Schnauzer's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 25.5–27.5 in (65–70 cm) at the shoulder for males and 23.5–25.5 in (60–65 cm) for females. Giants weigh about 34–43 kg (75–95 lb) for males and 25–36.5 kg (55–80 lb) for females. A Giant reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's big, square and strong. It needs a large vehicle and, because of its height, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=giant-schnauzer) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

### Three Schnauzer sizes

The Giant is the biggest of three related breeds. The [Standard Schnauzer](/compare/standard-schnauzer-vs-giant-schnauzer/) is much smaller, about 19 cm shorter and half the weight, and the Miniature Schnauzer is smaller still. They look alike, with the beard, eyebrows and wiry coat, but they're separate breeds with very different energy and space needs. Next to a [Hovawart](/compare/giant-schnauzer-vs-hovawart/), another German working dog, the Giant is similar in size.

### Temperament and exercise

Giant Schnauzers are intelligent, energetic and protective. They're loyal to their family and wary of strangers. They need a lot of daily exercise and mental work, such as training, tracking, protection sports or agility. Without it, they can become hard to handle. They need early socialisation and consistent, confident training, so they're best for experienced owners.

### Coat and health

The wiry, weather-resistant coat sheds little but needs brushing a couple of times a week and clipping or hand-stripping every 6–8 weeks. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, hypothyroidism, eye diseases and toe cancer (squamous cell carcinoma), which is more common in black breeds. Giants often live 10–13 years.
