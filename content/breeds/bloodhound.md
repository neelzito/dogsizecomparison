The Bloodhound is the classic scent hound. It descends from the St Hubert hound, bred by monks at the Abbey of Saint-Hubert in the Ardennes (in today's Belgium) from the Middle Ages. In Britain the breed was refined for tracking deer and, later, people. Its nose is so reliable that in the US, evidence from a trailing Bloodhound has been accepted in court. Bloodhounds are still used by police and search-and-rescue teams to follow trails that are days old. The name comes from "blooded" hounds, meaning pure-bred, not from any taste for blood.

### What a Bloodhound's size means at home

A male Bloodhound stands about 64–69 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 41–50 kg (90–110 lb). Females are about 59–64 cm and 36–45 kg (80–100 lb). It's a big, heavy-boned dog with loose skin, long ears and deep wrinkles. It needs a large vehicle and, because of its height and long head, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bloodhound) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male. Expect a lot of drool.

### Bloodhound vs Coonhound and Otterhound

The [Black and Tan Coonhound](/compare/black-and-tan-coonhound-vs-bloodhound/) is similar in height and colour but lighter, with less loose skin. The [Otterhound](/compare/bloodhound-vs-otterhound/), a rare British scent hound, is similar in size but has a shaggy, waterproof coat.

### Living with a Bloodhound

Bloodhounds are gentle, affectionate and good-natured with people, including children. But they're powerful, stubborn and driven by their nose. Once on a scent, they ignore almost everything else, so they need a long line or a secure area, and a strong person on the lead. They need daily walks long enough to sniff and explore, and they love scent games and mantrailing. They bay loudly and can be messy.

### Care and health

The short coat sheds moderately. The wrinkles, ears and droopy eyelids need regular cleaning to prevent infection. Bloodhounds are at very high risk of bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus), a life-threatening emergency, so feed smaller meals and avoid exercise right after eating. Hip and elbow dysplasia and eyelid problems are also common. Bloodhounds often live 10–12 years.
