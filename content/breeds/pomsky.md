The Pomsky is a cross between a Pomeranian and a Siberian Husky, one of the newest designer crosses. It became an internet sensation in the early 2010s, when photos of "mini Husky" puppies went viral, and the first deliberate litters were bred around that time. Because of the huge size difference between the parents, first-generation Pomskies are usually produced by artificial insemination, with a Husky mother. As a crossbreed, the Pomsky has no official standard, and its size and look vary a lot.

### How big does a Pomsky get?

Most Pomskies stand about 25–38 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult, and weigh about 7–14 kg (15–30 lb). First-generation Pomskies (50% of each parent) tend to be bigger. Multigeneration Pomskies, bred Pomsky to Pomsky, are often more predictable. Ads for "teacup" or "toy" Pomskies usually overpromise. Ask about both parents' size, and use the [puppy weight calculator](/calculators/puppy-weight/?breed=pomsky) as a rough guide.

### Pomsky vs Pomeranian and Husky

A Pomsky is [much bigger than a Pomeranian](/compare/pomsky-vs-pomeranian/), roughly four to five times the weight, and [much smaller than a Siberian Husky](/compare/pomsky-vs-siberian-husky/). Its look, a fluffy coat with Husky-like markings and often blue eyes, is what makes it popular, but not every puppy looks like a "mini Husky".

### What a Pomsky's size means at home

A Pomsky suits flats and houses and usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=pomsky). Most are over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit for dog and carrier on many airlines.

### Temperament and care

Pomskies are usually lively, clever and affectionate, but they can inherit the Husky's independence, escape skills and vocal "talking", and the Pomeranian's tendency to bark. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and consistent training. The thick double coat sheds heavily, especially once or twice a year, and needs brushing several times a week.

### Health

Pomskies can inherit problems from both parents, including loose kneecaps, eye diseases, dental problems and hip dysplasia. Choose a breeder who health-tests both parents, treats them well, and is open about how litters are produced. Pomskies often live 13–15 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standards for the Pomeranian and the Siberian Husky; Pomsky breeder associations.*
