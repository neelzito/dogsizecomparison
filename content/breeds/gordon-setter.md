The Gordon Setter is Scotland's setter, named after the 4th Duke of Gordon, who bred black-and-tan setters at Gordon Castle in the 1820s. Black-and-tan setters were known in Scotland before then, but the Duke's kennel made them famous. Gordons were bred for stamina and a good nose rather than speed, to work the moors for grouse all day. It's the most heavily built of the setter breeds and a loyal, affectionate family dog for active homes.

### What a Gordon's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24–27 in (61–68.5 cm) and 55–80 lb (25–36.5 kg) for males, and 23–26 in (58.5–66 cm) and 45–70 lb (20.5–32 kg) for females. A Gordon reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's sturdy, well-muscled and athletic. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=gordon-setter).

### Gordon vs Irish and English Setter

The [Irish Setter](/compare/gordon-setter-vs-irish-setter/) is a little taller and similar in weight, with a mahogany coat. The [English Setter](/compare/english-setter-vs-gordon-setter/) is a little taller and heavier on average, with a speckled belton coat. The Gordon is always black and tan, with a heavier build and head than the other setters.

### Temperament and exercise

Gordons are loyal, affectionate and confident, devoted to their family and sometimes reserved with strangers. They make better watchdogs than other setters. They mature slowly and stay puppyish for a few years. They need at least one to two hours of exercise every day, ideally with free running in a safe area. They have a strong bird-hunting instinct, so recall needs a lot of work. They can be stubborn, so patient, consistent training works best.

### Coat and health

The long, silky coat with feathering needs brushing a few times a week and regular trimming around the feet and ears. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, hypothyroidism, and progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and cerebellar cortical degeneration (a nerve disease), both of which have DNA tests. Gordons often live 12–13 years.
