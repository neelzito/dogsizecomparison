The Bracco Italiano is an ancient Italian pointing dog. Dogs like it appear in Renaissance paintings and frescoes, and it was kept by noble families such as the Medici and the Gonzaga for hunting birds with nets and, later, with guns. It nearly died out by the end of the 19th century, but Italian enthusiasts revived it. With its long ears, loose lips and gentle expression, it looks like a cross between a pointer and a hound. The AKC recognised it in 2022.

### What a Bracco's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 58–67 cm at the shoulder for males and 55–62 cm for females. The figures on this page, about 53.5–68.5 cm and 25–41 kg (55–90 lb), cover both sexes. A Bracco reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's solid, muscular and square. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bracco-italiano).

### Bracco vs Spinone

The [Spinone Italiano](/compare/bracco-italiano-vs-spinone-italiano/), the other Italian pointer, is a little taller and heavier on average, with a wiry coat. The Bracco is short-coated, in white and orange or white and chestnut.

### Temperament and exercise

Braccos are gentle, affectionate and sociable, great with children and other dogs. They hunt at a fast, high-headed trot. They need daily exercise and space to run, and they're sensitive, so gentle training works best. Expect some drool.

### Care and health

The short coat needs little grooming, but the long ears need regular cleaning. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat and kidney disease. Braccos often live 10–14 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 202 (Bracco Italiano); AKC breed standard; Bracco Italiano Club of America.*
