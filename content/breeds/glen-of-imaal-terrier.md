The Glen of Imaal Terrier comes from the remote Glen of Imaal in County Wicklow, Ireland. Farmers there used it to hunt badgers and foxes and to kill rats. According to tradition, it also worked as a turnspit dog, running in a wheel to turn meat roasting over the fire. It's low, sturdy and quieter than many terriers, and it's rare even in Ireland.

### What a Glen's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 12.5–14 in (32–35.5 cm) at the shoulder and about 35 lb (16 kg) for males, with females a little lighter. A Glen reaches just below an adult's knee, but it's very heavy for its height, which puts it in the medium size class on this site. It usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=glen-of-imaal-terrier). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Glen vs Staffordshire Bull Terrier

The [Staffordshire Bull Terrier](/compare/glen-of-imaal-terrier-vs-staffordshire-bull-terrier/) is a little taller and lighter, with a smooth coat. The Glen has bowed front legs and a harsh, medium-length coat in wheaten or blue brindle.

### Temperament and exercise

Glens are calm, loyal and affectionate, but still brave, independent terriers that can be scrappy with other dogs. They need daily walks and play.

### Coat and health

The coat needs regular brushing and hand-stripping a couple of times a year. Known health problems include progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), for which a DNA test exists, and hip and elbow dysplasia. Glens often live 10–15 years.
