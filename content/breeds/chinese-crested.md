The Chinese Crested is a small, elegant companion breed, famous for its mostly hairless body with silky hair on the head (the crest), feet (the socks) and tail (the plume). Its origins are uncertain. A popular story says Chinese sailors kept these dogs on ships as ratters, but hairless dogs probably came from Africa or the Americas. The breed was developed in Europe and America from the late 19th century. A Chinese Crested named Sam won the "World's Ugliest Dog" contest three years running in the 2000s, but most Cresteds are dainty and graceful.

### Hairless and Powderpuff

Chinese Cresteds come in two varieties, often born in the same litter:

- **Hairless:** mostly bare skin, with silky hair on the head, feet and tail.
- **Powderpuff:** covered in a soft, long double coat.

The hairless gene is dominant, and puppies inheriting two copies don't survive. That's why Hairless dogs are always bred with Powderpuffs in the background.

### What a Chinese Crested's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 11–13 in (28–33 cm) at the shoulder. Most Cresteds weigh about 3.5–5.5 kg (8–12 lb). A Crested reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's fine-boned and light, and it suits any home. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=chinese-crested). It easily fits the airline cabin limit. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=chinese-crested).

### Crested vs Italian Greyhound and Min Pin

The [Italian Greyhound](/compare/chinese-crested-vs-italian-greyhound/) is a little taller and similar in weight, with a short coat. The [Miniature Pinscher](/compare/chinese-crested-vs-miniature-pinscher/) is about the same size but sturdier and more terrier-like.

### Caring for bare skin

Hairless Cresteds need sunscreen in sunny weather, coats and jumpers in the cold, and regular baths to prevent blackheads and acne. Their skin can be sensitive to some products. Powderpuffs need regular brushing to prevent mats, like any long-coated dog. Neither variety sheds much.

### Temperament and health

Cresteds are affectionate, playful and very attached to their people. They like to cuddle and often struggle when left alone. They're gentle and sensitive, so they need gentle handling. Known health problems include dental problems (common in Hairless dogs, which often have missing teeth), loose kneecaps, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and lens luxation, and skin problems. Cresteds are long-lived, often 13–18 years.
