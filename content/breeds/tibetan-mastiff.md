The Tibetan Mastiff is an ancient guardian breed from the Himalayas. For centuries, nomadic herders and monasteries in Tibet used these massive, thick-coated dogs to guard flocks, tents and villages against wolves, leopards and intruders. They were often tied up by day and let loose at night. In the 2000s and 2010s, Tibetan Mastiffs became a status symbol in China, and some puppies reportedly sold for very high prices before the trend collapsed. Today the breed is a powerful, independent guardian that needs an experienced owner.

### What a Tibetan Mastiff's size means at home

The AKC standard sets minimum heights of 26 in (66 cm) for males and 24 in (61 cm) for females. Most males stand about 66–76 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weigh about 41–68 kg (90–150 lb). Females weigh about 32–54.5 kg (70–120 lb). The huge mane makes it look even bigger, and its weight puts it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle, a secure garden and a 54″ (137 cm) crate. ### Tibetan Mastiff vs Caucasian Shepherd and Estrela

The [Caucasian Shepherd](/compare/tibetan-mastiff-vs-caucasian-shepherd-dog/), another mountain guardian, is a little shorter on average but usually heavier. The Portuguese [Estrela Mountain Dog](/compare/estrela-mountain-dog-vs-tibetan-mastiff/) is a little smaller and lighter. All of these livestock guardians are independent and protective.

### Temperament

Tibetan Mastiffs are calm, loyal and devoted to their family but aloof and suspicious with strangers. They're strongly protective and independent, bred to make their own decisions. They can be stubborn and hard to train, and they need extensive early socialisation and a confident, experienced owner. Many are nocturnal guardians that bark at night, which may not suit close neighbours. Like wild canines, females usually come into season only once a year.

### Coat and care

The thick double coat sheds heavily once a year, usually in spring or summer, and needs a lot of brushing during the moult. The rest of the year it needs weekly brushing. Tibetan Mastiffs cope well with cold but struggle with heat.

### Health

Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, hypothyroidism, eye problems and some inherited nerve diseases. Tibetan Mastiffs are long-lived for a giant breed, often 10–12 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 230 (Do-Khyi); American Tibetan Mastiff Association.*
