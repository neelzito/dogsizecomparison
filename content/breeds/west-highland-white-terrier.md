The West Highland White Terrier comes from the western Highlands of Scotland, where small, hardy terriers hunted foxes, badgers and vermin among the rocks. According to a well-known story, Colonel Edward Malcolm of Poltalloch began breeding white terriers in the 19th century after one of his reddish terriers was shot by mistake for a fox. Whatever the truth of the story, the white "Poltalloch terriers" became the Westie, recognised in the early 1900s. Today it is a confident, cheerful and independent companion.

### What a Westie's size means at home

A Westie stands about 28 cm at the shoulder (males) and 25 cm (females), around mid-shin on an adult, and weighs about 7–9 kg (15–20 lb). It is compact and sturdy, with a deep chest and short legs. It suits flats and small homes, and it enjoys walks and play. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=west-highland-white-terrier). Most adult Westies are too heavy for the cabin once you add a carrier, since many airlines cap dog and carrier at 8 kg (17.6 lb).

### Westie vs Cairn and Scottie

These three Scottish terriers are closely related and similar in height. The [Cairn Terrier](/compare/west-highland-white-terrier-vs-cairn-terrier/) is a little lighter, with a shaggier coat in sandy, grey or brindle shades. The [Scottish Terrier](/compare/west-highland-white-terrier-vs-scottish-terrier/) is heavier, with a long head, a beard and usually a black coat.

### Coat care

The Westie's double coat has a harsh outer layer and a soft undercoat. It sheds little but needs brushing a few times a week and regular trimming. Show dogs are hand-stripped to keep the coat harsh and white. Pet Westies are often clipped, which makes the coat softer. Keeping a Westie truly white takes effort, so expect some light-brown staining around the mouth and feet.

### Temperament

Westies are friendly, curious and self-assured, with a real terrier spirit. They like to dig, chase and bark at intruders, and they can be stubborn. They do well with patient, reward-based training. Most get on well with people, but small pets may trigger their prey drive.

### Health

Skin problems are the Westie's best-known health issue. Allergic skin disease (atopic dermatitis) is common and often shows up as itching, licking and red skin. Westies are also prone to a lung disease in older age (pulmonary fibrosis, sometimes called "Westie lung disease") and to some joint problems. They often live 13–15 years.
