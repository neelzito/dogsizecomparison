The Parson Russell Terrier is the closest modern breed to the original fox terriers of the Reverend John "Jack" Russell, a hunting parson in Devon in the 19th century. He bred mostly white terriers with long enough legs to keep up with horses and hounds, and a narrow enough chest to follow a fox underground. Over time, Russell-type terriers split into several breeds. The Parson is the taller, square-built type, while the Jack Russell Terrier is shorter in the leg.

### What a Parson's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 14 in (35.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 13 in (33 cm) for females. Parsons weigh about 6–7.5 kg (13–17 lb). A Parson reaches just below an adult's knee. It's square, athletic and narrow-chested. It suits most homes but needs a lot of exercise. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=parson-russell-terrier).

### Can a Parson fly in the cabin?

Sometimes. A light Parson in a light carrier may stay within the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, but bigger ones won't. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=parson-russell-terrier).

### Parson vs Jack Russell and Border Terrier

The [Jack Russell Terrier](/compare/jack-russell-terrier-vs-parson-russell-terrier/) is shorter in the leg and slightly longer than tall, at about 25–30 cm. The [Border Terrier](/compare/border-terrier-vs-parson-russell-terrier/) is a similar size but has an otter-like head and a grizzle or red coat. A [Miniature Schnauzer](/compare/miniature-schnauzer-vs-parson-russell-terrier/) is about the same height.

### Temperament and exercise

Parsons are energetic, clever, bold and full of fun, real working terriers. They need plenty of exercise every day and mental stimulation: long walks, games, agility, earthdog trials or flyball. They love to dig, chase and explore, so a secure garden is essential, and recall can be unreliable near small animals. A bored Parson can be noisy and destructive. With enough to do, they're affectionate and entertaining companions.

### Coat and health

Parsons come in a smooth coat and a broken (rough) coat. Both shed and need weekly brushing, and broken coats benefit from occasional hand-stripping. Known health problems include lens luxation (a displaced lens in the eye), deafness, ataxia and loose kneecaps, and DNA tests exist for some of these. Parsons often live 13–15 years.
