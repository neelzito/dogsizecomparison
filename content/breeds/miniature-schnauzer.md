The Miniature Schnauzer was developed in Germany in the late 19th century from the Standard Schnauzer, a farm dog used to catch rats and guard the yard. Farmers wanted a smaller ratter, so they bred small Standard Schnauzers together and probably added the Affenpinscher and possibly the Poodle. The result kept the Schnauzer's look, with its wiry coat, bushy eyebrows and beard, in a compact size. *Schnauze* is German for "snout", after that famous whiskered muzzle. Today the Miniature Schnauzer is a popular, cheerful and alert family dog.

### What a Miniature Schnauzer's size means at home

A Miniature Schnauzer stands about 30–36 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult, and weighs about 5–9 kg (11–20 lb). It is sturdy and square, small enough for a flat but tough enough for long walks and an active family. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=miniature-schnauzer).

### Can a Miniature Schnauzer fly in the cabin?

Sometimes. A light dog of about 5–6 kg in a light carrier may fit under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, but bigger Miniature Schnauzers won't. Use the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=miniature-schnauzer) for your dog.

### Three sizes of Schnauzer

The Miniature is the smallest of three related breeds. The [Standard Schnauzer](/compare/miniature-schnauzer-vs-standard-schnauzer/) is the original, medium-sized farm dog, and the [Giant Schnauzer](/compare/standard-schnauzer-vs-giant-schnauzer/) is a big, powerful working dog. They look alike but are separate breeds with very different sizes and energy levels. In the US, the Miniature is classed as a terrier, while the FCI groups it with the pinschers and schnauzers.

### Coat care

The Miniature Schnauzer's wiry double coat sheds very little. It needs brushing a few times a week and regular trimming. Show dogs are hand-stripped (the dead hair is plucked out) to keep the coat harsh and the colour rich. Most pet dogs are clipped every 6–8 weeks instead, which makes the coat softer.

### Health and diet

Miniature Schnauzers are prone to high levels of fat in the blood (hyperlipidaemia), which can lead to pancreatitis. Avoid fatty foods and table scraps, and keep your dog at a healthy weight. Bladder stones and certain eye diseases also occur in the breed. Miniature Schnauzers often live 12–15 years.
