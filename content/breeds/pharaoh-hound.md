The Pharaoh Hound is the national dog of Malta, where it is called *Kelb tal-Fenek*, "rabbit dog". Maltese hunters have used it for centuries, often at night, to find rabbits with its keen sight, hearing and nose. Despite its name and resemblance to dogs in ancient Egyptian art, genetic studies don't show a direct link to Egyptian dogs. The breed is famous for "blushing": when it's excited or happy, its nose and ears turn a rosy pink.

### What a Pharaoh Hound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 23–25 in (58.5–63.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 21–24 in (53.5–61 cm) for females. Pharaohs weigh about 20.5–25 kg (45–55 lb). A Pharaoh reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's tall, lean and elegant, light for its height, so its weight puts it in the medium size class on this site. Because of its height and big upright ears, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=pharaoh-hound) suggests a 48″ (122 cm) crate for an average male.

### Pharaoh Hound vs Ibizan Hound and Podenco Canario

The [Podenco Canario](/compare/podenco-canario-vs-pharaoh-hound/) is very similar in size and shape. The Ibizan Hound is a little taller, with a red, white or red-and-white coat. The Pharaoh is always tan to chestnut red, sometimes with a white tail tip.

### Temperament and exercise

Pharaoh Hounds are friendly, playful and affectionate with their family, and often reserved with strangers. They need daily exercise, including the chance to run flat-out in a large, securely fenced area. Their hunting instinct is strong, so recall is often unreliable and small pets may not be safe. They can jump high and should have a tall fence. They're intelligent but independent, so gentle, positive training works best. Some are vocal.

### Care and health

The short, glossy coat needs very little grooming. Pharaohs feel the cold, so they need a coat in winter and a soft bed. Like other sighthounds, they can be sensitive to some anaesthetics and medicines. The breed is generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, kneecap problems and allergies. Pharaoh Hounds often live 12–14 years.
