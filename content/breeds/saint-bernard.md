The Saint Bernard takes its name from the Great St Bernard Hospice, a refuge for travellers on one of the highest passes in the Swiss Alps. From the 17th century, the monks kept large dogs there that guided them through snow and fog and helped find lost or buried travellers. The most famous, Barry, is said to have saved more than 40 people in the early 1800s. His body is still on display in the Natural History Museum in Bern. The image of a Saint Bernard with a barrel of brandy round its neck is a myth, but the breed's gentle, patient nature is real.

### What a Saint Bernard's size means at home

A male Saint Bernard stands about 71–76 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 64–82 kg (140–180 lb). Females stand about 66–71 cm and weigh 55–64 kg. With its massive head and body, a Saint fills a room and a car. It needs a van or very large estate, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=saint-bernard)) and a strong, calm owner on the lead. Expect a lot of drool.

### Saint Bernard vs Newfoundland, Mastiff and Bernese

The Saint is [a little taller and heavier than a Newfoundland](/compare/newfoundland-vs-saint-bernard/), and [much heavier than a Bernese Mountain Dog](/compare/bernese-mountain-dog-vs-saint-bernard/). The [English Mastiff](/compare/mastiff-english-vs-saint-bernard/) is bigger still, both taller and heavier.

### Short coat or long coat

Saint Bernards come in two coats: short-haired (smooth, dense and closer to the original hospice dogs) and long-haired. Both shed heavily, especially twice a year, and need brushing at least weekly. Both are bred for cold mountains and struggle in heat, so they need shade, water and rest in summer.

### Growth and health

Puppies grow enormously fast, gaining several kilos a month at their peak. Feed a giant-breed puppy food, keep them lean, and protect their joints from jumping, stairs and long walks until the growth plates close at around 18 months. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat, heart disease (dilated cardiomyopathy), eyelid problems and bone cancer. Like most giant breeds, Saint Bernards live a short life, usually 8–10 years.
