The Miniature American Shepherd was developed in California in the late 1960s by breeding small Australian Shepherds together. The aim was a smaller dog with the same looks, intelligence and herding instinct. For years these dogs were called "Miniature Australian Shepherds" or "Mini Aussies". In 2015 the AKC recognised them as a separate breed under the name Miniature American Shepherd. Popular with horse people, it's a lively, loyal and clever companion.

### What a Mini American Shepherd's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 14–18 in (35.5–45.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 13–17 in (33–43 cm) for females. Weights range from about 9 to 18 kg (20–40 lb). A typical Mini American reaches just below an adult's knee. It's compact and agile, a good size for most homes and cars. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=miniature-american-shepherd). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Mini American Shepherd vs Australian Shepherd

Next to an [Australian Shepherd](/compare/australian-shepherd-vs-miniature-american-shepherd/), a Mini American is about 14 cm shorter and roughly half the weight. Otherwise they look very similar, with the same merle colours, bobtails and striking eyes. The Hungarian [Puli](/compare/miniature-american-shepherd-vs-puli/) is about the same size but looks completely different, with its corded coat.

### Energy and herding

Mini Americans are smaller, but they're still true herding dogs. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental work, such as herding, agility, obedience or trick training. Bored Mini Americans may herd children, cats or bicycles, and nip at heels. They're devoted to their family, often reserved with strangers, and very trainable.

### Coat and colours

The medium-length double coat sheds a lot, especially seasonally, and needs weekly brushing. Like Aussies, Mini Americans come in blue merle, red merle, black and red, often with white and copper markings. Two merle dogs should never be bred together, because "double merle" puppies are at high risk of deafness and blindness.

### Health

Many Mini Americans carry the MDR1 gene variant, which makes them sensitive to some common medicines. A DNA test is worth doing, and your vet should know the result. Eye diseases, hip dysplasia and epilepsy are also known. Mini Americans often live 12–13 years or more.
