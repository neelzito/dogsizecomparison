The Kerry Blue Terrier comes from County Kerry in south-west Ireland, where it was used as an all-round farm dog: hunting rats, badgers and otters, herding livestock and guarding the home. It became a symbol of Irish nationalism in the early 20th century, and Michael Collins owned a famous Kerry Blue. The breed's most striking feature is its soft, wavy blue-grey coat, which is born black and gradually lightens.

### What a Kerry Blue's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–19.5 in (45.5–49.5 cm) at the shoulder for males, with females slightly smaller. Males weigh about 15–18 kg (33–40 lb), and females a little less. A Kerry Blue reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's square, athletic and well-muscled. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=kerry-blue-terrier). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Kerry Blue vs Soft Coated Wheaten

The [Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier](/compare/kerry-blue-terrier-vs-soft-coated-wheaten-terrier/), another Irish terrier, is about the same size. The two have a similar soft, wavy coat, but the Wheaten is wheat-coloured and usually more easy-going with other dogs. The [American Pit Bull Terrier](/compare/american-pit-bull-terrier-vs-kerry-blue-terrier/) is a little taller and clearly heavier.

### Born black, turning blue

Kerry Blue puppies are born black. Their coat gradually clears to blue-grey, a process that can take up to 18 months or longer. Adult shades range from deep slate to light silver-blue.

### Temperament and exercise

Kerry Blues are clever, lively and loyal, affectionate with their family and good watchdogs. Like many terriers, they can be strong-willed and may not get on with other dogs, especially of the same sex, so early socialisation is important. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation and enjoy agility, herding and earthdog trials.

### Coat and health

The soft, single coat sheds very little but grows continuously. It needs brushing a couple of times a week and a trim every 4–8 weeks. Known health problems include eye problems, skin cysts, and cerebellar abiotrophy (a nerve disease) and factor XI deficiency (a bleeding disorder), which have DNA tests. Kerry Blues often live 12–15 years.
