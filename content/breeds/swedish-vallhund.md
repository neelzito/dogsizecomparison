The Swedish Vallhund is an ancient Swedish cattle dog, often called the "Viking dog", whose ancestors herded cattle on farms for over a thousand years. It's low, sturdy and agile, and it drives cattle by nipping at their heels. In the 1940s, Count Björn von Rosen helped save it from extinction. It looks a little like a Corgi with a wolf-grey coat, and some Vallhunds are born with a natural bobtail.

### What a Vallhund's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 12.5–13.5 in (32–34.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 11.5–12.5 in (29–32 cm) for females, and Vallhunds weigh about 9–16 kg (20–35 lb). A Vallhund reaches just below an adult's knee. Because of its long body, it usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=swedish-vallhund). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Vallhund vs Pembroke Corgi

The [Pembroke Welsh Corgi](/compare/pembroke-welsh-corgi-vs-swedish-vallhund/) is a little shorter and about the same weight. The two may share ancestry, but nobody knows which came first.

### Temperament and exercise

Vallhunds are friendly, energetic and clever, alert watchdogs and vocal. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they excel at herding and agility.

### Coat and health

The double coat sheds heavily once or twice a year. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and retinopathy. Vallhunds often live 12–15 years.
