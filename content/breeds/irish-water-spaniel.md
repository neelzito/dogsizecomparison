The Irish Water Spaniel is the tallest of the spaniels and one of the oldest. It was developed in Ireland in the 19th century, largely by Justin McCarthy of Dublin, whose dog Boatswain is considered the breed's foundation. It was bred to retrieve ducks and geese from cold Irish rivers and marshes. With its tight, liver-coloured curls, a topknot of curls on its head and a smooth "rat tail", it's one of the most unusual-looking gundogs.

### What an Irish Water Spaniel's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 22–24 in (56–61 cm) at the shoulder and 55–68 lb (25–31 kg) for males, and 21–23 in (53.5–58.5 cm) and 45–58 lb (20.5–26.5 kg) for females. An IWS reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's strong, athletic and well-boned. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=irish-water-spaniel) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male.

### IWS vs Vizsla and GSP

The [Vizsla](/compare/irish-water-spaniel-vs-vizsla/) is about the same height and a little lighter. The [German Shorthaired Pointer](/compare/german-shorthaired-pointer-vs-irish-water-spaniel/) is a little taller and about the same weight. The IWS is often mistaken for a Poodle or a Curly-Coated Retriever, but it has its own topknot and bare, rat-like tail.

### Temperament and exercise

Irish Water Spaniels are clever, playful and clownish with their family, often reserved with strangers. They need plenty of daily exercise, especially swimming and retrieving, and mental work. They can be stubborn, so training should be consistent and fun.

### Coat and health

The curly coat sheds little but needs brushing and trimming every few weeks to prevent mats. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, hypothyroidism and sensitivity to some medicines. IWS often live 12–13 years.
