The American Foxhound is one of the oldest American breeds, descended from English foxhounds brought to Virginia in 1650 and later crossed with French hounds. George Washington bred foxhounds at Mount Vernon, and the American Foxhound is the state dog of Virginia. It's bred to hunt foxes in packs over long distances, often in rough country, and to give voice as it follows the scent.

### What an American Foxhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 22–25 in (56–63.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 21–24 in (53.5–61 cm) for females. Typical weights are about 27–32 kg (60–70 lb). An American Foxhound reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's lean, long-legged and athletic. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=american-foxhound) suggests a 48″ (122 cm) crate for an average male.

### American vs English Foxhound

The [English Foxhound](/compare/american-foxhound-vs-english-foxhound/) is about the same height and weight, but heavier-boned and more solid. The American is lighter, faster and leggier. Both share the same friendly pack temperament.

### Temperament and exercise

American Foxhounds are sweet-natured, easy-going and good with other dogs. They need a lot of exercise, ideally long runs, and their nose leads them, so recall is often unreliable. They have a loud, musical bay. Because they were bred to live in packs, many don't like being left alone.

### Care and health

The short, hard coat needs only weekly brushing. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and thrombocytopathy (a blood platelet disorder). American Foxhounds often live 11–13 years.
