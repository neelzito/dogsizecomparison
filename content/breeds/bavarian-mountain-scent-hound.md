The Bavarian Mountain Scent Hound (*Bayerischer Gebirgsschweisshund*) was developed in Bavaria in the late 19th century by crossing the heavy Hanoverian Scent Hound with lighter mountain hounds. Its specialty is blood tracking: following the trail of wounded deer and other game, sometimes many hours old, through steep mountain terrain, so hunters can find the animal. It's still mainly a working dog, kept by hunters and gamekeepers.

### What a Bavarian's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 47–52 cm at the shoulder for males and 44–48 cm for females, and Bavarians weigh about 17–30 kg (37–66 lb). A Bavarian reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's lean, light and agile. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bavarian-mountain-scent-hound).

### Bavarian vs Basset, GBGV and Elkhound

A [Basset Hound](/compare/basset-hound-vs-bavarian-mountain-scent-hound/) is much shorter and about the same weight. The [Grand Basset Griffon Vendéen](/compare/bavarian-mountain-scent-hound-vs-grand-basset-griffon-vendeen/) is shorter and lighter. The [Norwegian Elkhound](/compare/bavarian-mountain-scent-hound-vs-norwegian-elkhound/) is a little taller and heavier.

### Temperament and exercise

Bavarians are calm, loyal and devoted to their handler, often reserved with strangers. They need a lot of exercise and nose work. Without it, they become restless. Many breeders sell puppies only to hunters.

### Care and health

The short coat needs little grooming. Bavarians are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia. They often live 12–15 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 217 (Bayerischer Gebirgsschweisshund); Klub für Bayerische Gebirgsschweisshunde.*
