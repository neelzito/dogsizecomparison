The Portuguese Water Dog (*Cão de Água Português*) worked for centuries alongside fishermen on the coast of Portugal, especially in the Algarve. It herded fish into nets, retrieved lost tackle and broken nets from the water, and carried messages between boats and the shore. As fishing modernised in the 20th century, the breed nearly died out. A Portuguese shipping magnate, Vasco Bensaude, helped save it from the 1930s onwards. It became world-famous when the Obama family brought two Portuguese Water Dogs, Bo and Sunny, to the White House.

### What a PWD's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 20–23 in (51–58.5 cm) at the shoulder and 42–60 lb (19–27 kg) for males, and 17–21 in (43–53.5 cm) and 35–50 lb (16–22.5 kg) for females. A PWD reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's a sturdy, muscular, athletic dog. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=portuguese-water-dog). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Lion clip or retriever clip

The PWD has a single coat, curly or wavy, that sheds very little, so it's popular with people who dislike dog hair. In the traditional **lion clip**, the hindquarters and muzzle are shaved and the rest left long, a style from the breed's working days. The **retriever clip** is an even, short trim all over. Either way, the coat needs brushing several times a week and a clip every 6–8 weeks. No dog is truly hypoallergenic.

### Temperament and exercise

PWDs are intelligent, energetic and affectionate, and they love water. They need plenty of daily exercise and mental work, and they excel at swimming, water work, agility and obedience. They're eager to please and usually easy to train, but bored PWDs can be mouthy, noisy or destructive. They're very people-oriented and don't like being left alone.

### Health

Known health problems include hip dysplasia, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), Addison's disease, and GM1 storage disease, a fatal inherited nerve disease for which a DNA test exists. Responsible breeders test for these. PWDs often live 11–13 years.
