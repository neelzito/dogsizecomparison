The Bolognese is a small white Italian companion dog from the Bichon family, named after the city of Bologna. It was a favourite of Italian nobility during the Renaissance, and the Medici family gave Bolognese dogs as gifts to other royal courts. Unlike the Bichon Frise's curly coat, the Bolognese has a coat of loose, fluffy ringlets. It's a calm, devoted and quiet companion.

### What a Bolognese's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for about 27–30 cm at the shoulder for males and 25–28 cm for females, and Bolognese weigh about 2.5–4.5 kg (6–10 lb). A Bolognese reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It suits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bolognese). It easily fits the airline cabin limit. ### Bolognese vs Bichon, Maltese and Havanese

The Bolognese is closely related to the Bichon Frise, the Maltese and the Havanese. It's usually a little smaller and calmer than the Bichon. Next to a [Miniature Pinscher](/compare/bolognese-vs-miniature-pinscher/), it's about the same size, with a completely different coat and temperament.

### Temperament

Bolognese are gentle, calm and very attached to their people, often more reserved than the Bichon. They need short daily walks and play. They can be shy with strangers, and many don't like being left alone.

### Coat and health

The fluffy white coat sheds very little but mats easily, so it needs brushing several times a week. Bolognese are generally healthy. Known problems include loose kneecaps, dental problems and eye conditions. They often live 12–14 years.
