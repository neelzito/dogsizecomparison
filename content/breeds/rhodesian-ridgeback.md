The Rhodesian Ridgeback was developed in southern Africa by European settlers, who crossed their dogs with the half-wild, ridged dogs of the Khoikhoi people. They needed a tough, adaptable dog that could guard farms, cope with heat and little water, and hunt. In what was then Rhodesia (now Zimbabwe), packs of these dogs were famously used to track lions and hold them at bay until the hunters arrived, which earned the breed the nickname "African Lion Hound". The first breed standard was written in Bulawayo in 1922.

### The ridge

The breed's trademark is the ridge: a strip of hair along the back that grows in the opposite direction to the rest of the coat, with two matching whorls ("crowns") at the front. It is caused by a genetic variant, and some Ridgebacks are born without it. The same variant is linked to dermoid sinus, a tube-like defect under the skin along the spine that can become infected. Breeders check puppies for it at birth, and it can be removed surgically.

### What a Ridgeback's size means at home

The AKC standard gives a height range and one ideal weight: males stand about 64–69 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and should weigh about 38.5 kg (85 lb). Females stand about 61–66 cm and weigh about 32 kg (70 lb). Ridgebacks are muscular and athletic. They need a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=rhodesian-ridgeback).

### Ridgeback vs Vizsla

At a glance, a Ridgeback can look like a large [Vizsla](/compare/vizsla-vs-rhodesian-ridgeback/), but it is taller and much heavier, with a darker wheaten coat and, of course, the ridge.

### Temperament and training

Ridgebacks are loyal, dignified and affectionate with their families, and reserved with strangers. They're independent and intelligent, and they can be stubborn, so they need consistent, positive training from puppyhood. They need daily exercise and like to run, but they're usually calm at home. Their prey drive is strong, and they're good at stealing food off kitchen counters.

### Care and health

The short coat needs very little grooming. Known health problems besides dermoid sinus include hip and elbow dysplasia, hypothyroidism and degenerative myelopathy, a spinal cord disease. Ridgebacks often live 10–12 years.
