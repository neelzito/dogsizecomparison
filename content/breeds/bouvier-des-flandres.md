The Bouvier des Flandres comes from Flanders, the region spanning northern France and Belgium. Farmers used it as an all-round farm dog: driving cattle (*bouvier* means "cowherd"), guarding the farm and pulling carts. During the First World War, it served as a messenger and ambulance dog, and the breed nearly vanished as the fighting destroyed Flanders. It was rebuilt from a few survivors, especially a dog called Nic de Sottegem. President Ronald Reagan's Bouvier, Lucky, briefly made the breed famous in the US. Today it's a calm, loyal and powerful working dog.

### What a Bouvier's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24.5–27.5 in (62–70 cm) at the shoulder for males and 23.5–26.5 in (59.5–67.5 cm) for females. Bouviers weigh about 32–50 kg (70–110 lb). A Bouvier reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's compact, square and very strong, and its shaggy coat makes it look even bigger. It needs a large boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. ### Bouvier vs Beauceron

The French [Beauceron](/compare/beauceron-vs-bouvier-des-flandres/) is a similar size, but it's short-coated, usually black and tan, and more athletic in build. Both are powerful herding and guardian breeds.

### Temperament and exercise

Bouviers are calm, steady and devoted to their family, and naturally protective. They're intelligent and willing but can be independent and dominant, so they need consistent, confident training and early socialisation. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they excel at herding, tracking, obedience and protection sports. They're happiest as part of family life, not left alone in a kennel.

### Coat care

The rough, tousled double coat sheds little but needs a lot of care: thorough brushing a couple of times a week and trimming or hand-stripping every 6–8 weeks. The beard collects food and water and needs regular cleaning. The coat traps dirt and leaves, so expect mess indoors.

### Health

Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, hypothyroidism, eye diseases (glaucoma, cataracts), and laryngeal paralysis, an inherited condition affecting the voice box. Bouviers often live 10–12 years.
