The Komondor is a large Hungarian livestock guardian, probably brought to Hungary centuries ago by the Cumans, a nomadic people from the east. Hungarian shepherds used it to guard flocks against wolves, bears and thieves, and its long, white, corded coat let it blend in with the sheep and protected it from bites and weather. It's often called the "king of Hungarian sheepdogs" and is a Hungarian national treasure. Its coat makes it one of the most recognisable dogs in the world.

### What a Komondor's size means at home

The AKC standard sets minimum heights: 27.5 in (70 cm) for males and 25.5 in (65 cm) for females. Males weigh at least 100 lb (45.5 kg) and often more, and females at least 80 lb (36.5 kg). A Komondor reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and its coat makes it look even bigger. It's heavy enough to put it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=komondor)) and a large, securely fenced property.

### Komondor vs Great Pyrenees and Black Russian

The [Great Pyrenees](/compare/great-pyrenees-vs-komondor/) is about the same size, with a long, flat white coat. The [Black Russian Terrier](/compare/black-russian-terrier-vs-komondor/) is a little shorter and lighter. The Komondor is a giant cousin of the much smaller Puli, which also has a corded coat.

### The cords

Komondor puppies have soft, fluffy coats. From about 9 months, the coat starts to form cords, which grow longer over the years and can reach the ground. Cords must be separated by hand regularly to stop them matting into a solid mat. Bathing takes a long time, and drying a full coat can take a day or more. The coat should never be brushed. Many pet owners keep it shorter.

### A guardian, not a playmate

Komondors are calm, dignified and devoted to their family, but very protective and suspicious of strangers. They're independent, bred to guard without instruction. They need early socialisation, an experienced owner and a secure property. They aren't suitable for busy households or homes with frequent visitors.

### Health

Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat and skin problems under the coat. Komondors often live 10–12 years.
