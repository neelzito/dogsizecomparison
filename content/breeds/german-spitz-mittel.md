The German Spitz is one of Europe's oldest dog types, kept for centuries as a farm and house watchdog in Germany. The FCI recognises it as one breed in five sizes: the Wolfspitz (or Keeshond), the Giant Spitz, the Medium Spitz (*Mittelspitz*), the Miniature Spitz (*Kleinspitz*) and the Toy Spitz (the Pomeranian). This page shows the Medium Spitz, a lively, alert and devoted companion.

### What a Medium German Spitz's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 30–38 cm at the shoulder, with weight in proportion. Most weigh about 7–11.5 kg (15–25 lb). A Medium Spitz reaches just below an adult's knee. Its thick, stand-off coat makes it look round and fluffy. It suits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=german-spitz-mittel). Most are over the 8 kg (17.6 lb) cabin limit for dog and carrier on many airlines.

### German Spitz sizes and relatives

The Keeshond (Wolfspitz) is much bigger, and the Pomeranian (Toy Spitz) much smaller. The [Japanese Spitz](/compare/german-spitz-mittel-vs-japanese-spitz/), which descends from white German Spitz dogs, is about the same height and a little lighter, always white. Next to a [French Bulldog](/compare/french-bulldog-vs-german-spitz-mittel/), the Medium Spitz is a little taller and lighter.

### Temperament and exercise

Medium Spitzes are cheerful, alert and very attached to their family, and natural watchdogs that bark at anything new. They can be wary of strangers. They need daily walks and play, and they're clever and fairly easy to train. They don't like being left alone.

### Coat and health

The double coat sheds heavily once or twice a year and needs brushing a couple of times a week. Don't shave it. German Spitzes are generally healthy. Known problems include loose kneecaps and eye conditions. They often live 13–15 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 97 (German Spitz); Verein für Deutsche Spitze; AKC breed information.*
