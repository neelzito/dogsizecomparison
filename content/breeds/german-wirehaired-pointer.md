The German Wirehaired Pointer (*Deutsch Drahthaar*) was developed in Germany in the late 19th century. Hunters wanted a tough, versatile gundog that could work in any terrain and weather, point and retrieve birds, track wounded game, and handle cold water and thick brambles. Breeders crossed pointers with wire-haired breeds such as the Pudelpointer and Griffon. The result is the rough-coated cousin of the German Shorthaired Pointer, and one of the most popular hunting dogs in Germany today.

### What a GWP's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24–26 in (61–66 cm) at the shoulder for males, with females smaller but not under 22 in (56 cm). GWPs weigh about 22.5–32 kg (50–70 lb). A GWP reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's muscular and athletic, and it needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. ### GWP vs German Shorthaired Pointer

The two are similar in size and build. The German Shorthaired Pointer has a short, smooth coat, while the GWP has a harsh, wiry, water-resistant coat with a distinctive beard and eyebrows. GWPs are often a little more reserved with strangers and more protective than GSPs.

### Exercise: lots of it

GWPs are working hunting dogs with huge stamina. They need at least one to two hours of hard exercise every day, such as running, swimming, retrieving, hunting or dog sports, plus mental challenges. They suit active owners, ideally with access to the countryside. Without enough to do, they can become noisy or destructive. Their hunting drive is strong, so small pets and wildlife may need protecting.

### Coat care

The wiry coat sheds little and dries quickly. It needs weekly brushing, and occasional hand-stripping helps it stay harsh. The beard should be cleaned after meals. The coat protects well against cold and brambles but holds burrs, so check it after walks.

### Health

GWPs are generally hardy and long-lived for their size, often 14–16 years. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, eye conditions, and von Willebrand's disease, a bleeding disorder for which a DNA test exists.
