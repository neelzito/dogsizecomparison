The Boerboel is a large South African mastiff, developed by Dutch, German and French settlers from the 17th century onwards. On isolated farms, they needed a powerful dog to guard the homestead against leopards, hyenas, baboons and intruders. They crossed their own mastiff-type dogs with local and imported breeds. The name means "farmer's dog" in Afrikaans. Boerboels remained mostly working farm dogs until breed clubs were formed in the 1980s. The AKC recognised the breed in 2015.

### What a Boerboel's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24–27 in (61–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22–25 in (56–63.5 cm) for females. Boerboels typically weigh about 68–90.5 kg (150–200 lb). That makes it one of the heaviest breeds on this site, even though it isn't especially tall: it reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's extremely muscular and solid. It needs a large vehicle, a ramp, and a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=boerboel).

### Boerboel vs Tosa

The Japanese [Tosa](/compare/boerboel-vs-tosa/) is taller on average but usually lighter. Both are powerful guardian breeds that need very experienced owners.

### Laws and restrictions

Boerboels are banned or restricted in some countries, regions and cities, and some insurers and landlords have rules for them. Check the current laws where you live, and where you plan to travel or move, before getting one.

### Temperament and training

A well-bred Boerboel is calm, confident and devoted to its family, often gentle with children it knows. But it's a serious guardian: territorial, protective and wary of strangers, and it may not tolerate other dogs. Its strength means training and socialisation from puppyhood are essential. An untrained 80 kg dog is dangerous to handle. It isn't a breed for first-time owners. It needs daily exercise and a secure, fenced property.

### Care and health

The short coat needs little grooming. Puppies grow very fast, so feed a giant-breed puppy food, keep them lean and avoid hard exercise until the growth plates close. Known health problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, eyelid problems, bloat and heart disease. Boerboels often live 9–11 years.
