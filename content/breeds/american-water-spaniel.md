The American Water Spaniel was developed in the 19th century around the Great Lakes, especially in Wisconsin, where hunters needed a compact retriever that could work from small boats, flush birds and retrieve ducks from cold water. It's the state dog of Wisconsin. With its dense, curly liver-brown coat, it looks like a small Irish Water Spaniel, its likely ancestor.

### What an AWS's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 15–18 in (38–45.5 cm) at the shoulder, with weights of about 30–45 lb (13.5–20.5 kg) for males and 25–40 lb (11.5–18 kg) for females. An AWS reaches just below an adult's knee. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=american-water-spaniel). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### AWS vs Boykin and Sussex Spaniel

The [Boykin Spaniel](/compare/american-water-spaniel-vs-boykin-spaniel/) is about the same size and a little lighter. The [Sussex Spaniel](/compare/american-water-spaniel-vs-sussex-spaniel/) is shorter but heavier and longer-bodied.

### Temperament and exercise

American Water Spaniels are eager, friendly and energetic, devoted to their family and sometimes reserved with strangers. They need plenty of daily exercise and love swimming and retrieving.

### Coat and health

The curly, oily coat sheds moderately and needs weekly brushing. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and eye conditions. They often live 10–14 years.
