The Azawakh is a sighthound from the Sahel region of West Africa, named after the Azawakh valley in today's Mali and Niger. Tuareg and other nomadic peoples have kept it for centuries to hunt gazelles and hares, and to guard their camps. It's extraordinarily lean and long-legged, built for speed and endurance in desert heat. Azawakhs reached Europe in the 1970s, and the AKC recognised the breed in 2019.

### What an Azawakh's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 25–29 in (63.5–73.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 23.5–27.5 in (59.5–70 cm) for females. Azawakhs weigh only about 20–25 kg (44–55 lb) for males and 15–20 kg (33–44 lb) for females. An Azawakh reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's so light for its height that its weight puts it in the medium size class on this site, and its bones and muscles are clearly visible under the skin. Because of its height, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=azawakh) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

### Azawakh vs Ibizan Hound and Sloughi

The [Ibizan Hound](/compare/azawakh-vs-ibizan-hound/) is a little shorter and about the same weight. The Sloughi, another African sighthound, is similar in height but less extremely lean.

### Temperament and exercise

Azawakhs are loyal, sensitive and devoted to their family, but aloof and suspicious with strangers, a legacy of their work as camp guardians. They need early, careful socialisation and gentle, patient training. Like all sighthounds, they have a strong urge to chase and need regular chances to run in a large, secure area. They love warmth and dislike cold and rain.

### Care and health

The short, fine coat needs almost no grooming, but it gives little protection from the cold, so a coat is essential in winter. Known health problems include heart disease, autoimmune conditions, seizures and sensitivity to anaesthesia, as in other sighthounds. Azawakhs often live 12–15 years.
