The Black and Tan Coonhound is an American scent hound developed in the Appalachian and Blue Ridge mountains to hunt raccoons at night. It follows the trail with its nose and then "trees" the raccoon, baying at the foot of the tree until the hunter arrives. Its ancestors include the Bloodhound and the black-and-tan foxhounds of colonial America. In 1945 it became the first coonhound breed recognised by the AKC.

### What a Coonhound's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 25–27 in (63.5–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 23–25 in (58.5–63.5 cm) for females. Weights range widely, from about 29.5 to 50 kg (65–110 lb). A Black and Tan reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's tall, powerful and long-eared. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. ### Black and Tan vs Bloodhound and Ridgeback

The [Bloodhound](/compare/black-and-tan-coonhound-vs-bloodhound/) is about the same height but heavier, with much looser skin and deeper wrinkles. The [Rhodesian Ridgeback](/compare/black-and-tan-coonhound-vs-rhodesian-ridgeback/) is about the same height and weight, but a very different dog in coat and temperament.

### Temperament and exercise

Black and Tans are easy-going, friendly and gentle at home, and usually good with children and other dogs. Outdoors, their nose takes over. They need long daily walks, and many can't be trusted off the lead near scents. They have a loud, deep bay, which neighbours may notice.

### Care and health

The short, dense coat needs weekly brushing. The long, low-hanging ears need regular cleaning to prevent infections. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, ear infections and eye problems. Black and Tans often live 10–12 years.
