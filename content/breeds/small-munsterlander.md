The Small Munsterlander (*Kleiner Münsterländer*) is a German versatile gundog from the Münster region, developed in the early 20th century from old German long-haired bird dogs. Despite its name, it isn't a small version of the Large Munsterlander. The two have different histories. It points, retrieves and tracks, and it's a popular all-round hunting companion in Germany and Scandinavia.

### What a Small Munsterlander's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 50–56 cm at the shoulder, and Small Munsterlanders weigh about 18–27 kg (40–60 lb). One reaches roughly an adult's knee. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=small-munsterlander). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Small Munsterlander vs Wirehaired Pointing Griffon

The [Wirehaired Pointing Griffon](/compare/small-munsterlander-vs-wirehaired-pointing-griffon/) is a little taller and heavier, with a wiry coat. The Small Munsterlander is brown and white or brown roan, with a long, feathered coat.

### Temperament and exercise

Small Munsterlanders are friendly, eager and affectionate, very good with children. They need a lot of exercise and mental work, ideally hunting or retrieving.

### Coat and health

The long coat needs brushing a couple of times a week. Known health problems include hip dysplasia. They often live 12–14 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 102 (Kleiner Münsterländer); Verband Kleiner Münsterländer.*
