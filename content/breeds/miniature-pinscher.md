The Miniature Pinscher, or "Min Pin", is often called a miniature Doberman, but it's actually the older breed. Small pinschers were kept on German farms for centuries to catch rats, and the Min Pin (*Zwergpinscher* in German) was well established by the time the Doberman was created in the 1890s. It probably descends from the German Pinscher, with some Italian Greyhound and Dachshund in its background. Nicknamed "the King of Toys", it is a bold, energetic and confident little dog.

### What a Min Pin's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 10–12.5 in (25.5–32 cm) at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult. Min Pins weigh only about 3.5–4.5 kg (8–10 lb). They are compact, muscular and fine-boned, and they move with a distinctive high-stepping "hackney" gait. A Min Pin fits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=miniature-pinscher).

### Flying with a Min Pin

A Min Pin is a good size for cabin travel. Even with a carrier, it stays well under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set. Check carrier sizes with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=miniature-pinscher).

### Min Pin vs Doberman and Chihuahua

Despite the similar colours, a Min Pin isn't a small Doberman. The Doberman is more than twice as tall and about ten times as heavy, and the two were developed separately. Next to a Chihuahua, a Min Pin is taller and leggier, with a longer muzzle and a more terrier-like personality.

### Temperament and exercise

Min Pins are fearless, curious and full of energy. They're quick learners but can be stubborn, so short, fun, reward-based training sessions work best. They make alert, noisy watchdogs. They need daily walks and play, and they're famous escape artists that can squeeze through small gaps and jump surprisingly high. A secure garden and a reliable harness are essential. Many have a strong prey drive towards small animals.

### Care and health

The short, smooth coat needs almost no grooming. Min Pins feel the cold, so a coat or jumper helps in winter. Known health problems include loose kneecaps, Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (a hip condition), eye diseases and dental problems. In Europe, the ears and tail are left natural. In the US they're sometimes still cropped and docked. Min Pins are long-lived, often 12–16 years.
