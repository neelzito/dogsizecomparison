The Skye Terrier comes from the Isle of Skye in the Scottish Hebrides, where it hunted foxes, otters and badgers among the rocks. Its long, low body and long, flowing coat make it one of the most distinctive terriers. It was very fashionable in Victorian times, when Queen Victoria kept Skyes. Greyfriars Bobby, the famous Edinburgh dog who is said to have guarded his master's grave for 14 years, is often described as a Skye Terrier. The breed is now rare, and The Kennel Club lists it as vulnerable.

### What a Skye's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 10 in (25.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 9.5 in (24 cm) for females, and the dog should be twice as long as it is tall. Skyes are heavy for their height: the figures on this page show about 16–20.5 kg (35–45 lb) for males and 11–14 kg for females, putting them in the medium size class on this site. A Skye reaches around mid-shin on an adult. Because of its length, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=skye-terrier) suggests a 30″ (76 cm) crate.

### Skye vs Dandie Dinmont and Scottish Terrier

The [Dandie Dinmont](/compare/dandie-dinmont-terrier-vs-skye-terrier/) is about the same height but much lighter, with a topknot and a shorter coat. The Scottish Terrier is about the same height and much lighter.

### Temperament and exercise

Skyes are loyal, brave and often devoted to one person, and reserved with strangers. They need daily walks and play. Like all long-backed dogs, they should be kept lean and discouraged from jumping.

### Coat and health

The long, straight coat needs brushing several times a week. Known health problems include back problems and, in puppies, damage to the growth plates of the front legs from too much exercise or jumping. Skyes often live 12–14 years.
