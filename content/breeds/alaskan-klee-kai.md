The Alaskan Klee Kai was developed in Alaska in the 1970s by Linda Spurlin, who wanted a small companion dog that looked like a Siberian Husky. Its foundation included Alaskan and Siberian Huskies, with Schipperke and American Eskimo Dog added to reduce size. The name comes from Inuit words often translated as "small dog". For years only a few breeders worked with it. The UKC recognised it in 1997, and it has since become a popular "mini Husky" for people who love the look but can't fit a full-size sled dog into their lives.

### Three sizes

The Klee Kai comes in three sizes, measured at the shoulder:

- **Toy:** up to 13 in (33 cm)
- **Miniature:** over 13 in up to 15 in (33–38 cm)
- **Standard:** over 15 in up to 17.5 in (38–44.5 cm)

Weights range from about 2.7 to 11.5 kg (6–25 lb). A typical Klee Kai reaches just below an adult's knee. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=alaskan-klee-kai) suggests a 30″ (76 cm) crate for an average dog, and a Toy may need a smaller one.

### Can a Klee Kai fly in the cabin?

Toy and small Miniature Klee Kai often can, staying within the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dog and carrier. Standards usually won't. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=alaskan-klee-kai).

### Klee Kai vs Siberian Husky

A Klee Kai looks like a [miniature Siberian Husky](/compare/alaskan-klee-kai-vs-siberian-husky/), standing about two-thirds of the Husky's height and weighing less than a third as much. But it's a different breed with a different temperament: more reserved with strangers, more alert, and more likely to bark. The Japanese [Kai Ken](/compare/alaskan-klee-kai-vs-kai-ken/), despite the similar name, is an unrelated breed and taller.

### Temperament and care

Klee Kai are intelligent, energetic and very attached to their family, but often shy or wary with strangers, so early socialisation matters. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation, and they can be vocal, "talking" and howling like Huskies. Their double coat sheds heavily, especially once or twice a year. Known health problems include loose kneecaps, heart conditions, factor VII deficiency (a bleeding disorder), and liver and eye problems. Klee Kai often live 13–16 years.
