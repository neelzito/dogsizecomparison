The Dandie Dinmont Terrier comes from the Scottish Borders, where it hunted otters, badgers and foxes. It's the only dog breed named after a fictional character: Dandie Dinmont, a farmer in Sir Walter Scott's novel *Guy Mannering* (1815), who kept a pack of these terriers. With its long, low body, large domed head and silky topknot, it looks unlike any other terrier. It's now rare, and The Kennel Club lists it as a vulnerable native breed.

### What a Dandie's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 8–11 in (20.5–28 cm) at the shoulder and 18–24 lb (8–11 kg). A Dandie reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's long, low and sturdy, much longer than it is tall. Because of its length, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=dandie-dinmont-terrier) suggests a 36″ (91 cm) crate for an average adult. Most Dandies are over the cabin limit for dog and carrier.

### Dandie vs Scottish and Skye Terrier

The [Scottish Terrier](/compare/dandie-dinmont-terrier-vs-scottish-terrier/) is about the same height and weight but has a long, narrow head and a harsh black coat. The [Skye Terrier](/compare/dandie-dinmont-terrier-vs-skye-terrier/) is about the same height but heavier, with a long, flowing coat. The Dandie comes in "pepper" (grey) or "mustard" (fawn).

### Temperament and exercise

Dandies are calm, dignified and affectionate, gentler than most terriers but still independent and brave. They need daily walks and play. They can be stubborn, so patient training works best.

### Coat and health

The coat is a mix of soft and harsh hair, with a silky topknot. It needs regular brushing and hand-stripping. Like other long-backed breeds, Dandies can be prone to disc problems (IVDD), so keep them lean and discourage jumping. Glaucoma is also known. Dandies often live 12–15 years.
