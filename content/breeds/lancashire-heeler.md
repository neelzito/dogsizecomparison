The Lancashire Heeler is a small cattle dog from the Ormskirk area of Lancashire in north-west England, where it drove cattle to market by nipping at their heels and caught rats on farms. It probably descends from Welsh Corgis and Manchester Terriers. It's usually black and tan, low and sturdy, and it's now rare: The Kennel Club lists it as a vulnerable native breed.

### What a Lancashire Heeler's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 10–12 in (25.5–30.5 cm) at the shoulder, and Heelers weigh about 4–7.5 kg (9–17 lb). A Heeler reaches around mid-shin on an adult. Because of its long body, it usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=lancashire-heeler). A light Heeler may fit the cabin limit. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=lancashire-heeler).

### Lancashire Heeler vs Pembroke Corgi

A [Pembroke Welsh Corgi](/compare/lancashire-heeler-vs-pembroke-welsh-corgi/) is about the same height but roughly twice as heavy.

### Temperament and exercise

Heelers are lively, clever and affectionate, with a herding and ratting instinct. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation. Some "smile" by pulling back their lips when happy.

### Care and health

The short coat needs little grooming. Known health problems include primary lens luxation, for which a DNA test exists, and collie eye anomaly. Heelers often live 12–15 years.
