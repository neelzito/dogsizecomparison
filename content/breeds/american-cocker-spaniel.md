The American Cocker Spaniel (just called the Cocker Spaniel in the US) descends from English Cocker Spaniels brought to America in the 19th century. American breeders gradually developed a smaller dog with a rounder head, a shorter muzzle and a much fuller coat. The AKC recognised the English and American Cockers as separate breeds in 1946. The American Cocker was the most popular breed in the US for many years in the mid-20th century, helped by Disney's *Lady and the Tramp* (1955), whose Lady is an American Cocker.

### What an American Cocker's size means at home

The AKC standard sets ideal heights of 15 in (38 cm) for males and 14 in (35.5 cm) for females, with a small tolerance. Most stand about 34.5–39.5 cm at the shoulder, just below an adult's knee. Males weigh about 11.5–13.5 kg (25–30 lb), females 9–11.5 kg (20–25 lb). It suits flats and houses and usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=american-cocker-spaniel). It is too heavy for an airline cabin.

### American vs English Cocker

The two are separate breeds. The [English Cocker Spaniel](/compare/english-cocker-spaniel-vs-american-cocker-spaniel/) is taller and a little heavier, with a longer muzzle, a flatter head and less coat. It is still widely used as a working gundog. The American Cocker is now mostly a companion and show dog. In the UK and Europe, "Cocker Spaniel" usually means the English breed.

### Coat care

The American Cocker's long, silky coat is its most striking feature and its biggest chore. It needs brushing several times a week and professional grooming every 6–8 weeks. Many pet owners choose a shorter clip. The long, low-set ears trap moisture, so check and clean them weekly to prevent infection, which is common in the breed.

### Temperament and health

American Cockers are gentle, cheerful and affectionate, and usually good with children. They enjoy walks and play but don't need as much exercise as working spaniels. Known health problems include ear infections, eye diseases (glaucoma, cataracts, progressive retinal atrophy), skin problems and autoimmune diseases. American Cockers often live 10–14 years.
