The Cane Corso is an Italian mastiff, a descendant of the Roman war and guard dogs. For centuries, Italian farmers used it to guard property and livestock, hunt wild boar and drive cattle, especially in southern Italy. As farming changed in the 20th century, the breed nearly died out. Italian enthusiasts revived it from the few remaining dogs in the 1970s and 80s, and it was recognised by the FCI in 1996 and the AKC in 2010. The name roughly means "guardian dog". Today the Cane Corso is a popular, powerful and loyal guardian that needs an experienced owner.

### What a Cane Corso's size means at home

A male Cane Corso stands about 64–70 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and females about 60–66 cm. The AKC standard only says weight should be in proportion to height. Typical weights are around 45–50 kg (99–110 lb) for males and 40–45 kg (88–99 lb) for females, and many dogs are heavier. That's heavy enough to put it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle and a 54″ (137 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=cane-corso). Its strength on the lead is considerable, so training from puppyhood is essential.

### Cane Corso vs Rottweiler, Mastiff and Dogo Argentino

The Cane Corso is [a little taller than the Rottweiler](/compare/cane-corso-vs-rottweiler/) but usually a little lighter, and it's more athletic and has a broader, square muzzle. The [English Mastiff](/compare/cane-corso-vs-mastiff-english/) is much bigger and heavier. The white [Dogo Argentino](/compare/cane-corso-vs-dogo-argentino/) is slightly smaller and lighter.

### Temperament and training

A well-bred Cane Corso is calm and affectionate with its family, gentle with its own children, and naturally suspicious of strangers. It was bred to guard, so it needs extensive socialisation from a young age and consistent, confident training. It isn't a good choice for first-time dog owners. Some countries and regions have special rules for large guarding breeds, such as licences, insurance or muzzle requirements. Check local laws first.

### Ears, growth and health

Cane Corsos are often pictured with cropped ears. Cropping is banned in the UK and much of Europe, so there they keep their natural drop ears. Puppies grow very fast, so feed a large-breed puppy food, keep them lean and protect their joints from hard exercise. Hip and elbow dysplasia, eyelid problems (entropion, ectropion) and bloat occur in the breed. Life expectancy is around 9–12 years.
