The Kangal Shepherd Dog is the national dog of Turkey, named after the town of Kangal in Sivas province in central Anatolia. Turkish shepherds have used it for centuries to protect flocks from wolves, bears and jackals. It typically wears a spiked collar for protection, and it patrols and guards with great independence. In Turkey the Kangal is a source of national pride: it has appeared on stamps and coins, and exports of pure Kangals have been restricted. Since the 1990s, Kangals have also been used in Namibia and Kenya to protect livestock from cheetahs.

### What a Kangal's size means at home

The FCI standard calls for 72–78 cm at the shoulder and about 48–60 kg for males, and 65–73 cm and about 40–50 kg for females. A Kangal reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's tall, powerful and athletic, heavy enough to put it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle, a 54″ (137 cm) crate (see the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=kangal-shepherd-dog)) and a large, securely fenced property.

### Kangal vs Anatolian Shepherd and Great Pyrenees

The [Anatolian Shepherd](/compare/kangal-shepherd-dog-vs-anatolian-shepherd-dog/) is very similar in size. The FCI renamed its Anatolian standard "Kangal Shepherd Dog" in 2018, but some clubs still keep the two apart. The Kangal is always a solid dun or fawn with a black mask. The [Great Pyrenees](/compare/great-pyrenees-vs-kangal-shepherd-dog/) is about the same height and a little lighter, with a long white coat.

### A livestock guardian

Kangals are calm, loyal and gentle with their family and animals, and very protective. They're independent, bred to work without commands, so training takes patience, and recall is often unreliable. They need a large, secure property, early socialisation and an experienced owner. They're happiest with a job, ideally guarding livestock. In some countries and regions, large guardian breeds are subject to special rules, so check local laws.

### Care and health

The short, dense double coat sheds heavily once or twice a year but otherwise needs little care. Kangals cope well with both heat and cold. They're generally hardy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, entropion and sensitivity to anaesthesia. Kangals often live 12–15 years.

*Sources: FCI standard No. 331 (Kangal Shepherd Dog); United Kennel Club breed standard; Cheetah Conservation Fund.*
