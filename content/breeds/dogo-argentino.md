The Dogo Argentino was developed in the 1920s in Córdoba, Argentina, by Dr Antonio Nores Martínez. He wanted a brave, athletic dog that could hunt big game such as wild boar and puma in packs, and also guard the family. He crossed the old Córdoba fighting dog with breeds including the Great Dane, Boxer, Bull Terrier, Pointer and Great Pyrenees. The result was a powerful, all-white dog with great stamina. The FCI recognised it in 1973 and the AKC in 2020.

### What a Dogo's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 24–26.5 in (61–67.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 24–25.5 in (61–65 cm) for females. Dogos weigh about 36.5–45.5 kg (80–100 lb). A Dogo reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's muscular, athletic and very strong. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=dogo-argentino).

### Dogo vs Cane Corso

The [Cane Corso](/compare/cane-corso-vs-dogo-argentino/) is a little taller and heavier, with a short, dark coat. The Dogo is always white, sometimes with one dark patch on the head.

### Laws and restrictions

The Dogo Argentino is banned or restricted in a number of countries. In the UK, for example, it is one of the types banned under the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991. Elsewhere, owners may need a licence, insurance or a muzzle in public. Rules change, so check the current laws where you live, and where you plan to travel or move, before getting one.

### Temperament

A well-bred Dogo is loyal, affectionate with its family and often playful. But it was bred to hunt big game and to guard, so it has a strong prey drive and can be protective and intolerant of other dogs. It needs an experienced owner, extensive early socialisation and consistent training, plus plenty of daily exercise and mental work. It isn't a good choice for first-time owners.

### Care and health

The short white coat needs very little grooming, but white dogs burn easily, so protect the nose and ears from strong sun. Deafness in one or both ears is common, as in other white breeds, so ask breeders whether puppies have been BAER hearing tested. Hip dysplasia, skin allergies and hypothyroidism also occur. Dogos often live 9–15 years.
