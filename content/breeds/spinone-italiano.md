The Spinone Italiano is an ancient Italian pointing breed, probably from the Piedmont region. It was bred as an all-round gundog to point, flush and retrieve game in thick cover and water. Its wiry coat protected it from thorns, and its name may come from the *pino*, a thorny bush. Unlike faster pointing breeds, the Spinone hunts at a steady, efficient trot. It's known for its gentle, soulful expression and its calm, patient nature.

### What a Spinone's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 23–27 in (58.5–68.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 22–25 in (56–63.5 cm) for females. Males weigh about 34–39 kg (75–86 lb), females about 28–32 kg (62–71 lb). A Spinone reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's square, muscular and solid. It needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. ### Spinone vs Bracco and Weimaraner

The [Bracco Italiano](/compare/bracco-italiano-vs-spinone-italiano/), the other Italian pointer, is a little shorter and lighter on average, with a short coat. The [Weimaraner](/compare/spinone-italiano-vs-weimaraner/) is a little taller and about the same weight.

### Temperament and exercise

Spinoni are gentle, patient and affectionate, very good with children and other dogs. They're less frantic than many gundogs but still need daily exercise. They're sensitive and respond best to gentle, positive training.

### Coat and health

The wiry coat needs weekly brushing and occasional hand-stripping. The beard collects food and water. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, bloat, and cerebellar ataxia, a nerve disease for which a DNA test exists. Spinoni often live 10–12 years.
