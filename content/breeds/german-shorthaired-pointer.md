The German Shorthaired Pointer was developed in Germany in the 19th century as a versatile hunting dog, one that could do everything: find and point game birds, retrieve on land and from water, track wounded game and even hunt rabbits and foxes. German breeders crossed older pointing dogs with English Pointers and other breeds to add speed and style. The result was one of the most capable all-round gundogs in the world. Today the GSP is a popular hunting companion and an energetic family dog for active homes.

### What a GSP's size means at home

A male GSP stands about 58–64 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 25–32 kg (55–70 lb). Females are about 53–59 cm and 20–27 kg. It is lean, muscular and athletic. It usually needs a 48″ (122 cm) crate (check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=german-shorthaired-pointer)) and a big boot.

### GSP vs Vizsla and Wirehaired Vizsla

The GSP is [a little taller and heavier than the Vizsla](/compare/german-shorthaired-pointer-vs-vizsla/), and usually liver or liver-and-white, often ticked or roan, rather than golden-rust. The [Wirehaired Vizsla](/compare/german-shorthaired-pointer-vs-wirehaired-vizsla/) is similar in size, with a wiry coat and a beard. The German Wirehaired Pointer is the GSP's rough-coated cousin.

### Exercise: a lot of it

GSPs are among the most energetic of all breeds. They need at least one to two hours of hard exercise every day: running, swimming, retrieving, hunting or dog sports. They make excellent partners for runners and cyclists once fully grown. Without enough exercise and mental work, many become restless, noisy or destructive. Their hunting instinct is strong, so cats, poultry and wildlife may need protecting, and recall needs a lot of training.

### Coat and care

The short, dense coat needs little more than a weekly brush, and it dries quickly after swimming. It sheds moderately all year. GSPs feel the cold more than long-coated dogs, so a coat helps on cold, wet days when they're not working.

### Health

GSPs are generally healthy. Known problems include hip and elbow dysplasia, bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus, as in other deep-chested breeds), some eye diseases, and a rare form of lupus (EFCLE) for which a DNA test exists. They often live 10–12 years.
