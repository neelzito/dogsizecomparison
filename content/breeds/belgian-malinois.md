The Belgian Malinois is named after the city of Mechelen (*Malines* in French) in Belgium, where it was developed as a herding dog in the late 19th century. It is one of four varieties of Belgian Shepherd, along with the Groenendael, the Tervuren and the Laekenois, which differ mainly in coat. Its intelligence, speed and drive made it a favourite with police and military units worldwide. A Malinois famously took part in the US Navy SEAL raid on Osama bin Laden in 2011. Today it is the top choice for police, protection and detection work, and a formidable dog-sports competitor.

### One breed or four?

The FCI treats the Belgian Shepherd as **one breed with four coat varieties**. The Malinois is the short-coated one. The AKC and some other clubs treat the varieties as separate breeds. The long-haired black [Groenendael](/compare/belgian-malinois-vs-belgian-sheepdog-groenendael/) is the same size as the Malinois, with a very different coat.

### What a Malinois's size means at home

A male Malinois stands about 61–66 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 27–36 kg (60–80 lb). Females are about 56–61 cm and 18–27 kg. It's lean, square and athletic. It usually needs a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=belgian-malinois).

### Malinois vs German Shepherd and Dutch Shepherd

The Malinois is often mistaken for a German Shepherd. It's about the [same height as a German Shepherd](/compare/german-shepherd-dog-vs-belgian-malinois/) but lighter and squarer, with a straighter back, a shorter coat and a more refined head. The [Dutch Shepherd](/compare/belgian-malinois-vs-dutch-shepherd/) is similar in build and work, but a little smaller and usually brindle.

### Not a pet for most homes

The Malinois is one of the most intense working breeds. It needs hours of exercise and, above all, demanding mental work every day: training, tracking, protection sports or similar. Without a real job, many Malinois become anxious, destructive or hard to handle. In the right hands, with experienced, active owners, it is a brilliant, loyal partner. For most families, a less driven breed is a better fit.

### Care and health

The short double coat sheds seasonally and needs little grooming. Malinois are generally healthy and long-lived for their size, often 12–14 years or more. Hip and elbow dysplasia, eye diseases and epilepsy are known in the breed, so ask breeders for the parents' health tests.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 15 (Chien de Berger Belge); American Belgian Malinois Club.*
