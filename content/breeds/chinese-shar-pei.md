The Chinese Shar-Pei is an ancient breed from southern China, used for centuries as a farm guardian, hunter and, sadly, fighting dog. Its name means "sand skin", after its short, harsh coat. Under Communist rule in the mid-20th century, dog keeping was discouraged, and the Shar-Pei almost died out. In 1978 Guinness listed it as the world's rarest breed. A Hong Kong breeder's appeal to American dog lovers in the 1970s saved it, and the breed became very popular in the US. Its deep wrinkles, hippo-like muzzle and blue-black tongue make it unmistakable.

### What a Shar-Pei's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–20 in (45.5–51 cm) at the shoulder and 45–60 lb (20.5–27 kg). A Shar-Pei reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's compact, square and solid, with a broad head. It suits most homes and usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=chinese-shar-pei). As a short-nosed breed, it isn't accepted in the hold by some airlines, and it's far too heavy for the cabin.

### Shar-Pei vs Chow Chow

The [Chow Chow](/compare/chinese-shar-pei-vs-chow-chow/), another ancient Chinese breed, is about the same height and a little heavier, with a thick mane instead of wrinkles. Both have a blue-black tongue and a dignified, independent temperament.

### Wrinkles and skin

Puppies are covered in wrinkles. Adults keep them mainly on the head, neck and shoulders. The folds need regular checking and gentle cleaning to prevent skin infections. Some Shar-Peis have the very short "horse coat", others the slightly longer "brush coat". Both shed seasonally.

### Temperament

Shar-Peis are loyal, calm and devoted to their family, but reserved or suspicious with strangers. They can be independent and stubborn, and some don't get on with other dogs. Early socialisation and consistent, calm training are important. They need only moderate exercise and are usually clean and easy to house-train.

### Health

The breed has several important health problems. Familial Shar-Pei fever causes recurring fevers and swollen hocks and can lead to kidney disease (amyloidosis). Eyelid problems (entropion) are common, and many puppies need surgery. Skin and ear infections, hypothyroidism and hip dysplasia are also known. Choose a breeder who selects for less extreme wrinkling and healthy eyes. Shar-Peis often live 8–12 years.
