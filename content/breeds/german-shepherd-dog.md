The German Shepherd Dog is one of the youngest of the famous working breeds. In 1899 Captain Max von Stephanitz registered a dog called Horand von Grafrath as the first German Shepherd and founded the breed club. His aim was a single, standardised German herding dog that was intelligent, obedient and tireless. As fewer sheep needed herding, he promoted the breed for police and military work, and that versatility made it famous. Today German Shepherds work as police, guide, search-and-rescue and detection dogs, and they are also popular family dogs. In the UK the breed was long called the Alsatian, a name chosen after the First World War to avoid the word "German".

### What a German Shepherd's size means at home

A male German Shepherd stands about 61–66 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh. Females are about 5 cm smaller and noticeably lighter. With their long body, a German Shepherd needs a big boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. See the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=german-shepherd-dog). It is a large dog that wants to be close to its people, so expect it to follow you from room to room.

### Show lines and working lines

German Shepherds vary a lot depending on their lines. **Show lines** (especially American and Canadian show dogs) are often bigger, with a more strongly sloping back and a deeply angled hindquarter. **Working lines** are usually more compact and straighter in the back, and often have even more drive. The sloping back of some show dogs has been criticised for affecting movement, and several kennel clubs now ask judges to favour sound, balanced dogs. Whatever the type, look for a dog that stands and moves easily.

### Exercise and training

This is a dog bred to work with people all day. It needs daily exercise plus mental work: training, tracking games, obedience or dog sports. A bored German Shepherd may become noisy, destructive or overprotective. Early socialisation helps it learn that visitors are normal.

### Growth and health

German Shepherds reach adult height at about 12–18 months and fill out until about 2–3 years. Their shedding is heavy and year-round, with two big seasonal moults. Hip and elbow dysplasia are known problems, so ask breeders for hip and elbow scores for both parents. Degenerative myelopathy, a spinal cord disease in older dogs, also occurs in the breed. A DNA test can show whether a puppy's parents carry the gene for it.
