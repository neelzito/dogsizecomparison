The Standard Schnauzer is the original Schnauzer, from which the Miniature and Giant breeds were later developed. It comes from southern Germany, where farmers used it for centuries as an all-round farm dog: catching rats, guarding the yard and travelling with carts to market. Dogs of this type appear in paintings by Albrecht Dürer and Rembrandt. The name comes from the German *Schnauze*, "snout", after its famous bearded muzzle. Today the Standard Schnauzer is a clever, lively and loyal companion.

### What a Standard Schnauzer's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18.5–19.5 in (47–49.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 17.5–18.5 in (44.5–47 cm) for females. Standards weigh about 16–22.5 kg (35–50 lb) for males and 13.5–20.5 kg (30–45 lb) for females. A Standard reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's square, robust and medium-sized. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=standard-schnauzer). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Three Schnauzer sizes

The [Miniature Schnauzer](/compare/miniature-schnauzer-vs-standard-schnauzer/) is much smaller, about 15 cm shorter and less than half the weight. The [Giant Schnauzer](/compare/standard-schnauzer-vs-giant-schnauzer/) is much bigger, about 19 cm taller and twice the weight. All three share the wiry coat, beard and eyebrows, but they are separate breeds, and the Standard sits in the middle in size and energy.

### Temperament and exercise

Standard Schnauzers are intelligent, spirited and devoted to their family. They make good watchdogs, alert and wary of strangers without being aggressive. They need daily exercise and mental work, and they excel at obedience, agility and tracking. They can be headstrong, so consistent training and early socialisation are important. A bored Standard Schnauzer may find its own jobs.

### Coat and health

The harsh, wiry double coat sheds little. Show dogs are hand-stripped, and pets are often clipped every 6–8 weeks. The beard needs regular cleaning. Standard Schnauzers are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, eye conditions and hypothyroidism. They're long-lived, often 13–16 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard and breed history; FCI standard No. 182 (Schnauzer); Standard Schnauzer Club of America.*
