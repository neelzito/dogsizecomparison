The Plott Hound is named after Johannes Plott, a German immigrant who brought hunting dogs to North Carolina around 1750. The Plott family bred these dogs for generations to hunt bears and wild boar in the Great Smoky Mountains. Unlike most American coonhounds, which descend from English foxhounds, the Plott has German roots. It's the state dog of North Carolina, and it's known for its brindle coat and bold, determined nature.

### What a Plott's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 20–25 in (51–63.5 cm) at the shoulder and 50–60 lb (22.5–27 kg) for males, and 20–23 in (51–58.5 cm) and 40–55 lb (18–25 kg) for females. A Plott reaches roughly an adult's knee. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=plott-hound) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male.

### Plott vs Pharaoh Hound and Thai Ridgeback

The [Pharaoh Hound](/compare/pharaoh-hound-vs-plott-hound/) is taller and a little lighter. The [Thai Ridgeback](/compare/plott-hound-vs-thai-ridgeback/) is about the same size.

### Temperament and exercise

Plotts are loyal, brave and intelligent, affectionate with their family. They need a lot of exercise, and their nose and hunting drive are strong. They have a loud, ringing bay.

### Care and health

The short, glossy coat needs only weekly brushing. Known health problems include bloat and hip dysplasia. Plotts often live 12–14 years.
