The Puli is a Hungarian herding dog with a history of more than a thousand years. Its ancestors probably arrived with the Magyars, who settled in the Carpathian Basin in the 9th century. Hungarian shepherds used the small, agile Puli to herd sheep, while the large Komondor guarded the flock. The Puli is famous for its corded coat, which forms natural dreadlocks that protected it from weather and wolves. It's quick, clever and full of energy, with a bouncy, springy movement.

### What a Puli's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 17 in (43 cm) at the shoulder for males and 16 in (40.5 cm) for females, and about 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb). A Puli reaches just below an adult's knee, and its coat makes it look bigger and rounder. It suits most homes and usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=puli). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Puli vs Komondor and Mini American Shepherd

The Komondor is the Puli's giant, white-corded cousin: much taller and about four times as heavy. The [Miniature American Shepherd](/compare/miniature-american-shepherd-vs-puli/) is about the same size but looks completely different, with a normal double coat. The [Norwegian Buhund](/compare/norwegian-buhund-vs-puli/) is a little taller and heavier.

### The cords

Puli puppies have soft, fluffy coats. From about 9 months, the coat begins to form cords, which must be separated by hand regularly to stop them matting into large clumps. A fully corded coat can reach the ground and takes a long time to bathe and dry. The coat should never be brushed once it has corded. Many pet owners keep the coat short, which is much easier. Pulik are usually black, but also come in white, grey and fawn.

### Temperament and exercise

Pulik are lively, intelligent and very loyal, devoted to their family and often wary of strangers, which makes them good watchdogs. They're true herders: energetic, quick and sometimes bossy. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation and do well at agility, obedience and herding. They can be stubborn, so training should be consistent and fun.

### Health

Pulik are generally healthy. Known problems include hip dysplasia, eye diseases such as progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), and degenerative myelopathy. They often live 10–15 years.
