The Toy Poodle is the smallest of the Poodle sizes, bred down from the Miniature Poodle as a companion. Toy Poodles were popular in 18th-century France and England, and became fashionable lapdogs in the 20th century, especially in the US. Despite their size, they have the same intelligence and lively character as their bigger relatives. Today the Toy Poodle is one of the most popular small breeds worldwide, and the parent of crosses such as the Maltipoo.

### What a Toy Poodle's size means at home

The AKC defines a Toy Poodle as 10 in (25.5 cm) or under at the shoulder. Most stand about 20–25 cm, around mid-shin on an adult, and weigh only 2–3 kg (4–6 lb). It fits easily into the smallest flat and usually needs an 18″ (46 cm) crate or carrier. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=toy-poodle). Its fine bones mean it can be hurt by falls, being stepped on, or rough play with children or bigger dogs.

### Flying with a Toy Poodle

A Toy Poodle is an ideal size for cabin travel. Even with a carrier, it is far under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set. Check carrier sizes with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=toy-poodle).

### Toy vs Miniature Poodle

The [Miniature Poodle](/compare/toy-poodle-vs-miniature-poodle/) is over 10 in and up to 15 in (25.5–38 cm), and usually two to three times as heavy. The FCI sets the Toy at 24–28 cm, so the European definition is a little different. Very small dogs sold as "teacup" or "micro" Poodles aren't a recognised size, and dogs bred below the standard often have more health problems. The [Russian Toy](/compare/russian-toy-vs-toy-poodle/) is about the same size but a very different, fine-boned breed.

### Coat care

The Toy Poodle's curly, low-shedding coat keeps growing and mats easily, especially behind the ears and under the legs. Brush down to the skin several times a week and book a clip every 4–6 weeks. Tear staining around the eyes is common, and gentle daily cleaning helps.

### Temperament and health

Toy Poodles are bright, affectionate and playful, and often very attached to one person. They learn quickly but can become barky or anxious without training and company. Dental disease is very common because the teeth are crowded in the small jaw, so brush them daily. Loose kneecaps, eye diseases and Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (a hip condition) are also known. Toy Poodles are long-lived, often 14–18 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standard; FCI standard No. 172 (Poodle); The Kennel Club breed standard.*
