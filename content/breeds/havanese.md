The Havanese is Cuba's national dog and its only native breed. It descends from small Bichon-type dogs brought to Cuba by Spanish settlers and traders, which became the pampered companions of wealthy Cuban families in the 18th and 19th centuries. After the Cuban revolution of 1959, some families left for the United States with their dogs, and breeders in the US rebuilt the Havanese from a small number of these dogs. Today the Havanese is a popular, cheerful and sociable small dog, known for its springy gait and its love of people.

### What a Havanese's size means at home

A Havanese stands about 22–29 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult, and weighs about 3–6 kg (7–13 lb). It is sturdy for a toy breed and slightly longer than it is tall. It fits easily into a flat or a small car and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=havanese). It enjoys walks and play but doesn't need a lot of exercise.

### Flying with a Havanese

A Havanese usually fits in the cabin, because even with a carrier it stays under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set. Check your airline's carrier sizes with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=havanese).

### Havanese vs Bichon Frise

The two are close relatives. The [Bichon Frise](/compare/havanese-vs-bichon-frise/) is about the same height but clearly heavier, always white, and its curly coat is trimmed into a round shape. The Havanese comes in almost every colour, and its coat is long, soft and silky, often wavy, and usually left more natural.

### Coat care

The Havanese coat is soft and light, designed for the Cuban climate rather than cold. It sheds very little, but it mats easily, so a long coat needs brushing several times a week. Many owners keep it in a shorter "puppy cut". Some owners cord the coat into long natural ropes, which takes a lot of work. The hair over the eyes can be tied up or trimmed.

### Temperament and health

Havanese are very attached to their families. They often follow their people from room to room and don't like being left alone for long. They're bright and easy to train, and they love learning tricks. Health issues include loose kneecaps, eye problems and a bone condition affecting the front legs (chondrodysplasia), so ask breeders about the parents' health tests. Havanese often live 14–16 years.
