The Chiweenie is a cross between a Chihuahua and a Dachshund, usually a Miniature Dachshund. It became popular in the US in the 1990s and 2000s as a small, lively companion. Chiweenies vary a lot in looks: some have the Dachshund's long body and short legs, others the Chihuahua's apple head and large ears. As a crossbreed, it has no official standard.

### How big does a Chiweenie get?

Most Chiweenies stand about 15–25 cm at the shoulder, around ankle height on an adult, and weigh about 2.5–5.5 kg (5–12 lb). Size depends on the parents, especially on whether the Dachshund parent is a Miniature or a Standard. Ask about both parents' size, and use the [puppy weight calculator](/calculators/puppy-weight/?breed=chiweenie) as a rough guide. Next to a [Maltipoo](/compare/chiweenie-vs-maltipoo/), a Chiweenie is usually a little shorter and lighter.

### What a Chiweenie's size means at home

A Chiweenie suits any home, including small flats. Because many have the Dachshund's long body, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=chiweenie) suggests a 30″ (76 cm) crate for an average dog. Measure yours, as shorter-bodied Chiweenies fit a smaller crate. Chiweenies easily fit airline cabin limits. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=chiweenie).

### Protect the back

Long-bodied Chiweenies can inherit the Dachshund's risk of disc problems (IVDD). Keep your dog lean, use ramps or steps instead of letting it jump off furniture, and support its back when lifting.

### Temperament and health

Chiweenies are affectionate, playful and loyal, often bonding closely with one person. They can be wary of strangers and quick to bark, and some are stubborn to train. They need daily walks and play. Known health problems include back problems, dental disease, loose kneecaps and allergies. Chiweenies are long-lived, often 12–16 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standards for the Chihuahua and the Dachshund.*
