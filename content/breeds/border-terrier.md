The Border Terrier comes from the hills on the border between England and Scotland, where farmers and hunters needed a small, tough terrier that could keep up with horses and hounds and then bolt a fox from its den. It had to be narrow enough to follow a fox underground and hardy enough to work all day in wind and rain. Its distinctive otter-like head and harsh, weatherproof coat are part of that job. Today the Border Terrier is one of the most popular terriers in the UK: friendly, adaptable and less fiery than many terriers.

### What a Border Terrier's size means at home

The Kennel Club standard sets weights rather than heights: about 5.9–7 kg (13–15.5 lb) for males and 5.1–6.4 kg (11.5–14 lb) for females. Most stand about 30–38 cm at the shoulder, just below an adult's knee. A Border suits flats and houses and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=border-terrier).

### Can a Border Terrier fly in the cabin?

Sometimes. A lighter Border in a light carrier may stay within the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, but heavier ones won't. Use the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=border-terrier).

### Border vs Parson Russell and Patterdale

The [Parson Russell Terrier](/compare/border-terrier-vs-parson-russell-terrier/) and the [Patterdale Terrier](/compare/border-terrier-vs-patterdale-terrier/) are similar in size, both working terriers from northern England. The Parson is mostly white and leggier, and the Patterdale is usually black and more intense.

### Temperament and exercise

Border Terriers are cheerful, affectionate and usually good with children and other dogs. They have plenty of energy and stamina and need daily walks and play. They like to dig and chase, and many have a strong prey drive, so small pets and wildlife may not be safe, and recall needs work. They're clever and usually easy to train with rewards.

### Coat and health

The harsh, wiry coat sheds little. It is traditionally hand-stripped two or three times a year to keep it weatherproof. Known health problems include canine epileptoid cramping syndrome (CECS, also called Spike's disease), a movement disorder linked to gluten sensitivity, along with hip dysplasia and heart conditions. Borders are generally hardy and often live 12–15 years.
