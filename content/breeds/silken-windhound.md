The Silken Windhound is a young American breed. Francie Stull began developing it in Kentucky in the 1980s, crossing Borzois with smaller sighthounds and lurchers. The aim was a dog with the Borzoi's looks and gentle nature in a size that's easier to live with. The International Silken Windhound Society was founded in 1999, and the United Kennel Club recognised the breed in 2011. Silkens are still rare, but they have a devoted following among lure coursing and racing enthusiasts.

### What a Silken Windhound's size means at home

The breed standard allows 18½–23½ in (47–59.5 cm) at the shoulder for both sexes, roughly an adult's knee to mid-thigh. Males usually weigh about 15–25 kg (33–55 lb) and females about 10–20 kg (22–44 lb), though the standard itself sets no weight. That puts a Silken at about the height of a [Whippet](/breeds/whippet/), but with a long, silky coat that makes it look like a [Borzoi](/breeds/borzoi/) in miniature. A big male Borzoi stands about 25 cm taller and weighs more than twice as much.

### Temperament and exercise

Silkens are affectionate, gentle and sociable, usually friendly with people and other dogs, and calm indoors. They're more biddable than many sighthounds and respond well to kind, reward-based training. They still have a strong urge to chase and can sprint very fast, so most owners only let them run off the lead in safe, enclosed areas. Daily walks plus regular chances to gallop keep them happy.

### Coat and health

The coat is long and silky but has little undercoat, so it's easier to keep than it looks: a brush once or twice a week usually prevents tangles. Silkens are generally healthy and long-lived, often reaching 14 years or more. Many carry the MDR1 gene mutation, which makes them sensitive to some common drugs such as ivermectin, so ask your breeder about testing and tell your vet. Like other sighthounds, they can also be sensitive to some anaesthetics.
