The Icelandic Sheepdog is Iceland's only native dog breed. Its ancestors came to Iceland with the Vikings in the 9th and 10th centuries, and for more than a thousand years it herded sheep and horses and guarded farms. Disease and imports nearly wiped it out in the 20th century, but breeders in Iceland and England saved it. It's a cheerful, friendly spitz with a curled tail and a fox-like face.

### What an Icelandic Sheepdog's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 18 in (45.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 16.5 in (42 cm) for females, with typical weights around 13.5 kg (30 lb) for males and 11.5 kg (25 lb) for females. An Icelandic Sheepdog reaches roughly an adult's knee. It's a little longer than tall. It usually needs a 36″ (91 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=icelandic-sheepdog). It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Icelandic Sheepdog vs Pumi and Buhund

The Hungarian [Pumi](/compare/icelandic-sheepdog-vs-pumi/) is a little smaller and lighter, with a curly coat. The [Norwegian Buhund](/breeds/norwegian-buhund/), a close Nordic relative, is similar in size.

### Temperament and exercise

Icelandic Sheepdogs are friendly, playful and curious, usually good with children, other dogs and visitors. They're vocal herders and alert watchdogs. They need daily exercise and like to be part of family life.

### Coat and health

The thick double coat comes in short and long versions and sheds heavily once or twice a year. Icelandic Sheepdogs often have double dewclaws on their hind legs. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and eye conditions. They often live 12–14 years.
