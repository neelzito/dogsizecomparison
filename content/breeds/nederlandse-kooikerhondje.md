The Nederlandse Kooikerhondje (say "KOY-ker-hond-yuh") is a small Dutch spaniel bred to help with duck decoys, called *eendenkooien*. The dog would trot along the edge of a pond, waving its white-tipped tail, and curious ducks would follow it into a funnel-shaped trap. Kooikerhondjes appear in Dutch paintings from the 17th century. The breed nearly died out by the Second World War, but Baroness van Hardenbroek van Ammerstol revived it from a few dogs, starting with a female called Tommy.

### What a Kooikerhondje's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 16 in (40.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 15 in (38 cm) for females. Kooikers weigh about 9–13.5 kg (20–30 lb). A Kooiker reaches just below an adult's knee. It's compact, athletic and elegant. It usually needs a 30″ (76 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=nederlandse-kooikerhondje). It's too heavy for most airline cabins.

### Kooikerhondje vs Cocker Spaniel and Toller

The Kooiker is about [the same size as an American Cocker Spaniel](/compare/american-cocker-spaniel-vs-nederlandse-kooikerhondje/), but longer-legged, with a red-and-white coat and black-tipped ears ("earrings"). It's sometimes confused with the Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, which also lures ducks, but the Toller is bigger and red with white markings.

### Temperament and exercise

Kooikers are lively, alert and affectionate, devoted to their family but often shy or reserved with strangers, so early socialisation is important. They're intelligent and quick to learn, and they excel at agility, obedience and trick training. They need daily exercise and mental stimulation. They're sensitive, so gentle, reward-based training works best.

### Coat and health

The medium-length, slightly wavy coat sheds moderately and needs brushing once or twice a week. Known health problems include hereditary necrotising myelopathy (a nerve disease), von Willebrand's disease (a bleeding disorder) and epilepsy. DNA tests exist for the first two. Kooikers often live 12–15 years.
