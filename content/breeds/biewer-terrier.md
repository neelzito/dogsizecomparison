The Biewer Terrier (say "BEE-ver") was developed in Germany in 1984 by Werner and Gertrud Biewer, who bred a Yorkshire Terrier puppy with unusual white, black and tan colouring. The couple bred more of these tricoloured, piebald Yorkies, and the Biewer gradually became a breed of its own. Genetic studies have shown that it descends from the Yorkshire Terrier. The AKC recognised it in 2021. It's a playful, affectionate toy dog with a long, silky coat.

### What a Biewer's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 7–11 in (18–28 cm) at the shoulder, and Biewers weigh about 2–3.5 kg (4–8 lb). A Biewer reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's small and fine-boned, and it suits any home. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=biewer-terrier). It's an easy size for the airline cabin. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=biewer-terrier). Teach children to sit down when holding one, because falls can hurt it.

### Biewer vs Yorkshire Terrier and Toy Poodle

The Biewer is about the same size as a Yorkshire Terrier, but tricoloured: white, black or blue, and tan. Its temperament is often described as a little softer than the Yorkie's. A [Toy Poodle](/compare/biewer-terrier-vs-toy-poodle/) is about the same height but a little lighter, with a curly coat.

### Temperament and exercise

Biewers are cheerful, playful and very attached to their people. They're usually friendly with other dogs and visitors, and they adapt well to flats. They need daily walks and play but not a lot of exercise. They're clever and enjoy short, fun training sessions.

### Coat and health

The long, silky coat sheds very little but needs brushing several times a week, or a short "puppy cut". Known health problems include loose kneecaps, dental disease and liver shunts. Biewers are long-lived, often around 16 years.
