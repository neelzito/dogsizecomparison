The Schnoodle is a cross between a Schnauzer and a Poodle. It became popular in the US in the 1980s and 90s. The most common type is the Miniature Schnoodle, from a Miniature Schnauzer and a Miniature or Toy Poodle. Both parents have low-shedding coats, so most Schnoodles shed little. As a crossbreed, it has no official standard.

### How big does a Miniature Schnoodle get?

Most Miniature Schnoodles stand about 28–38 cm at the shoulder, just below an adult's knee, and weigh about 5–9 kg (11–20 lb). Giant and Standard Schnoodles, from larger Schnauzers and Standard Poodles, are much bigger, and Toy Schnoodles smaller. Next to a [Cavachon](/compare/cavachon-vs-schnoodle-miniature/), a Miniature Schnoodle is about the same size. Use the [puppy weight calculator](/calculators/puppy-weight/?breed=schnoodle-miniature) as a rough guide.

### What a Schnoodle's size means at home

A Miniature Schnoodle suits flats and houses and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=schnoodle-miniature). Lighter dogs may fit the airline cabin limit. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=schnoodle-miniature).

### Temperament and exercise

Schnoodles are clever, lively and affectionate, often good watchdogs. They need daily walks and play, and mental stimulation. They learn quickly but can be stubborn.

### Coat and health

The coat can be wiry like a Schnauzer's or soft and curly like a Poodle's. It sheds little but needs regular brushing and a trim every 6–8 weeks. Schnoodles can inherit the Miniature Schnauzer's tendency to high blood fats and pancreatitis, along with eye problems and loose kneecaps. They often live 12–15 years.

*Sources: AKC breed standards for the Miniature Schnauzer and the Poodle.*
