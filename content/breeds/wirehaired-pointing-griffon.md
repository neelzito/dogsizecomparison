The Wirehaired Pointing Griffon was developed in the 1870s by Eduard Korthals, a Dutchman who worked in Germany and later France. He wanted the "supreme gundog": a versatile hunting dog that points, retrieves and tracks on land and in water, at a pace suited to hunters on foot. In Europe it's often called the Korthals Griffon. Its harsh, wiry coat, beard and eyebrows give it a rugged, unkempt look.

### What a Griffon's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 22–24 in (56–61 cm) at the shoulder for males and 20–22 in (51–56 cm) for females. Males weigh about 22.5–32 kg (50–70 lb), females 16–22.5 kg (35–50 lb). A Griffon reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's sturdy and a little longer than tall. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=wirehaired-pointing-griffon) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate.

### Griffon vs Vizsla and Small Munsterlander

The [Vizsla](/compare/vizsla-vs-wirehaired-pointing-griffon/) is about the same size, with a short, golden coat. The [Small Munsterlander](/compare/small-munsterlander-vs-wirehaired-pointing-griffon/) is a little shorter and lighter. The [Irish Red and White Setter](/compare/irish-red-and-white-setter-vs-wirehaired-pointing-griffon/) is taller and lighter.

### Temperament and exercise

Griffons are friendly, eager to please and devoted to their family, and good with children. They need plenty of exercise and mental work, and they love water.

### Coat and health

The wiry coat sheds little and needs weekly brushing and occasional hand-stripping. Known health problems include hip dysplasia and eye conditions. Griffons often live 12–15 years.
