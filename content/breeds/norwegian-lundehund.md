The Norwegian Lundehund is one of the most unusual dog breeds in the world. It was bred on the remote islands of northern Norway to hunt puffins (*lunde*) on steep cliffs and in narrow rock crevices. To do that, it developed six toes on each foot, extremely flexible shoulders that let its front legs stretch out to the side, a neck that bends backwards until the head touches the spine, and ears that fold shut. When puffin hunting was banned, the breed almost died out, and it was rebuilt from just a handful of dogs.

### What a Lundehund's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 13–15 in (33–38 cm) at the shoulder for males and 12–14 in (30.5–35.5 cm) for females, with weights of about 6.5–8 kg (15–18 lb) for males and 5.5–7 kg for females. A Lundehund reaches just below an adult's knee. It usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=norwegian-lundehund). A light Lundehund may fit the cabin limit. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=norwegian-lundehund).

### Lundehund vs Beagle and Podengo Pequeno

The Lundehund is about [the same height as a Beagle](/compare/beagle-vs-norwegian-lundehund/) but much lighter. The [Portuguese Podengo Pequeno](/compare/norwegian-lundehund-vs-portuguese-podengo-pequeno/) is smaller.

### Temperament

Lundehunds are alert, playful and independent. They can be hard to house-train and are often reserved with strangers. They need daily exercise and like to climb and explore.

### Health

The small founding population means the breed is prone to "Lundehund syndrome", a group of digestive disorders that affect how food is absorbed. A careful diet and regular vet checks are important. Lundehunds often live 12–15 years.
