The Schipperke is a small black dog from Belgium, known for its fox-like face, its thick ruff and its tailless rump. Its name is Flemish and is usually translated as "little skipper", because Schipperkes guarded barges on Belgium's canals and caught rats, but it may mean "little shepherd". In the 17th century, the shoemakers' guild in Brussels held competitions for their Schipperkes. The breed became fashionable in the 1880s after Queen Marie Henriette of Belgium acquired one.

### What a Schipperke's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 11–13 in (28–33 cm) at the shoulder for males and 10–12 in (25.5–30.5 cm) for females. Schipperkes weigh about 4.5–7.5 kg (10–16 lb). A Schipperke reaches around mid-shin on an adult. It's compact, sturdy and slopes from its heavy ruff down to its hindquarters. It suits any home and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=schipperke).

### Can a Schipperke fly in the cabin?

Often. Lighter Schipperkes in a light carrier stay within the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set, though heavier ones may not. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=schipperke).

### Schipperke vs Miniature Poodle and Volpino

A [Miniature Poodle](/compare/miniature-poodle-vs-schipperke/) is about the same size but completely different in coat and character. The [Volpino Italiano](/compare/schipperke-vs-volpino-italiano/), a small Italian spitz, is similar in size, usually white.

### Temperament and exercise

Schipperkes are curious, bold and full of energy, always alert to what's going on. They make excellent watchdogs and can be quite vocal. They're independent and clever, sometimes stubborn, so training should be consistent and varied. They need daily walks and play, and they're known escape artists, so a secure garden is important. Their instinct to chase small animals is strong.

### Coat and health

The double coat is short on the face and legs and long around the neck (the ruff), the "cape" and the "culottes" on the back of the thighs. It sheds heavily once or twice a year and needs weekly brushing. Known health problems include Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (a hip condition), loose kneecaps, eye conditions and MPS IIIB, a rare inherited disease for which a DNA test exists. Schipperkes are long-lived, often 12–16 years.
