The Greater Swiss Mountain Dog (*Grosser Schweizer Sennenhund*) is the largest and probably oldest of the four Swiss mountain dog breeds. For centuries, Swiss farmers used it as a draught dog to pull carts of milk and cheese, to drive cattle and to guard the farm. It was sometimes called "the poor man's horse". By 1900 it had almost disappeared. In 1908, the geologist Albert Heim recognised a large short-haired dog at a show as a distinct type and helped revive the breed. Today the "Swissy" is a calm, loyal and powerful family dog.

### What a Swissy's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 25.5–28.5 in (65–72.5 cm) at the shoulder for males and 23.5–27 in (60–68.5 cm) for females. Swissies weigh about 52–63.5 kg (115–140 lb) for males and 38.5–50 kg (85–110 lb) for females. A Swissy reaches roughly an adult's mid-thigh. It's heavy enough to put it in the giant size class on this site. It needs a large vehicle and, because of its height and bulk, the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=greater-swiss-mountain-dog) suggests a 54″ (137 cm) crate for an average male.

### Swissy vs Bernese and Bullmastiff

The [Bernese Mountain Dog](/compare/bernese-mountain-dog-vs-greater-swiss-mountain-dog/) has the same tricolour markings but a long coat, and it's a little shorter and clearly lighter. Next to a [Bullmastiff](/compare/bullmastiff-vs-greater-swiss-mountain-dog/), the Swissy is a little taller and similar in weight. The other two Swiss mountain dogs, the Appenzeller and the Entlebucher, are much smaller.

### Temperament and exercise

Swissies are confident, gentle and devoted to their family, and they make alert watchdogs with a loud bark. They're strong and can be stubborn, and puppies are boisterous, so early training and socialisation are important. They need daily exercise and enjoy pulling sports such as carting. They mature slowly and stay puppyish for two to three years.

### Care and health

The short, dense double coat is easy to groom but sheds heavily, especially twice a year. Swissies struggle in the heat. Known health problems include bloat, splenic torsion (a twisted spleen), hip and elbow dysplasia, epilepsy, and "lick fits" (episodes of frantic licking and swallowing, often linked to stomach upset). Like other giant breeds, Swissies have a fairly short life, usually 8–11 years.
