The Bull Terrier was created in England in the 1850s and 60s by James Hinks of Birmingham, who crossed the old "bull and terrier" fighting dogs with the white English Terrier and possibly the Dalmatian. He wanted an elegant, all-white dog with a clean outline and a good temperament, a "gentleman's companion". The breed's unique egg-shaped head developed later. Today Bull Terriers are known as playful, clownish and affectionate dogs, and they've starred in films and adverts, including as the mascot of the US retailer Target.

### What a Bull Terrier's size means at home

Unusually, the Bull Terrier's breed standards set no height or weight limits. They ask only that the dog be as substantial as possible for its size. Most stand about 53–56 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's knee, and weigh about 22.5–32 kg (50–70 lb). Muscular and solid, it's heavy enough to put it in the large size class on this site. It usually needs a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=bull-terrier). There is also a separate Miniature Bull Terrier breed, which stands no more than about 35.5 cm.

### Bull Terrier vs Airedale

The [Airedale Terrier](/compare/airedale-terrier-vs-bull-terrier/), the "King of Terriers", is a little taller but similar in weight. Its wiry tan-and-black coat and long, flat head look very different from the Bull Terrier's smooth coat and egg-shaped head.

### Temperament

Bull Terriers are fun-loving, energetic and very attached to their people. They're often described as "a three-year-old child in a dog suit". They can be stubborn and need consistent, patient training and early socialisation, especially with other dogs. They need daily exercise and play, and they don't like being left alone for long. Some develop compulsive behaviours, such as tail chasing or spinning, which are best discussed with a vet or behaviourist.

### Care and health

The short, flat coat needs very little grooming, but white Bull Terriers are prone to sunburn on the nose and ears. Deafness is more common in white Bull Terriers, so ask breeders whether puppies have been hearing (BAER) tested. Known health problems also include skin allergies, kidney disease, heart disease and lethal acrodermatitis, a rare inherited condition. Bull Terriers often live 12–13 years.
