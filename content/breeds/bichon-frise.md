The Bichon Frise belongs to an old family of small white Mediterranean companion dogs that also includes the Maltese, Havanese and Bolognese. Its ancestors, the "Tenerife" dogs, were spread by sailors and traders. They became favourites of European royalty in the 16th century, especially at the French court, and later earned their living as street performers and circus dogs. The name comes from the French *bichon à poil frisé*, "curly-haired little dog". Today the Bichon is a cheerful, affectionate family companion that loves people and attention.

### What a Bichon's size means at home

A Bichon Frise stands about 24–29 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult, and weighs about 5.5–8 kg (12–18 lb). It is sturdy rather than delicate, and that powder-puff coat makes it look bigger than it is. It suits flats and small homes, enjoys daily walks and play, and usually needs a 24″ (61 cm) crate. ### Can a Bichon fly in the cabin?

Sometimes. With a carrier, smaller Bichons can stay under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set for dog and carrier, but bigger ones won't. Check yours with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=bichon-frise).

### Bichon vs Maltese and Havanese

The Bichon is bigger and sturdier than the [Maltese](/compare/bichon-frise-vs-maltese/), and its coat is curly rather than straight and silky. The [Havanese](/compare/havanese-vs-bichon-frise/), its Cuban cousin, is similar in height but clearly lighter, and it comes in many colours, not just white.

### Coat care

The Bichon's double coat is soft and dense underneath, with a curly outer coat. It sheds very little, which is why many people with allergies get on well with Bichons. But because the loose hair stays in the coat, it mats quickly. Plan on brushing down to the skin several times a week and a professional groom every 4–6 weeks for the classic rounded trim. Keep the hair around the eyes trimmed, and wipe away tear stains.

### Temperament and health

Bichons are friendly with almost everyone, including other pets, and are usually good with children. They can be hard to house-train, so be patient and consistent. They often dislike being left alone. Health issues include allergies and skin problems, loose kneecaps, bladder stones and dental disease. Bichons often live 14–15 years or more.
