The Brussels Griffon started out as a stable dog in 19th-century Brussels, kept by coachmen to catch rats. Crosses with the Pug, the King Charles Spaniel and the Affenpinscher gave it its flat face and big personality, and it became a favourite of Belgian royalty, especially Queen Marie Henriette. Its almost human expression, with large eyes and a bearded, monkey-like face, made it a film star in *As Good as It Gets* (1997). Today it's an affectionate, sensitive and confident companion.

### One breed or three?

The FCI recognises three related breeds: the **Griffon Bruxellois** (rough, red coat), the **Griffon Belge** (rough coat in black or black and tan) and the **Petit Brabançon** (smooth coat). The AKC and The Kennel Club treat them as one breed, the Brussels Griffon, with rough and smooth coats and several colours.

### What a Brussels Griffon's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for about 8–10 lb (3.5–4.5 kg). Brussels Griffons stand about 18–25 cm at the shoulder, around mid-shin on an adult. They're square, sturdy little dogs with a short back. A Griffon suits any home and usually needs an 18″ (46 cm) crate or carrier. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=brussels-griffon).

### Flying with a Brussels Griffon

A Griffon easily fits in the cabin, staying far under the 8 kg (17.6 lb) limit many airlines set, even with a carrier. Check with the [airline cabin check](/calculators/airline-cabin/?breed=brussels-griffon). Because of its flat face, many airlines won't accept it in the hold anyway.

### Griffon vs Pekingese and Japanese Chin

The [Pekingese](/compare/brussels-griffon-vs-pekingese/) and the [Japanese Chin](/compare/brussels-griffon-vs-japanese-chin/) are similar in size and also flat-faced. The Peke is lower and longer, with a long coat, and the Chin is more elegant, with a silky coat.

### Temperament and health

Griffons are very attached to their people, often one person in particular. They're sensitive, alert and sometimes stubborn, with a lot of self-importance. They need gentle training and don't like being left alone. Rough coats need regular brushing and hand-stripping, while smooth coats shed more but need less care. Health issues include breathing problems from the flat face, eye injuries, loose kneecaps, and syringomyelia, a condition affecting the spinal cord. Griffons often live 12–15 years.
