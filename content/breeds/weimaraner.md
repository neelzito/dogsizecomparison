The Weimaraner was developed in the early 19th century at the court of Grand Duke Karl August of Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach in Germany. Nobles wanted an all-round hunting dog with speed, a good nose, courage and intelligence, at first for big game such as boar and deer, and later for birds. For decades the Germans guarded the breed closely, so few dogs left the country until the 20th century. Its silvery-grey coat and pale amber, grey or blue-grey eyes earned it the nickname "the grey ghost".

### What a Weimaraner's size means at home

A male Weimaraner stands about 64–69 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's mid-thigh, and weighs about 32–41 kg (70–90 lb). Females are about 59–64 cm and 25–34 kg. It is tall, lean and athletic, with a lot of strength. It needs a large car boot and usually a 48″ (122 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=weimaraner).

### Weimaraner vs Vizsla

People often compare the two. The [Vizsla](/compare/weimaraner-vs-vizsla/) is smaller and lighter, with a golden-rust coat. The Weimaraner is taller, heavier and grey. Both are affectionate "Velcro dogs" that want to be with their people all the time, and both need a lot of exercise.

### Exercise and separation

Weimaraners are high-energy hunting dogs. They need at least an hour or two of hard exercise every day, such as running, swimming, retrieving or dog sports, plus mental challenges. They're very people-focused and are known for separation anxiety: left alone too long, many become destructive or noisy. They suit active owners who are often at home. Their prey drive is strong, so small pets and wildlife may not be safe.

### Coat and care

The short, smooth grey coat needs very little grooming. A long-haired variety also exists, and the FCI recognises it, but it's rare. The short coat gives little protection against cold, so a coat helps in winter.

### Health

Weimaraners are deep-chested, which puts them at high risk of bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus), a life-threatening emergency. Feed two or three smaller meals a day and avoid hard exercise right after eating. Hip dysplasia, eyelid problems and some immune and blood disorders are also known. Weimaraners often live 10–13 years.
