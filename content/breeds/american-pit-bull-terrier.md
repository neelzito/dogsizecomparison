The American Pit Bull Terrier was developed from bulldog and terrier crosses brought to the US by immigrants from Britain and Ireland in the 19th century. These dogs were bred for dog fighting, which is now illegal, but they also worked on farms as catch dogs and guardians and were kept as family dogs. The United Kennel Club (UKC) recognised the breed in 1898. The American Kennel Club doesn't recognise the APBT itself, but it does recognise the closely related American Staffordshire Terrier. The word "pit bull" is often used loosely for a whole group of similar-looking dogs and mixes, which causes a lot of confusion.

### What an APBT's size means at home

The UKC standard gives typical ranges: males about 46–53 cm at the shoulder, roughly an adult's knee, and 16–27 kg (35–60 lb); females about 43–51 cm and 13.5–23 kg (30–50 lb). Muscular, athletic and strong for its size, it needs a medium-to-large car boot and usually a 42″ (107 cm) crate. Check with the [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=american-pit-bull-terrier). Lead training from puppyhood is important, because a strong APBT can pull hard.

### APBT vs American Staffordshire and Staffordshire Bull Terrier

The [American Staffordshire Terrier](/compare/american-pit-bull-terrier-vs-american-staffordshire-terrier/) is a little shorter but stockier and heavier, bred more for the show ring. The [Staffordshire Bull Terrier](/compare/american-pit-bull-terrier-vs-staffordshire-bull-terrier/) from England is clearly shorter and lighter.

### Legal restrictions

Many countries and cities restrict or ban pit bull-type dogs. In the UK, for example, the "pit bull terrier" type is banned under the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991. In other places, owners may need a licence, insurance, a muzzle in public or special permission. Before getting one, or travelling or moving with one, check the current laws where you live and where you'll go. Many airlines also have rules for "strong" breeds.

### Temperament and training

A well-bred, well-raised APBT is typically affectionate, eager to please and very people-oriented. Many are known for being gentle with their families. Like any powerful dog, it needs early socialisation, consistent reward-based training and responsible handling. Some are not tolerant of other dogs, so introductions should be careful and supervised. APBTs are energetic and need daily exercise and mental challenges.

### Care and health

The short coat needs very little grooming. Known health problems include hip dysplasia, skin allergies, hypothyroidism and some heart and eye conditions. APBTs often live 12–14 years.

*Sources: United Kennel Club breed standard (American Pit Bull Terrier); AKC breed standard (American Staffordshire Terrier); UK Dangerous Dogs Act 1991.*
