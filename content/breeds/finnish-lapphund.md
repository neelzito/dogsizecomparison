The Finnish Lapphund comes from Lapland, in the far north of Finland, Sweden and Norway, where the Sámi people used it for centuries to herd reindeer. It's a hardy spitz-type dog with a thick coat that protects it in Arctic winters. After the Second World War, when snowmobiles began to replace herding dogs, Finnish breeders worked to preserve it. Today it's one of the most popular breeds in Finland: friendly, calm and good-natured.

### What a Lappie's size means at home

The AKC standard calls for 18–21 in (46–52 cm) at the shoulder for males and 16–19 in (41–47 cm) for females. Lappies weigh about 15–24 kg (33–53 lb). A Lappie reaches roughly an adult's knee, and its thick coat makes it look bigger. The [crate size calculator](/calculators/crate-size/?breed=finnish-lapphund) suggests a 42″ (107 cm) crate for an average male. It's too heavy for an airline cabin.

### Lapphund vs Australian Cattle Dog

The [Australian Cattle Dog](/compare/australian-cattle-dog-vs-finnish-lapphund/), another herder, is about the same size but short-coated. The Swedish Lapphund is a close relative, usually black.

### Temperament and exercise

Lappies are friendly, gentle and calm, usually good with children and other dogs. They still have a herding instinct and can be vocal. They need daily exercise and enjoy herding, agility and long walks, especially in cold weather. They're eager to please and fairly easy to train.

### Coat and health

The long, thick double coat sheds heavily, especially once or twice a year, and needs brushing at least weekly, more during moults. Lappies struggle in hot weather. Known health problems include progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), for which a DNA test exists, hip dysplasia and cataracts. Lappies often live 12–15 years.
